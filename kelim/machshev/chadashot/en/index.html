What is Israel News in 3 Minutes?

Israel News in 3 Minutes is a short digest of the day's important news from Israel, written for people who want to stay informed without living on a news site. Three times a day, at 7:00, 13:00 and 20:00 Israel time, a new edition appears at the top of this page. It holds 8 to 10 short points, each one or two plain sentences, so a full read takes about three minutes. Then you are done, and you can get on with your day.

The project is a free kindness project. It was built for olim (new immigrants), for Jews living abroad with family in Israel, and for anyone who wants to know what happened in Israel today without the noise. There are no ads, you never have to sign up, and nobody makes a profit from it.

How the digest is made

The digest starts from the Hebrew edition, החדשות ב-3 דקות ("The News in 3 Minutes"), which is built from the headlines of several large Israeli news sites. Stories that appear in more than one source rise to the top, because that is a good sign that they really matter. The points are written in simple words, checked against the original sources, and then checked a second time automatically: every number that appears in a point must also appear in the source, otherwise the point is dropped.

The English edition is a translation of those same checked points. Nothing new is added during translation, and the same safeguard applies: a point whose numbers do not match its source is not published. This keeps the English edition as careful as the Hebrew one.

Every point has its source

Under each point you will find the name of the news site it comes from, with a link. The sources are Israeli sites in Hebrew, among them Ynet, Walla, Maariv, Arutz Sheva, Channel 14, Srugim, Kipa, Kikar HaShabbat and Globes. If you read Hebrew, or use your browser's translate button, you can open the original story with one tap. The digest is meant to save you time, not to ask you to trust it blindly.

Calm, not graphic

The digest leaves out graphic descriptions and sensational wording. It tells you what happened, in a sober tone, and then stops. A calm summary is easier to read in the morning, and easier to forward to a parent or a child. The points are grouped into top stories, the Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) community, economy and the cost of living, and a short good-news item when there is one.

Shabbat and holidays

The service is Shabbat-friendly. No edition is produced from Friday evening until Saturday night, or on Jewish holidays. After Shabbat ends there is a special edition, "What happened on Shabbat", so that nothing is missed. Once a week there is also a longer summary of the week.

Where to get it

You can simply come back to this page three times a day. If you prefer to receive the digest without remembering to visit, join the Telegram or WhatsApp channel using the buttons above. Both channels are quiet by design: only the digest is posted. Every edition ends with a link back to this page, so the sources are always one tap away.

The Hebrew version of the same project is at the Hebrew news page (Hebrew). It also has an archive of past editions (Hebrew) and a kosher-phone line (Hebrew) for people without internet. Frequently asked questions

What happened in Israel today?

Open the latest edition at the top of this page. It lists the main stories of the day in a few short points, each with a link to the original report. A new edition appears at 7:00, 13:00 and 20:00 Israel time.

Is there a daily Israel news digest in English?

Yes, this is one. It is a short digest of the day's news from Israel, three times a day, translated from a carefully checked Hebrew edition. You can read it here or follow the Telegram or WhatsApp channel.

Can I read Israel news without graphic images?

Yes. This digest is text only. It avoids graphic descriptions and sensational wording, so you can read it calmly, even with the family around.

How much does it cost, and do I need to sign up?

It is free, with no ads and no sign-up. It is a kindness project and nobody profits from it.

Who is it for?

For olim, for Jews abroad who care about Israel, for students, for visitors, and for anyone who wants the key points of the day in about three minutes.

Why are the source links in Hebrew?

The digest is built from Israeli news sites, which publish in Hebrew. The English text is a translation of the Hebrew points. If you want to read the original story, open the link and use your browser's translate feature.

Is the digest published on Shabbat and holidays?

No. The service pauses from Friday evening until Saturday night and on Jewish holidays. After Shabbat or the holiday ends, a "What happened on Shabbat" edition is published.

How accurate is the translation?

Each point is translated from a Hebrew point that was already checked against its sources. Names and numbers are checked again after translation, and a point that does not match is left out instead of being published.

Does it cover the Haredi community and the economy too?

Yes. Besides the top stories, each edition can include points about the Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) community, the economy and the cost of living, and a short good-news item when there is one.
