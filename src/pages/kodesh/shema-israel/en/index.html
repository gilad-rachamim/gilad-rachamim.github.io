Shema Yisrael – the first verse

Deuteronomy 6:4

English: Metsudah Chumash, Metsudah Publications, 2009 (CC-BY), from Sefaria. The transliteration is a reading aid prepared for this page.

יחיד אומר

Someone praying alone first says:

אֵל מֶלֶךְ נֶאֱמָן:

El melech ne'eman.

שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ ה' אֶחָד:

Shema yisra'el A-donai E-loheinu A-donai echad.

Listen, Yisroel! Adonoy is our God, Adonoy is one.

בלחש

Quietly:

בָּרוּךְ שֵׁם כְּבוֹד מַלְכוּתוֹ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד:

Baruch shem kevod malchuto le'olam va'ed.

Ve'ahavta – "You shall love"

Deuteronomy 6:5–9

English: Metsudah Chumash, Metsudah Publications, 2009 (CC-BY), from Sefaria. The transliteration is a reading aid prepared for this page.

וְאָהַבְתָּ אֵת ה' אֱלֹקֶיךָ בְּכָל־לְבָבְךָ וּבְכָל־נַפְשְׁךָ וּבְכָל־מְאֹדֶךָ:

Ve'ahavta et A-donai E-lohecha bechol levavcha uvchol nafshecha uvchol me'odecha.

You are to love Adonoy, your God, with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your possessions.

וְהָיוּ הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוְּךָ הַיּוֹם עַל־לְבָבֶךָ:

Vehayu hadevarim ha'ele asher anochi metzavecha hayom al levavecha.

And these words that I command you today shall be upon your heart.

וְשִׁנַּנְתָּם לְבָנֶיךָ וְדִבַּרְתָּ בָּם בְּשִׁבְתְּךָ בְּבֵיתֶךָ וּבְלֶכְתְּךָ בַדֶּרֶךְ וּבְשָׁכְבְּךָ וּבְקוּמֶךָ:

Veshinantam levanecha vedibarta bam beshivtecha beveitecha uvlechtecha vaderech uvshachbecha uvkumecha.

You are to teach them to your children and you are to discuss them, when you sit at home, and when you journey on the road, and when you go to sleep, and when you rise.

וּקְשַׁרְתָּם לְאוֹת עַל־יָדֶךָ וְהָיוּ לְטֹטָפֹת בֵּין עֵינֶיךָ:

Ukshartam le'ot al yadecha vehayu letotafot bein einecha.

You are to tie them as a sign on your arm and they are to be totafos between your eyes.

וּכְתַבְתָּם עַל־מְזוּזֹת בֵּיתֶךָ וּבִשְׁעָרֶיךָ:

Uchtavtam al mezuzot beitecha uvish'arecha.

You are to write them on the doorposts of your house, and on your gateposts.

Vehaya im shamoa – the second paragraph

Deuteronomy 11:13–21

English: Metsudah Chumash, Metsudah Publications, 2009 (CC-BY), from Sefaria. The transliteration is a reading aid prepared for this page.

וְהָיָה אִם־שָׁמֹעַ תִּשְׁמְעוּ אֶל־מִצְוֹתַי אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוֶּה אֶתְכֶם הַיּוֹם לְאַהֲבָה אֶת־ה' אֱלֹקֵיכֶם וּלְעָבְדוֹ בְּכָל־לְבַבְכֶם וּבְכָל־נַפְשְׁכֶם:

Vehaya im shamoa tishme'u el mitzvotai asher anochi metzave etchem hayom le'ahava et A-donai E-loheichem ul'avdo bechol levavchem uvchol nafshechem.

Should you thoroughly heed My commandments that I am commanding you today—to love Adonoy, your God, and to serve Him with all your desires and with your entire beings,

וְנָתַתִּי מְטַר־אַרְצְכֶם בְּעִתּוֹ יוֹרֶה וּמַלְקוֹשׁ וְאָסַפְתָּ דְגָנֶךָ וְתִירֹשְׁךָ וְיִצְהָרֶךָ:

Venatati metar artzechem be'ito yore umalkosh ve'asafta deganecha vetiroshcha veyitzharecha.

I will provide the rain of your land in its time, fall rain and spring rain, and you will harvest your grain and your wine and your olive oil.

וְנָתַתִּי עֵשֶׂב בְּשָׂדְךָ לִבְהֶמְתֶּךָ וְאָכַלְתָּ וְשָׂבָעְתָּ:

Venatati esev besadcha livhemtecha ve'achalta vesavata.

And I will provide grass in your field for your animals, and you will eat and be full.

הִשָּׁמְרוּ לָכֶם פֶּן יִפְתֶּה לְבַבְכֶם וְסַרְתֶּם וַעֲבַדְתֶּם אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים וְהִשְׁתַּחֲוִיתֶם לָהֶם:

Hishamru lachem pen yifte levavchem vesartem va'avadtem elohim acherim vehishtachavitem lahem.

Look out for yourselves lest your heart be misled and you turn away and serve other gods and bow to them,

וְחָרָה אַף־ה' בָּכֶם וְעָצַר אֶת־הַשָּׁמַיִם וְלֹא־יִהְיֶה מָטָר וְהָאֲדָמָה לֹא תִתֵּן אֶת־יְבוּלָהּ וַאֲבַדְתֶּם מְהֵרָה מֵעַל הָאָרֶץ הַטֹּבָה אֲשֶׁר ה' נֹתֵן לָכֶם:

Vechara af A-donai bachem ve'atzar et hashamayim velo yihye matar veha'adama lo titen et yevulah va'avadtem mehera me'al ha'aretz hatova asher A-donai noten lachem.

And Adonoy’s anger will be aroused against you, and He will restrain the skies and there will be no rain, and the soil will not yield its produce, and you will swiftly be removed from upon the good land that Adonoy is giving you.

וְשַׂמְתֶּם אֶת־דְּבָרַי אֵלֶּה עַל־לְבַבְכֶם וְעַל־נַפְשְׁכֶם וּקְשַׁרְתֶּם אֹתָם לְאוֹת עַל־יֶדְכֶם וְהָיוּ לְטוֹטָפֹת בֵּין עֵינֵיכֶם:

Vesamtem et devarai ele al levavchem ve'al nafshechem ukshartem otam le'ot al yedchem vehayu letotafot bein eineichem.

You are to place these words of Mine on your mind and on your being, and bind them as a sign on your hand and they will be totafos on your head;

וְלִמַּדְתֶּם אֹתָם אֶת־בְּנֵיכֶם לְדַבֵּר בָּם בְּשִׁבְתְּךָ בְּבֵיתֶךָ וּבְלֶכְתְּךָ בַדֶּרֶךְ וּבְשָׁכְבְּךָ וּבְקוּמֶךָ:

Velimadtem otam et beneichem ledaber bam beshivtecha beveitecha uvlechtecha vaderech uvshachbecha uvkumecha.

And you are to teach them to your sons to speak in them when you sit at home, and when you journey on the road, and when you go to sleep, and when you rise;

וּכְתַבְתָּם עַל־מְזוּזוֹת בֵּיתֶךָ וּבִשְׁעָרֶיךָ:

Uchtavtam al mezuzot beitecha uvish'arecha.

And you are to write them on the doorposts of your home and your gateways.

לְמַעַן יִרְבּוּ יְמֵיכֶם וִימֵי בְנֵיכֶם עַל הָאֲדָמָה אֲשֶׁר נִשְׁבַּע ה' לַאֲבֹתֵיכֶם לָתֵת לָהֶם כִּימֵי הַשָּׁמַיִם עַל־הָאָרֶץ:

Lema'an yirbu yemeichem vimei veneichem al ha'adama asher nishba A-donai la'avoteichem latet lahem kimei hashamayim al ha'aretz.

In order that they will be many—your years and the years of your children on the soil that Adonoy swore to your forefathers to give them, like the years of heaven on earth.

וַיֹּאמֶר ה' אֶל־מֹשֶׁה לֵּאמֹר:

Vayomer A-donai el moshe lemor.

דַּבֵּר אֶל־בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל וְאָמַרְתָּ אֲלֵהֶם וְעָשׂוּ לָהֶם צִיצִת עַל־כַּנְפֵי בִגְדֵיהֶם לְדֹרֹתָם וְנָתְנוּ עַל־צִיצִת הַכָּנָף פְּתִיל תְּכֵלֶת:

Daber el benei yisra'el ve'amarta alehem ve'asu lahem tzitzit al kanfei vigdeihem ledorotam venatnu al tzitzit hakanaf petil techelet.

Speak to Bnei Yisroel and tell them to make for themselves, tzizis-fringes on the corners of their garments—throughout their generations; and they will place with the fringes of each corner a thread of blue.

וְהָיָה לָכֶם לְצִיצִת וּרְאִיתֶם אֹתוֹ וּזְכַרְתֶּם אֶת־כָּל־מִצְוֹת ה' וַעֲשִׂיתֶם אֹתָם וְלֹא־תָתֻרוּ אַחֲרֵי לְבַבְכֶם וְאַחֲרֵי עֵינֵיכֶם אֲשֶׁר־אַתֶּם זֹנִים אַחֲרֵיהֶם:

Vehaya lachem letzitzit ur'item oto uzchartem et kol mitzvot A-donai va'asitem otam velo taturu acharei levavchem ve'acharei eineichem asher atem zonim achareihem.

And it will be to you for fringes and you will look upon it and you will remember all of Adonoy’s commandments and you will perform them; and you will not search after [the desire of] your hearts and after your eyes which cause you to go astray.

לְמַעַן תִּזְכְּרוּ וַעֲשִׂיתֶם אֶת־כָּל־מִצְוֹתָי וִהְיִיתֶם קְדֹשִׁים לֵאלֹקֵיכֶם:

Lema'an tizkeru va'asitem et kol mitzvotai vihyitem kedoshim lE-loheichem.

In order that you will remember and perform all My commandments; and you will be holy unto your God.

אֲנִי ה' אֱלֹקֵיכֶם אֲשֶׁר הוֹצֵאתִי אֶתְכֶם מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם לִהְיוֹת לָכֶם לֵאלֹקִים אֲנִי ה' אֱלֹקֵיכֶם:

I am Adonoy, your God, Who brought you out of the land of Egypt to be your God. I am Adonoy your God.

Shema Israel: the full Shema with transliteration and English

Shema Israel (also written Shema Yisrael, or simply "the Shema") is the best-known verse in Jewish prayer: שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ ה' אֶחָד: (Deuteronomy 6:4). It opens the Shema prayer, three paragraphs from the Torah that are said every day. This page gives all three in full: the Hebrew with vowels, a transliteration under every verse so you can say the words even before you read Hebrew, and an English translation from a named source.

What is the Shema?

The Shema is a reading of three Torah passages, in this order: Shema and Ve'ahavta (Deuteronomy 6:4–9), Vehaya im shamoa (Deuteronomy 11:13–21) and Vayomer, the paragraph of tzitzit (Numbers 15:37–41). Its Hebrew name is Kriat Shema, "the reading of Shema". The first verse declares that God is one; the paragraphs that follow speak of loving God, of teaching these words to one's children, and of the commandments of tefillin, mezuzah and tzitzit.

Shema Yisrael in English

The English shown under each verse is the Metsudah Chumash translation as published on Sefaria. For the first verse it reads: "Listen, Yisroel! Adonoy is our God, Adonoy is one." Other translations open with the familiar "Hear, O Israel". We do not write our own translation of the prayers; every English line on this page comes from that one named source.

Shema transliteration

The transliteration lines follow the modern Israeli pronunciation: "ch" is the throat sound of Chanukah, and an apostrophe marks a short break inside a word, as in Yisra'el. The first verse is: Shema Yisra'el, Ado-nai Elo-heinu, Ado-nai echad. On the page the Names are hyphenated, as is customary in print. The line that follows it, said quietly, is: Baruch shem kevod malchuto le'olam va'ed. It is not a Torah verse, which is why it is said in a whisper and stands on its own line here.

When is the Shema said?

The Torah itself gives the time: "when you go to sleep, and when you rise" (Deuteronomy 6:7, Metsudah). So the Shema is said in the evening prayer and in the morning prayer, together with the blessings that surround it in the siddur. The first paragraph is also said at bedtime. The morning Shema has a latest time that changes every day and from city to city; the daily times for Israeli cities are on the Hebrew page זמני היום. For what to do in a specific case, ask a rabbi.

How to say it

Say it slowly, word by word, pronouncing each word clearly. A widespread custom is to cover the eyes with the hand for the first verse, to concentrate without distraction. Each verse stands on its own line here so that you do not lose your place. If the Hebrew is new to you, read the transliteration line aloud first and then look back at the Hebrew above it; after a few days the Hebrew words begin to look familiar.

Ve'ahavta, mezuzah and tefillin

Ve'ahavta ("You shall love") is the continuation of the first paragraph. The first two paragraphs of the Shema are also the passages written on the parchment inside a mezuzah, and two of the four passages inside tefillin. The source is in the text itself: "You are to tie them as a sign on your arm" and "You are to write them on the doorposts of your house" (Deuteronomy 6:8–9, Metsudah).

Who this page is for

For anyone who wants the Shema in front of them: people learning to pray, people on the path of Orthodox conversion, guests at a synagogue, parents teaching a child the first verse, and anyone without a siddur at hand. Questions about the Shema

What does Shema Israel mean?

The first verse, in the Metsudah translation shown on this page: "Listen, Yisroel! Adonoy is our God, Adonoy is one." It is a declaration that God is one. The words that follow speak of loving God with all one's heart.

Is it Shema Israel or Shema Yisrael?

Both are the same two Hebrew words. "Yisrael" follows the Hebrew pronunciation; "Israel" is the English spelling. Many people simply say "the Shema".

What are the three paragraphs of the Shema?

Shema and Ve'ahavta (Deuteronomy 6:4–9), Vehaya im shamoa (Deuteronomy 11:13–21) and Vayomer (Numbers 15:37–41). All three are on this page in full.

What is "Baruch shem kevod malchuto le'olam va'ed"?

A line said quietly right after the first verse. It is not part of the Torah passage, so it is said in a whisper. On this page it appears in Hebrew and transliteration.

When do you say the Shema?

In the morning and in the evening prayers, and the first paragraph again at bedtime. The latest time for the morning Shema changes daily; for a practical question, ask a rabbi.

What is Ve'ahavta?

The verses that follow the first verse (Deuteronomy 6:5–9), beginning with the word Ve'ahavta, "You are to love". Together with the first verse they form the first paragraph of the Shema.

Why are the Names written with a hyphen or in short form?

Out of respect for God's Name, it is customary not to write it out in full on a screen or in a text that may be discarded. In prayer the words are said as usual.
