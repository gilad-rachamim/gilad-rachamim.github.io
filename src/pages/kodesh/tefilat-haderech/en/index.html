Tefilat HaDerech

Said after leaving the city, once a day

היוצא לדרך, ושוהה בנסיעתו מחוץ לעיר לפחות מהלך שיעור פרסה (72 דקות) אומר תפלת הדרך. ברכה זו מברך לאחר שיצא מהעיר, וטוב לסמוך אותה לברכה אחרונה של אכילה או שתיה או אשר יצר.

יְהִי רָצוֹן מִלְּפָנֶיךָ ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ וֵאלֹקֵי אֲבוֹתֵינוּ, שֶׁתּוֹלִיכֵנוּ לְשָׁלוֹם, וְתַצְעִידֵנוּ לְשָׁלוֹם, וְתִסְמְכֵנוּ לְשָׁלוֹם, (אם חוזר בו ביום מוסיף וְתַחֲזִירֵנוּ לְשָׁלוֹם), וְתַצִּילֵנוּ מִכַּף כָּל־אוֹיֵב וְאוֹרֵב בַּדֶּרֶךְ, וְתִשְׁלַח בְּרָכָה בְּמַעֲשֵׂה יָדֵינוּ, וְתִתְּנֵנוּ לְחֵן וּלְחֶסֶד וּלְרַחֲמִים בְּעֵינֶיךָ וּבְעֵינֵי כָל־רוֹאֵינוּ. בָּרוּךְ (אַתָּה ה') שׁוֹמֵעַ תְּפִלָּה:

ויש מוסיפים פסוקים אלה לשמירה

וְיַעֲקֹב הָלַךְ לְדַרְכּוֹ וַיִּפְגְּעוּ־בוֹ מַלְאֲכֵי אֱלֹקִים: וַיֹּאמֶר יַעֲקֹב כַּאֲשֶׁר רָאָם מַחֲנֵה אֱלֹקִים זֶה וַיִּקְרָא שֵׁם־הַמָּקוֹם הַהוּא מַחֲנָיִם:

וַיִּסָּעוּ וַיְהִי חִתַּת אֱלֹקִים עַל־הֶעָרִים אֲשֶׁר סְבִיבוֹתֵיהֶם וְלֹא רָדְפוּ אַחֲרֵי בְּנֵי יַעֲקֹב: לִישׁוּעָתְךָ קִוִּיתִי ה': ג' פעמים

יְבָרֶכְךָ ה' וְיִשְׁמְרֶךָ: יָאֵר ה' פָּנָיו אֵלֶיךָ וִיחֻנֶּךָּ: יִשָּׂא ה' פָּנָיו אֵלֶיךָ וְיָשֵׂם לְךָ שָׁלוֹם: ג' פעמים

הִנֵּה אָנֹכִי שֹׁלֵחַ מַלְאָךְ לְפָנֶיךָ לִשְׁמָרְךָ בַּדָּרֶךְ וְלַהֲבִיאֲךָ אֶל־הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר הֲכִנֹתִי: ג' פעמים

הַמַּלְאָךְ הַגֹּאֵל אֹתִי מִכָּל־רָע יְבָרֵךְ אֶת־הַנְּעָרִים וְיִקָּרֵא בָהֶם שְׁמִי וְשֵׁם אֲבֹתַי אַבְרָהָם וְיִצְחָק וְיִדְגּוּ לָרֹב בְּקֶרֶב הָאָרֶץ: ג' פעמים

Transliteration

Yehi ratzon milefanecha A-donai E-loheinu ve'E-lohei avoteinu, shetolichenu leshalom, vetatz'idenu leshalom, vetismechenu leshalom, (one who returns on the same day adds: vetachazirenu leshalom), vetatzilenu mikaf kol oyev ve'orev baderech, vetishlach beracha bema'aseh yadeinu, vetitenenu lechen ulechesed ulerachamim be'einecha uve'einei chol ro'einu. Baruch (ata A-donai) shome'a tefila.

A reading aid only. The Hebrew above is the prayer itself; where the page writes the Name as A-donai or E-loheinu, it is said in the usual way. The small grey lines in the Hebrew are the siddur's own instructions.

English translation

May it be Your Will, our G·d & G·d of our fathers, that You will direct us in peace... And save us from the hand of any enemy or ambush on the way, or any bad situation or encounter. And allow us to arrive to our desired destination in peace. And give us grace & kindness & mercy, in Your eyes & in the eyes of all who see us. Blessed is He Who Listens to prayer.

Source: Sefaria Community Translation of Siddur Edot HaMizrach (CC0), quoted as it is there. It is a shortened translation and does not follow the Hebrew above word for word.

English translation

May it be Your will, Eternal One, our God and the God of our ancestors, that You lead us toward peace, support our footsteps towards peace, guide us toward peace, and make us reach our desired destination, for life, joy, and peace. May You rescue us from the hand of every foe, ambush, bandits and wild animals along the way, and from all manner of punishments that assemble to come to Earth. Tefilat HaDerech in Hebrew, transliteration and English, always in your pocket

Tefilat HaDerech (also spelled Tefilas Haderech or Tefillat HaDerech) is the short Jewish prayer said when setting out on a journey: that we arrive in peace, that we are saved from every harm on the way, and that we come back in peace. This page gives it in full: the Hebrew text with vowels in large print, a transliteration for anyone who does not read Hebrew fluently yet, and an English translation taken from a named source. You can read it in ten seconds before leaving the city, even without a siddur in your hand.

What is Tefilat HaDerech, the Traveler's Prayer?

The Hebrew name means “the prayer of the way”. In English it is called the Traveler's Prayer or the Wayfarer's Prayer. It is one paragraph that opens with the words “Yehi ratzon” (“May it be Your will”) and ends with the blessing “shome'a tefila” (“who hears prayer”). It is said in the plural: the traveler asks for everyone on the road, not only for himself. Many people keep it on a card in the car or in a wallet; here it sits on your phone, and it opens even without a connection.

Tefilat HaDerech text in three rites: Edot HaMizrach, Sefard and Ashkenaz

Choose your rite once, at the top of the page, and the page remembers it. In the Edot HaMizrach rite the page also shows the verses that some add for protection. In the Sefard rite it shows the verses that are said several times, a special addition for a flight, and short prayers for entering a city and leaving it. In the Ashkenaz rite it shows the shorter text, followed by the verses “And Jacob went on his way” and the priestly blessing. If you do not know which rite your family or community follows, ask your rabbi; the main paragraph is very similar in all three.

Tefilat HaDerech transliteration

Under the Hebrew of each rite you will find the main paragraph in Latin letters. The transliteration follows the modern Israeli pronunciation: “ch” is the throaty sound of the letters chet and chaf, and an apostrophe marks a short break between two vowels. It is a reading aid only, prepared for this page; the prayer itself is the Hebrew above it. The best way to use it is to read the Hebrew line with your eyes and the transliteration with your lips, until the Hebrew becomes familiar.

The verses said after the prayer

After the main paragraph many siddurim add verses about protection on the road: “And Jacob went on his way, and the angels of God met him” (Genesis 32:2–3), the priestly blessing (Numbers 6:24–26), “Behold, I send an angel before thee” (Exodus 23:20) and others. Which verses are added, and how many times each is said, differs from rite to rite, and the page shows each rite as it appears in its own siddur.

Questions and answers

When do you say Tefilat HaDerech?

In the Kitzur Shulchan Aruch: לְאַחַר שֶׁיָּצָא מֵעִבּוּרָהּ שֶׁל הָעִיר, דְּהַיְנוּ שִׁבְעִים אַמָּה וּשְׁנֵי שְׁלִישֵׁי אַמָּה לְאַחַר שֶׁכָּלוּ כָּל הַבָּתִים, מִתְפַּלֵּל תְּפִלַּת הַדֶּרֶךְ (Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 68:1). In English: “after having left the boundaries of the city, that is seventy and two-thirds amohs beyond the last house, you should say the traveller's prayer” (trans. Rabbi Avrohom Davis, Metsudah, 1996, from Sefaria).

From what distance is Tefilat HaDerech said?

אֵין לְהִתְפַּלֵּל אוֹתָהּ אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן יֵשׁ לוֹ לָלֶכֶת לְכָל הַפָּחוֹת פַּרְסָה (Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 68:2): “It should not be recited unless you have to travel at least a parsah.” The Edot HaMizrach siddur writes: לפחות מהלך שיעור פרסה (72 דקות) אומר תפלת הדרך, that is, a journey of at least the walking time of a parsah, 72 minutes. And one who forgot: וְאִם שָׁכַח, יָכוֹל לְהִתְפַּלֵּל כָּל זְמַן שֶׁהוּא בַדֶּרֶךְ (there): “If you forgot, you may recite it as long as you are still on the road” (trans. Davis, Metsudah). For your own trip, ask a rabbi.

Is Tefilat HaDerech said on every trip, or once a day?

אֵין אוֹמְרִים אוֹתָהּ אֶלָּא פַּעַם אַחַת בְּכָל יוֹם שֶׁהוּא הוֹלֵךְ (Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 68:5): “Tefillas Haderech is recited only once each day that you travel” (trans. Davis, Metsudah). The same paragraph also explains when it is said a second time.

Can you say Tefilat HaDerech while travelling?

יֵשׁ לְאָמְרָהּ בַּעֲמִידָה, וְאִם הוּא רוֹכֵב אוֹ הוֹלֵךְ בַּעֲגָלָה, אִם אֶפְשָׁר לְהַעֲמִיד הַבְּהֵמָה, יַעֲמִידֶנָּהּ, מִשּׁוּם דְּרוֹכֵב כִּמְהַלֵּךְ דָּמִי. וְאִם לָאו, יֹאמְרָהּ כָּךְ (Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 68:4): “It should be recited while standing. If you are riding an animal or travelling in a wagon, if it is possible to stop the animal, you should stop it … If it is not possible to stop the animal you may recite it while riding” (trans. Davis, Metsudah). With any practical question, such as a car, a bus or a train, ask a rabbi.

Is there a special Traveler's Prayer for a flight?

The Sefard siddur (“Torat Emet”) has a prayer about which it says באוירון מוסיפים, that is, on an airplane this prayer is added. It appears on this page in the Sefard rite, right after Tefilat HaDerech, with a transliteration.

Who says Birkat HaGomel?

ארבעה צריכים להודות יורדי הים כשעלו ממנה והולכי מדברות כשיגיעו ליישוב ומי שהיה חולה ונתרפא ומי שהיה חבוש בבית האסורים ויצא (Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chayim 219:1). In English: “Four people must give thanks: those who descend to the sea when they have come up from it; those who travel through deserts when they reach an inhabited area; one who was ill and recovered; and one who was imprisoned and was released” (Sefaria Community Translation). The text of the blessing and the answer of the congregation are on this page.

What does Tefilat HaDerech mean in English?

Tefila is prayer and derech is a way or a road: “the prayer of the way”. In English it is usually called the Traveler's Prayer or the Wayfarer's Prayer, and it is also spelled Tefilas Haderech, Tefillat HaDerech and Tefilat Haderech.

Is Tefilat HaDerech said in the singular or in the plural?

The Kitzur Shulchan Aruch (68:1) writes: “This prayer is said in the plural form” (trans. Davis, Metsudah), and it names one phrase that is said in the singular. The Hebrew text on this page is shown exactly as it appears in each siddur.

Should Tefilat HaDerech be said after another blessing?

The Kitzur Shulchan Aruch (68:3) writes: “Tefillas Haderech should be said following another berachah” (trans. Davis, Metsudah), and gives examples, such as eating or drinking something on the way and saying the blessing after it. The Edot HaMizrach siddur on this page says the same in its opening instruction.
