Zmanim, explained

Zmanim are the halachic times of the Jewish day. Jewish law ties many mitzvot to the sun: the morning Shema has a latest time, the Amidah of Shacharit has a latest time, Mincha has an earliest time, and the day ends at sunset and nightfall. Because the sun rises and sets at a different time every day and in every place, these times change constantly. In New York the latest time for Shema is around 9 AM in winter and close to 10 AM in summer. This page calculates every zman for your city and any date, so you can see today's times at a glance, see what comes next, and plan the week ahead.

The zmanim of the morning

Alot Hashachar (dawn) is the first light of morning. It is calculated here as the sun 16.1 degrees below the horizon, and the fixed 72 minutes before sunrise is shown too. Misheyakir is when there is enough light to recognize a person nearby; it is the earliest time for tallit and tefillin, calculated as the sun 11.5 degrees below the horizon. Sunrise (netz hachama) is the preferred time to begin the Amidah of Shacharit for those who pray "vatikin". Sunrise and sunset are calculated at sea level, the common practice in most calendars.

Latest time for Shema and for Shacharit

The Shema must be said in the first quarter of the day, three halachic hours, and the Amidah of Shacharit within the first third, four halachic hours. There are two main ways of counting the day. The Magen Avraham counts from dawn to nightfall, here 72 minutes before sunrise to 72 minutes after sunset, which gives earlier times. The Gra, the Vilna Gaon, counts from sunrise to sunset, which gives later times. The page shows both, side by side. Many people try to say the Shema by the earlier time; which time applies to you is a question for your rabbi.

Chatzot, Mincha and plag hamincha

Chatzot is midday, halfway between sunrise and sunset. Mincha Gedola, half a halachic hour after chatzot, is the earliest time to pray Mincha. Mincha Ketana is nine and a half halachic hours into the day, and plag hamincha is ten and three quarter hours, about an hour and a quarter before sunset. Plag hamincha is used for an early Shabbat in summer and in some customs for Chanukah candles. Chatzot halaila, midnight, is halfway between sunset and the next sunrise; it is used, for example, as the time for the Pesach afikoman.

Sunset, nightfall and Rabbeinu Tam

Sunset (shkiah) ends the day. Nightfall (tzeit hakochavim) is when three small stars can be seen; this page uses the sun 8.5 degrees below the horizon, usually 40 to 50 minutes after sunset in the continental US. Rabbeinu Tam held that night begins 72 minutes after sunset, and many people wait until then to end Shabbat and fasts. The weekly candle lighting and Havdalah times are on the Shabbat times page.

What is a halachic hour?

Zmanim are counted in halachic hours, shaot zmaniot: the day is divided into twelve equal parts, whatever its length. On a long summer day each part can be more than 75 minutes; on a short winter day under 50 minutes. So "three hours into the day" is about 9 AM in winter and close to 10 AM in summer. This is why the latest Shema time moves across the year and why a calculator is needed.

How the zmanim are calculated

The times come from the position of the sun for the latitude, longitude and time zone of your city, calculated with the open-source Hebcal library, the same astronomy used by many Jewish calendars. Times that mark the start of something (dawn, sunrise, nightfall) are rounded up to the next minute, and times that mark a deadline (latest Shema, sunset) are rounded down, so the rounding leaves you on the safe side. In practice, do not rely on the last minute: clocks differ and the horizon is not flat. This page is a calendar, not a halachic ruling; for how to apply the times, ask your rabbi.

Questions about zmanim

What are zmanim?

Zmanim (Hebrew for "times") are the times of the day that matter in Jewish law: the earliest and latest times for morning prayers, the latest time to say the Shema, midday, the earliest time for the afternoon prayer, sunset and nightfall. They are based on the position of the sun, so they change every day and are different in every city.

What time is sof zman Shema today?

Two times are shown. The Magen Avraham counts the day from 72 minutes before sunrise to 72 minutes after sunset, so his latest time for Shema is earlier. The Gra (Vilna Gaon) counts from sunrise to sunset, so his time is later. Both are three "halachic hours" into the day. Many people try to say the Shema by the earlier time; ask your rabbi which time to follow.

What is plag hamincha?

Plag hamincha is 10¾ halachic hours into the day, about an hour and a quarter before sunset. It is the earliest time some communities light candles and pray Maariv for an "early Shabbat" in summer, and it is also used for lighting Chanukah candles in some customs. How to use it depends on your community; ask your rabbi.

What is a halachic hour (shaah zmanit)?

A halachic hour is one twelfth of the day. In summer the day is long, so a halachic hour can be more than 75 minutes; in winter it can be under 50 minutes. Zmanim such as the latest Shema (3 hours) and plag hamincha (10¾ hours) are counted in these hours, which is why they move with the seasons.

What is the difference between Magen Avraham and Gra?

They disagree about when the day begins and ends for counting halachic hours. The Magen Avraham uses dawn to nightfall (here 72 minutes before sunrise and after sunset); the Gra uses sunrise to sunset. The Magen Avraham's morning times are therefore earlier. The page shows both so you can follow your own custom.

What is the earliest time to put on tallit and tefillin?

That time is called misheyakir, when there is enough light to recognize someone at a short distance. This page uses the common calculation of the sun 11.5 degrees below the horizon, usually 50 to 60 minutes before sunrise in the continental US. Some communities use a different degree; follow your rabbi.

When is the latest time for Mincha?

Mincha, the afternoon prayer, can be said from Mincha Gedola, half an hour after midday. The ideal latest time is sunset. Mincha Ketana and plag hamincha are also shown, because some customs prefer praying after Mincha Ketana. If you are late, ask your rabbi what to do.

Do zmanim change with daylight saving time?

Every clock time on the page jumps by an hour when daylight saving time starts in March and ends in November, because the sun's times on the clock move with it. The times are always in your city's local time, including Arizona and Hawaii, which do not use daylight saving time.
