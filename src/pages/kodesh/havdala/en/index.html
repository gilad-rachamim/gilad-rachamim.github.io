Havdalah for Saturday night

The one making Havdalah holds a full cup of wine in the right hand and says it aloud; everyone listening answers Amen after each blessing.

Holding the cup – verses of salvation

הִנֵּה אֵל יְשׁוּעָתִי אֶבְטַח וְלֹא אֶפְחָד: כִּי עָזִּי וְזִמְרָת יָהּ ה'. וַיְהִי לִי לִישׁוּעָה: וּשְׁאַבְתֶּם מַיִם בְּשָׂשׁוֹן. מִמַּעַיְנֵי הַיְשׁוּעָה: לַיהֹוָה הַיְשׁוּעָה. עַל עַמְּךָ בִרְכָתֶךָ סֶּלָה: ה' צְבָאוֹת עִמָּנוּ. מִשְׂגָּב לָנוּ אֱלֹהֵי יַעֲקֹב סֶלָה: ה' צְבָאוֹת. אַשְׁרֵי אָדָם בֹּטֵחַ בָּךְ: ה' הוֹשִׁיעָה. הַמֶּלֶךְ יַעֲנֵנוּ בְּיוֹם קָרְאֵנוּ: לַיְּהוּדִים הָיְתָה אוֹרָה וְשִׂמְחָה וְשָׂשׁוֹן וִיקָר. כֵּן תִּהְיֶה לָנוּ: כּוֹס יְשׁוּעוֹת אֶשָּׂא. וּבְשֵׁם ה' אֶקְרָא:

Say: Hineh El y'shu'ati evtach v'lo efchad, ki ozi v'zimrat Yah A-donai, vay'hi li lishu'ah. Ushavtem mayim b'sason mima'ainei hay'shu'ah. LA-donai hay'shu'ah, al am'cha virchatecha selah. A-donai Tz'va'ot imanu, misgav lanu Elohei Ya'akov selah. A-donai Tz'va'ot, ashrei adam botei'ach bach. A-donai hoshi'ah, hamelech ya'aneinu v'yom kor'einu. LaY'hudim hay'tah orah v'simchah v'sason vikar. Ken tih'yeh lanu. Kos y'shu'ot esa, uv'shem A-donai ekra.

Meaning: Behold, God is my salvation; I will trust, and will not be afraid; For GOD the LORD is my strength and song; And He is become my salvation. Therefore with joy shall ye draw water Out of the wells of salvation. Salvation belongeth unto the LORD; Thy blessing be upon Thy people. Selah The LORD of hosts is with us; The God of Jacob is our high tower. Selah O LORD of hosts, happy is the man that trusteth in Thee. Save, LORD; Let the King answer us in the day that we call. The Jews had light and gladness, and joy and honour. [So may it be for us.] I will lift up the cup of salvation, And call upon the name of the LORD.

Verses from Isaiah 12:2–3, Psalms and the Book of Esther (8:16). Many families say the line "LaY'hudim hay'tah orah..." together, and the leader repeats it.

Calling for attention

סַבְרִי מָרָנָן וְרַבָּנָן וְרַבּוֹתַי:

Say: Savri maranan v'rabanan v'rabotai.

Meaning: [Attention, my masters, teachers and friends.]

This line is in the Nusach Sefard siddur; the Daat Nusach Ashkenaz siddur does not have it.

Blessing over the wine

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם. בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הַגָּפֶן:

Say: Baruch ata A-donai, Eloheinu melech ha'olam, borei p'ri hagafen.

Meaning: Blessed are You, Lord our God, King of the universe, who creates the fruit of the vine.

Hold the cup, but do not drink yet – the wine is drunk at the end of Havdalah.

Everyone answers: Amen

Over the spices

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם. בּוֹרֵא מִינֵי בְשָׂמִים:

Say: Baruch ata A-donai, Eloheinu melech ha'olam, borei minei v'samim.

Meaning: Blessed are You, Lord our God, King of the universe, who creates varied spices.

After the blessing, smell the spices and pass them around the table.

Everyone answers: Amen

Over the candle

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם. בּוֹרֵא מְאוֹרֵי הָאֵשׁ:

Say: Baruch ata A-donai, Eloheinu melech ha'olam, borei m'orei ha'esh.

Meaning: Blessed are You, Lord our God, King of the universe, who creates the lights of the fire.

A candle with several wicks is lit for this blessing, or two candles held together.

Everyone answers: Amen

The Havdalah blessing

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם. הַמַּבְדִּיל בֵּין קֹדֶשׁ לְחוֹל. בֵּין אוֹר לְחשֶׁךְ. בֵּין יִשְׂרָאֵל לָעַמִּים. בֵּין יוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי לְשֵׁשֶׁת יְמֵי הַמַּעֲשֶׂה: בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה'. הַמַבְדִּיל בֵּין קֹדֶשׁ לְחוֹל:

Say: Baruch ata A-donai, Eloheinu melech ha'olam, hamavdil bein kodesh l'chol, bein or l'choshech, bein Yisra'el la'amim, bein yom hash'vi'i l'sheshet y'mei hama'aseh. Baruch ata A-donai, hamavdil bein kodesh l'chol.

Meaning: Blessed are You, Lord our God, King of the universe, who distinguishes between the holy and the profane, between light and darkness, between Israel and the nations, between the seventh day and the six working days. Blessed are You, O Lord, who distinguishes between the holy and the profane.

After this blessing the one who made Havdalah drinks the wine.

Everyone answers: Amen

How to make Havdalah, step by step

1. Wait until Shabbat is over

Havdalah is said after Shabbat has ended, when three stars are out. The time changes with your city and the season – see Shabbat times for your city or ask the rabbi of your community.

2. Set out the wine, spices and candle

Put on the table a cup filled with wine or grape juice, fragrant spices and a candle with several wicks. Many families have a braided Havdalah candle and a spice box, but simple things work too: two candles held together so the flames join, and cloves from the kitchen.

3. Say the verses and the blessings

Holding the cup, say the verses of salvation, then the blessings over wine, spices and fire, and last the Havdalah blessing. Everyone answers Amen. If it is your first time, tap "Transliteration only" and "Big print" – the words stay easy to read on a phone while one hand holds the cup.

4. Drink the wine and put out the candle

After the Havdalah blessing, the one who said it drinks the wine. Many put out the candle with a few drops of wine from the cup. The new week has begun.

Questions of Jewish law

Who can say Havdalah for others, what to do if you have no wine, spices or candle, and whether you may eat before Havdalah are questions of Jewish law, and customs differ. Ask an Orthodox rabbi about your family.

What the wine, spices and fire mean

Wine – joy

Shabbat begins with Kiddush over a cup of wine, and it ends over a cup of wine. The opening verses speak of salvation, light and joy – the words we carry into the week.

Spices – comfort

Tradition teaches that on Shabbat a person has an "extra soul", which leaves when Shabbat ends. The pleasant smell of the spices refreshes and comforts us at that moment.

Fire – the first work of the week

Fire is not lit on Shabbat, so the blessing over fire is said as it ends: the flame lit after Shabbat marks the start of the working week. Many raise their hands toward the candle and look at the light on their fingernails.

Separating holy from everyday

The last blessing lists what God has separated: holy from profane, light from darkness, Israel from the nations, the seventh day from the six working days. Saying it, we recognize that time is not all the same, and that the holiness of Shabbat is special.

How to read the transliteration

Each word is written in English letters the way it is said in most synagogues in Israel. "ch" is the throaty sound in Bach, not the ch of chair. An apostrophe marks a very short vowel or a break between syllables (b'sason, ha'esh). "ei" sounds like the ey in they, "ai" like the word eye. The Name of God is written with a hyphen (A-donai) out of respect. In many Ashkenazi communities some words sound different, such as "boruch" for "baruch" – both are correct.

Questions and answers

What are the Havdalah blessings?

Havdalah has four blessings: over wine ("borei p'ri hagafen"), over spices ("borei minei v'samim"), over the fire of a candle ("borei m'orei ha'esh"), and the Havdalah blessing itself, which ends "Baruch ata A-donai, hamavdil bein kodesh l'chol" – Blessed are You, who distinguishes between the holy and the profane. Before them, holding the cup, verses of salvation are said. All of it is on this page in Hebrew, transliteration and English.

What does Havdalah mean?

Havdalah means "separation" or "distinction". With it we mark the end of the holy Shabbat and the start of the six working days of the week.

When is Havdalah said?

On Saturday night, after Shabbat has ended – when three stars can be seen. The exact time in your city is on the Shabbat times page; follow your community's rabbi for the time to use.

What do I need for Havdalah?

A cup of wine or grape juice, fragrant spices such as cloves or myrtle, and a candle with several wicks – many use a braided Havdalah candle. What to do if something is missing is a question of Jewish law; ask an Orthodox rabbi.

How do I say Havdalah if I don't read Hebrew?

Read the line marked "Say:" under each Hebrew line – the same words in English letters. Tap "Transliteration only" and "Big print" to read it from your phone while one hand holds the cup, or print the page.

Why do people look at their fingernails during Havdalah?

After the blessing over the candle, many hold up their hands toward the flame and look at the light on their fingernails, to benefit from the light of the fire they have just blessed.

Why do some put out the candle in the wine?

It is a widespread custom: after Havdalah, a little wine is poured into a dish and the candle is put out in it. Customs differ between families.

Is Havdalah different after a holiday?

Yes. After a festival or Yom Kippur the order is different from Saturday night. This page is Havdalah at the end of Shabbat; for holidays use the siddur of your synagogue.

What is the difference between Nusach Sefard and Ashkenaz here?

In Havdalah the differences are small: the Nusach Sefard siddur has the call "Savri maranan" before the blessing over wine, and the Daat Nusach Ashkenaz siddur does not. The verses and the four blessings are the same.
