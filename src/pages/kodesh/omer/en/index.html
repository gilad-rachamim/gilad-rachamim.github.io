The blessing before the count

Say the blessing, then the count of the night

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו וְצִוָּנוּ עַל סְפִירַת הָעֹמֶר:

Say: Baruch ata A-donai Eloheinu melech ha'olam asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu al s'firat ha'omer.

Meaning: Blessed are You, Lord, our God, King of the universe, Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to count the Omer.

The blessing is the same in the Ashkenaz and Sefard siddurs. The Name of God is written in short form.

All 49 days of the omer count

Each night has its own words. The Hebrew date under each day is the day the count belongs to; the count itself is said on the evening before.

Say: Hayom yom echad la'omer.

How to count the omer, step by step

1. Find tonight's day

The omer count runs from the second night of Passover until the night before Shavuot. The box at the top of this page shows the day from today's Hebrew date, so you never have to work it out. If you are reading during the day, it shows the count for the coming night, and the list below marks it.

2. Say the blessing

The siddur prints the blessing before the count: “Baruch ata A-donai Eloheinu melech ha'olam asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu al s'firat ha'omer.” – blessed are You, Lord our God, King of the universe, who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to count the omer.

3. Say the count of the night

Right after the blessing, say the line for that night. On the first night it is “Hayom yom echad ba'omer” – today is day one of the omer. From the seventh night on, the count says both the days and the weeks, for example “Hayom sh'mona yamim shehem shavua echad v'yom echad ba'omer” – today is eight days, which are one week and one day, of the omer.

4. Continue with the prayers after it

After the count the siddur adds “Harachaman”, a short prayer for the return of the Temple service, and many add Psalm 67 and other prayers. Questions about the laws of the count – the time, a missed night, counting in another language – are for your rabbi.

What is Sefirat HaOmer?

Sefirat HaOmer means “the counting of the omer”. The omer was an offering of the new barley brought in the Temple on the 16th of Nisan, the second day of Passover. The Torah says: “And ye shall count unto you from the morrow after the day of rest, from the day that ye brought the sheaf of the waving; seven weeks shall there be complete” (Leviticus 23:15, JPS 1917, public domain). The fiftieth day is Shavuot, the festival of the giving of the Torah.

Counting days and weeks

The Torah speaks of counting both fifty days and seven weeks, so the count names the days, and from day seven also the weeks. Day 49 is “seven weeks”, and the next evening is Shavuot.

From Passover to Shavuot

The count ties the freedom of Passover to the giving of the Torah on Shavuot. Each night adds one step, like climbing toward Mount Sinai.

Ashkenaz or Sefard: ba'omer and la'omer

The words of the count are almost the same in every siddur. One small difference: the Metsudah Siddur Ashkenaz ends each night with “ba'omer” (in the omer), and the Metsudah Siddur Sefard with “la'omer” (to the omer). Use the buttons above to switch; the page remembers your choice on this device. If you are not sure which your community uses, follow your synagogue's siddur or ask your rabbi.

The omer calendar: important days

  • Day 1 – the second night of Passover, 16 Nisan.
  • Day 15 – 30 Nisan, the first day of Rosh Chodesh Iyar.
  • Day 33, Lag BaOmer – 18 Iyar, a day of celebration in many communities.
  • Day 49 – 5 Sivan, the last count; Shavuot begins that evening.

To see the dates in this year's calendar, use our Hebrew calendar or the Hebrew date converter.

Reading the transliteration

The transliteration spells each Hebrew word in English letters. “ch” sounds like the end of “Bach”, “tz” like the end of “cats”, and an apostrophe marks a very short vowel, as in sh'mona. The Name of God is written “A-donai” out of respect. To learn the letters themselves, see the Hebrew alphabet page.

Questions and answers

What is the omer count tonight?

The box at the top of this page shows tonight's count by itself, from today's date in the Hebrew calendar: the day number, the Hebrew words with vowels, the transliteration and the English. The count begins on the second night of Passover and ends on the night before Shavuot.

How many days do you count the omer?

Forty-nine days – seven full weeks. The Torah says to count from the day the omer offering was brought, the 16th of Nisan, "seven weeks shall there be complete" (Leviticus 23:15, JPS 1917), and the 50th day is Shavuot.

What does it mean to count the omer?

The omer was an offering of barley brought in the Temple on the second day of Passover. From that day the Torah commands counting the days and weeks until Shavuot, the day the Torah was given. Today we count each night with a blessing, saying how many days and weeks have passed.

How do you count the omer?

In the evening, say the blessing "Baruch ata A-donai Eloheinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu al s'firat ha'omer", and then the count of that night, for example "Hayom yom echad ba'omer" – today is day one of the omer. Many siddurs add prayers after the count. For any question about how to count, ask your rabbi.

What time do I count the omer?

The count is said at night, at the start of the Jewish day, usually after the evening prayer. The exact time from which to count, and what to do if you are not sure whether it is already night, are questions of Jewish law – ask your rabbi. Nightfall times for your city are on our zmanim page.

What if I forgot to count the omer?

What to do after a missed night – with or without the blessing – is a question of Jewish law with several cases. Ask your rabbi, and keep counting the next nights as he tells you.

What is forbidden during the omer?

Many communities keep customs of mourning during part of the omer period, such as not holding weddings and not cutting hair. Which days, and what exactly, differ between communities. Ask your rabbi what your community keeps.

Is it ba'omer or la'omer?

Both are printed. The Metsudah Siddur Ashkenaz says ba'omer ("in the omer") and the Metsudah Siddur Sefard says la'omer ("to the omer"). Choose your nusach on this page and follow the siddur of your community.

How much is an omer? How many cups is one omer?

The Torah says "an omer is the tenth part of an ephah" (Exodus 16:36, JPS 1917). Converting it into cups or liters depends on different opinions about ancient measures, so the numbers you see online differ. For a practical question, ask your rabbi.

What is Lag BaOmer?

Lag BaOmer is the 33rd day of the omer, the 18th of Iyar. "Lag" is the Hebrew letters lamed and gimel, which have the value 33. In many communities it is a day of celebration in the middle of the omer period.
