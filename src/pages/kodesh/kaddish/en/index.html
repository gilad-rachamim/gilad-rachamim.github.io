The Mourner's Kaddish (Kaddish Yatom)

The mourner stands and says the Kaddish aloud. After the lines marked below, the congregation answers, and the mourner waits for the answer before going on. The second part, “Y’hei sh’mei raba”, is said by everyone together.

Mourner

יִתְגַּדַּל וְיִתְקַדַּשׁ שְׁמֵהּ רַבָּא (אָמֵן)

Say: Yitgadal v'yitkadash sh'mei raba (Amen)

Meaning: Exalted and sanctified be His great Name

Congregation answers: Amen

Mourner

בְּעָלְ֒מָֽא דִּי־בְרָא כִרְעוּתֵהּ וְיַמְלִיךְ מַלְכוּתֵהּ בְּחַיֵּיכוֹן וּבְיוֹמֵיכוֹן וּבְחַיֵּי דְכָל־בֵּית יִשְׂרָאֵל בַּעֲגָלָא וּבִזְמַן קָרִיב וְאִמְרוּ אָמֵן:

Say: B'alma di vera chir'utei v'yamlich malchutei b'chayeichon uvyomeichon uvchayei d'chol beit yisra'el ba'agala uvizman kariv v'imru Amen.

Meaning: in the world which He created according to His will and may He rule His kingdom. In your lifetime and in your days, and in the lifetime of the entire House of Israel, speedily and in the near future— and say Amen.

Everyone together – mourner and congregation

יְהֵא שְׁמֵהּ רַבָּא מְבָרַךְ לְעָלַם וּלְעָלְ֒מֵי עָלְ֒מַיָּא

Say: Y'hei sh'mei raba m'varach l'alam ul'almei almaya

Meaning: May His great Name be blessed forever and for all eternity.

Mourner

יִתְבָּרַךְ וְיִשְׁתַּבַּח וְיִתְפָּאַר וְיִתְרוֹמַם וְיִתְנַשֵּׂא וְיִתְהַדָּר וְיִתְעַלֶּה וְיִתְהַלָּל שְׁמֵהּ דְקוּדְשָׁא בְּרִיךְ הוּא

Say: Yitbarach v'yishtabach v'yitpa'ar v'yitromam v'yitnase v'yit'hadar v'yit'aleh v'yit'halal sh'mei d'kudsha b'rich hu

Meaning: Blessed and praised, glorified, and exalted and uplifted, honored and elevated and extolled be the Name of the Holy One, blessed is He;

Congregation answers: B’rich hu

Mourner

לְעֵֽלָּא מִן־כָּל־ בִּרְכָתָֽא וְשִׁירָתָֽא תֻּשְׁבְּ֒חָתָֽא וְנֶחָמָתָֽא דַּאֲמִירָן בְּעָלְ֒מָֽא וְאִמְרוּ אָמֵן:

Say: L'eila min kol birchata v'shirata tushbechata v'nechemata da'amiran b'alma v'imru Amen.

Meaning: above all the blessings and hymns, praises and consolations which we utter in the world—and say Amen.

Between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur say: לְעֵֽלָּא לְעֵֽלָּא מִכָּל (l'eila l'eila mikol).

Congregation answers: Amen

Mourner

יְהֵא שְׁלָמָֽא רַבָּֽא מִן־שְׁמַיָּֽא וְחַיִּים עָלֵֽינוּ וְעַל־כָּל־יִשְׂרָאֵל וְאִמְרוּ אָמֵן:

Say: Y'hei sh'lama raba min sh'maya v'chayim aleinu v'al kol yisra'el v'imru Amen.

Meaning: May there be abundant peace from heaven and a good life for us and for all Israel, —and say Amen.

Congregation answers: Amen

Mourner

עוֹשֶׂה שָׁלוֹם בִּמְרוֹמָיו הוּא יַעֲשֶׂה שָׁלוֹם עָלֵֽינוּ וְעַל כָּל יִשְׂרָאֵל וְאִמְרוּ אָמֵן:

Say: Oseh shalom bimromav hu ya'aseh shalom aleinu v'al kol yisra'el v'imru Amen.

Meaning: He Who makes peace in His high heavens may He, in His mercy, make peace for us and for all Israel, —and say Amen.

Between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur say: הַשָּׁלוֹם (hashalom) instead of shalom.

Congregation answers: Amen

In Nusach Sefard and among Sephardic communities, after וְיַמְלִיךְ מַלְכוּתֵהּ (v’yamlich malchutei) the words וְיַצְמַח פּוּרְקָנֵהּ וִיקָרֵב (קֵץ) מְשִׁיחֵהּ (v’yatzmach purkanei vikarev m’shichei) are added, and the congregation answers Amen. Follow the siddur of your synagogue.

How to say the Mourner's Kaddish

1. Wait for the place in the prayers

The Mourner's Kaddish is said at set points in the morning, afternoon and evening prayers, usually near the end of the service, after Aleinu or after a psalm. In many synagogues the rabbi or the prayer leader nods to the mourners when it is time. If you are new to the synagogue, tell the gabbai (the person who runs the service) that you are saying Kaddish, and he will help you.

2. Stand and say it aloud

Kaddish is said standing, loud enough for the congregation to hear, because their answers are the heart of the prayer. Read slowly. It is better to say every word clearly than to keep pace with someone else. If several mourners are present, they usually say it together.

3. Pause for the congregation's answers

After the first line the congregation answers “Amen”. After “v’imru Amen” in the second line, everyone says together “Amen. Y’hei sh’mei raba m’varach l’alam ul’almei almaya”. After “sh’mei d’kudsha” they answer “B’rich hu”, and after every “v’imru Amen” they answer “Amen”. On this page each of these answers is marked in a gold box.

4. Oseh shalom

The last line, “Oseh shalom bimromav”, is a prayer for peace. Many take three steps back before it and bow, and step forward again at the end. The customs of bowing differ between communities; watch how your synagogue does it.

Questions about your own family

Who says Kaddish, for which relatives, for how many months, and what to do when there is no minyan nearby are questions of Jewish law, with different customs between communities. Ask an Orthodox rabbi about your own situation. Many synagogues can also arrange for Kaddish to be said on your behalf.

What the Kaddish means

A prayer of praise, not of mourning

The word Kaddish comes from the root for “holy”. The prayer never mentions death or the person who passed away. It asks that God's great Name be made great and holy in the world, that His kingdom be revealed soon, in our lifetime, and that there be peace for us and for all Israel.

Y’hei sh’mei raba

The central line, “May His great Name be blessed forever and for all eternity”, is almost the same as a verse in the book of Daniel (2:20). When a child says Kaddish and the congregation answers these words, it is seen as a merit for the soul of the parent.

The ten praises

The fourth line lists ten words of praise: blessed, praised, glorified, exalted, uplifted, honored, elevated, extolled, and more. The Metsudah siddur explains that they match the ten utterances with which the world was created.

Why Aramaic

Most of the Kaddish is in Aramaic, the spoken language of the Jewish people when the prayer was formed, so that everyone in the synagogue would understand it. Only the last line, “Oseh shalom”, is in Hebrew.

The different kinds of Kaddish

  • Half Kaddish (Chatzi Kaddish) – said by the prayer leader between parts of the service; it ends after “da’amiran b’alma, v’imru Amen”.
  • Full Kaddish (Kaddish Shalem) – said by the prayer leader after the Amidah; it adds a line asking that our prayers be accepted.
  • Mourner's Kaddish (Kaddish Yatom) – the text on this page, said by mourners.
  • Rabbis' Kaddish (Kaddish d’Rabbanan) – said after learning Torah; it adds a prayer for Torah teachers and their students.

Yahrzeit: the yearly memorial day

A yahrzeit is the anniversary of a passing on the Hebrew calendar, so its date in the regular calendar changes every year. On the yahrzeit many say Kaddish, light a memorial candle, learn Torah and give charity in the memory of the one who passed away. To find the date, enter the day of passing in the yahrzeit calculator: it shows the Hebrew date and the yahrzeit dates for the next five years, with the evening on which each one begins.

Reading Hebrew with transliteration

The transliteration spells each word in English letters, the way it is said in most synagogues. “ch” sounds like the end of “Bach”, “tz” like the end of “cats”, and an apostrophe marks a very short vowel, as in sh’mei. Some communities say “sh’meh” and “malchuteh”; both are fine. To learn the Hebrew letters themselves, see the Hebrew alphabet page.

Questions and answers

What are the words to the Mourner's Kaddish?

It begins "Yitgadal v'yitkadash sh'mei raba" ("Exalted and sanctified be His great Name"). The congregation answers "Amen" and "Y'hei sh'mei raba m'varach l'alam ul'almei almaya" ("May His great Name be blessed forever and for all eternity"), and the Kaddish ends with "Oseh shalom bimromav", a prayer for peace. The full text is on this page in Hebrew with vowels, in transliteration and in English, line by line.

How do you say Kaddish if you cannot read Hebrew?

Read the transliteration line marked "Say" under each Hebrew line: it spells the Aramaic words in English letters. "ch" is said like the ch in the Scottish word loch, and an apostrophe marks a short vowel or a short break, as in sh'mei. Press "Big print" to read it from your phone in the synagogue, or print it and keep it in your siddur.

What is the difference between Kaddish and the Mourner's Kaddish?

Kaddish is a family of prayers said by the prayer leader and by mourners. The Half Kaddish ends after "da'amiran b'alma"; the Full Kaddish adds a request that our prayers be accepted; the Rabbis' Kaddish (Kaddish d'Rabbanan) adds a prayer for Torah scholars. The Mourner's Kaddish (Kaddish Yatom) is the version on this page, said by mourners after the prayers.

What language is the Kaddish in?

Mostly Aramaic, the everyday language of the Jewish people in the time of the Talmud, with the last line, "Oseh shalom", in Hebrew. This is why some words look different from the Hebrew of other prayers.

Why is the Kaddish said for someone who passed away?

The Kaddish does not mention death at all. It praises God's great Name. A child saying it in front of the congregation brings others to answer "Amen, Y'hei sh'mei raba", and this is understood as a merit for the soul of the parent who passed away.

Who says Kaddish, for how long, and can a woman say it?

These are questions of Jewish law, and the customs differ between communities. Ask an Orthodox rabbi about your own family. The rabbi of your synagogue can also arrange for Kaddish to be said when you cannot be there.

Can you say Kaddish alone?

Kaddish is said with a minyan, a group of ten men praying together, because the congregation's answers are the heart of it. If you cannot get to a minyan, ask your rabbi what to do.

When do you bow during the Mourner's Kaddish?

Many congregations bow at certain words of the Kaddish, and before "Oseh shalom" the one saying it takes three small steps back, bows to the left, to the right and forward, and then steps forward again at the end. Customs differ; watch how your synagogue does it or ask the rabbi.

What is a yahrzeit, and when is it?

A yahrzeit is the yearly anniversary of a passing on the Hebrew calendar. Many say Kaddish and light a memorial candle on that day. To find the Hebrew date of passing and the yahrzeit dates for the coming years, use the free yahrzeit calculator on our Hebrew date converter.
