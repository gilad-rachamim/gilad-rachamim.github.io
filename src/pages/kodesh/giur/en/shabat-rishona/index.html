How to keep Shabbat for the first time

A first Shabbat is a big experience, and sometimes a stressful one. This page has a simple preparation checklist, a link to start and end times by city, and the Friday night Kiddush with full vowels and transliteration. The list is saved on your phone, and everything is free, no sign-up and no ads.

The tool helps you learn and organize. Every halachic and personal question – to your accompanying rabbi or the beit din.

Why it is worth preparing in advance

Shabbat is a day that begins in the evening and shapes everything that happens before it: shopping, cooking, tidying the house and planning the meals. Whoever prepares on Thursday and Friday enters Shabbat calmer and feels the rest. You do not have to manage everything, and you can start with one thing.

How to use the checklist

The list is divided into four parts: before Shabbat, Friday night, Shabbat day and after Shabbat. Mark each item you have already done, and the marks are saved. When you want, send your accompanying rabbi on WhatsApp what you have marked, to get advice that fits you.

Finding a host family

According to the official site of the conversion authority, during the studies there is hosting with families on Shabbat and holidays, and there is a service for matching an accompanying family. A Shabbat in a family's home is the best chance to learn up close, ask questions and feel part of a community.

Shabbat times

The beginning and end of Shabbat change by place and season. Do not try to remember a fixed hour; check every week in Shabbat times for your city (Hebrew), so you can prepare on time.

The Kiddush text

Kiddush is a blessing over wine at the beginning of Shabbat, and it declares the holiness of the day. The page has the full text from the siddur with vowels, and a transliteration to help with reading. Over time you move to reading the text in Hebrew. The text and the custom differ between communities, so it is worth asking your accompanying rabbi which version suits you.

Lighting candles

Lighting Shabbat candles is part of the preparations. You learn the blessing text and the details from the rabbi or the woman who accompanies you and from your host family, because this page does not publish a text that was not checked against a source.

On Shabbat day

In the morning you can go to a synagogue, listen and get used to it. Then a meal with the host family, rest, conversation and reading. You do not need to know everything. Someone who comes for the first time usually receives a smile and patience.

After Shabbat

On Saturday night it is worth writing down what you liked and what was hard, and preparing questions for your accompanying rabbi. That way the next Shabbat is already easier. The progress adds up, and Shabbat after Shabbat you feel more at home.

Frequently asked questions about the first Shabbat

How do I prepare for my first Shabbat?

Start with the checklist on the page: talk with your accompanying rabbi, find a host family, check the times and prepare food in advance. There is no need to do everything at once.

When does Shabbat begin?

It changes by place and week. You can see the exact time for your city in the Shabbat times tool on the site.

What is Kiddush?

A blessing over wine at the beginning of Shabbat. The text appears on the page, with vowels and transliteration.

I cannot read Hebrew. Can I still do it?

Yes. There is a transliteration for reading, and over time you move to the Hebrew text. You can also listen to others and repeat after them.

How do I find a host family?

According to the official site of the conversion authority, there is a service for matching an accompanying family. You can also ask your accompanying rabbi or the community.

Do I have to know everything before my first Shabbat?

No. The first Shabbat is a beginning. You ask, listen and learn over time.

What do I do if something is unclear?

Ask your accompanying rabbi or your host family. The tool does not give halachic rulings.

Can I print the checklist?

Yes. There is a print button, and your marks are printed with it.

Where are my marks saved?

Only in your browser. We do not keep them on a server.

Is this tool free?

Yes. Free, no limits, no sign-up and no ads.
