Glossary

Type a word in the search and see the explanation. If a word is missing, you can ask us with the button below.

Beit din (rabbinical court)
A body of three judges (dayanim) that accepts the convert after the studies. The beit din asks questions about the personal path, the family and the learning.
Dayan
A judge in a beit din. In conversion, the dayanim listen, ask and decide.
Accompanying rabbi
A rabbi or teacher who accompanies the learner along the way, answers questions and guides. Every personal question and ruling is asked of him.
Accompanying family
A family that hosts on Shabbat and holidays and accompanies the convert into the community. According to the official site of the conversion authority, there is a service for matching an accompanying family.
Conversion authority (Maarach HaGiyur)
The government body that arranges conversion studies in Israel, including referral to the beit din and help with documents.
Kabbalat mitzvot (accepting the mitzvot)
A declaration said before the beit din. The official site quotes it in Hebrew: “אני מקבל על עצמי לשמור ולקיים את כל המצוות הכתובות בתורה ואת המצוות מדברי חכמים ואת המנהגים הכשרים בישראל ואני מאמין בה' אחד”. In translation: “I accept upon myself to keep and fulfil all the mitzvot written in the Torah, the mitzvot of the words of the Sages and the proper customs of Israel, and I believe in one God.”
Tevila (immersion)
Immersion in a mikveh, a stage at the end of the conversion process. Women who convert receive guidance from the mikveh attendant.
Brit milah
Removal of the foreskin for men. In conversion it is done according to halacha by a mohel, in the presence of a doctor and a beit din team, according to the official site.
Hatafat dam brit
A short procedure for someone who had a circumcision not for the sake of conversion. According to the official site, it is a simple procedure that does not require surgical medical intervention.
Conversion certificate (teudat giyur)
The document received from the beit din at the end of the conversion process.
Change certificate (teudat hamara)
A certificate of the Ministry of Interior received at the end of the process, according to the official site.
Hebrew name
A name the convert chooses and receives from the beit din at the end of the process.
Shabbat
The seventh day of the week, a day of rest and holiness. It begins on Friday evening and ends on Saturday night.
Kiddush
A blessing over wine at the beginning of Shabbat or a holiday, declaring the holiness of the day.
Havdalah
Blessings at the end of Shabbat that separate Shabbat from the weekdays.
Kashrut
The food rules in Judaism. The details are learned with the accompanying rabbi.
Birkot hanehenin (blessings of enjoyment)
Blessings said before eating or drinking. See the blessings over food page on the site.
Birkat hamazon (grace after meals)
A blessing said after eating bread. The texts are in the bencher on the site.
Tefila (prayer)
A way of turning to God, three times a day: Shacharit, Mincha and Arvit.
Shacharit
The morning prayer.
Mincha
The afternoon prayer.
Arvit (Maariv)
The evening prayer.
Siddur
A book that contains the texts of the prayers.
Synagogue (beit knesset)
The place of prayer and of the community.
Minyan
A group of ten men required for some of the prayers. Questions about details – to the accompanying rabbi.
Tanach
The holy books: Torah, Prophets and Writings.
Torah
The five books of Moses: Bereshit, Shemot, Vayikra, Bamidbar and Devarim.
Mishnah
A collection of the words of the Sages, the basis of the Talmud.
Talmud
A foundational work that includes the Mishnah and the Gemara.
Halacha
The way in Judaism, and the rules that explain how to keep the mitzvot.
Mitzvah
A commandment or a good deed in Judaism.
Mezuzah
A parchment in a case on the doorpost.
Tefillin
Boxes with parchment and straps, placed on the arm and the head in the weekday morning prayer.
Tzitzit
Strings tied at the corners of a garment.
Challah
A special Shabbat bread.
Rosh Chodesh
The beginning of a Hebrew month.
Shofar
A horn that is blown on Rosh Hashanah.
Sukkah
A temporary structure in which one sits during the holiday of Sukkot.
Megillah
A written scroll. The Scroll of Esther is read on Purim.
Haggadah
A book that accompanies the Seder night on Passover.
Aron kodesh (holy ark)
A cabinet in the synagogue in which the Torah scrolls are kept.

Judaism glossary and conversion documents

Whoever starts learning about Judaism meets many new words: Kiddush, Havdalah, minyan, halacha and more. This page has a short glossary in plain English, with search, and a list of documents for opening a conversion request as it appears on the official site. Everything is free, no sign-up and no ads.

The tool helps you learn and organize. Every halachic and personal question – to your accompanying rabbi or the beit din.

How to use the glossary

Type a word in the search box and the list narrows down. You can search by the name of the term or by a word from the explanation. The explanations are short on purpose, so they are easy to understand, and the details are learned with your accompanying rabbi.

Terms of the conversion process

Beit din, dayan, accompanying rabbi, accompanying family, conversion authority, kabbalat mitzvot, tevila, conversion certificate and change certificate. These are the terms you meet along the process. The explanations rely on the official site of the conversion authority, and the page Conversion to Judaism in Israel has a full roadmap.

Shabbat and holiday terms

Shabbat, Kiddush, Havdalah, Rosh Chodesh, shofar, sukkah, megillah and Haggadah. Whoever is preparing for the first Shabbat can continue to My first Shabbat.

Prayer and blessing terms

Shacharit, Mincha, Arvit, siddur, minyan and synagogue, as well as the blessings over food and Birkat hamazon. There is a special page for practicing the blessings: Blessings over food.

Terms for the books

Tanach, Torah, Mishnah, Talmud and halacha. These are the foundational books and the terms that come up again and again in classes. The explanations here only open the door.

Objects and mitzvot

Mezuzah, tefillin, tzitzit and challah. These are objects you can see at home and in the synagogue. It is worth asking your accompanying rabbi to show them up close.

The documents list

According to the official site, citizens and permanent residents attach a copy of the identity card including the appendix and an extended registry extract from the Population and Immigration Authority. Holders of a legal stay visa attach a copy of the temporary identity card including the appendix, and a copy of the passport. The request is submitted online, free of charge, and at the end you receive a reference number.

Who to contact after submitting

After the request is received in the district, you are contacted to continue. If needed, you can call the district or the government call centre 1299. The up-to-date details are on the official site.

You can print it

There is a print button for the documents list, so you can take it to meetings. The marks are saved only on your phone. This page is also in Hebrew and Russian.

Frequently asked questions about the glossary and documents

Which documents do I need to attach to a conversion request?

According to the official site, a copy of the identity card including the appendix and an extended registry extract from the Population and Immigration Authority. For visa holders: a copy of the temporary identity card including the appendix and a copy of the passport.

How much does it cost to submit a request?

According to the official site, the service is free of charge.

What is a reference number?

A number you receive at the end of the online submission. It is worth keeping, because it helps identify the request when you make inquiries.

What is kabbalat mitzvot?

A declaration before the beit din, which appears on the official site. The full text is quoted in the glossary, in Hebrew with a translation.

What is the difference between a conversion certificate and a change certificate?

According to the official site, at the end of the process you receive a conversion certificate from the beit din and a change certificate from the Ministry of Interior.

What is an accompanying rabbi?

A rabbi or teacher who accompanies the learner, answers questions and guides. Personal halachic questions go to him.

What is an accompanying family?

A family that hosts on Shabbat and holidays and helps you enter the community. According to the official site, there is a service for matching an accompanying family.

Where can I see the up-to-date details?

On gov.il. The page links to the official service and to the information on the conversion authority services.

Does the glossary contain all the terms?

No, these are the common terms. You can ask us to add terms with the button on the page.

Is this tool free?

Yes. Free, no limits, no sign-up and no ads.
