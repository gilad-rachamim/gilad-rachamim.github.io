Conversion to Judaism in Israel: what a convert goes through, without mystery

Deciding to join the Jewish people is one of the biggest decisions in life, and the conversion process can look complicated from the outside. This page arranges it into seven simple stages: inquiry, opening a file, learning and experience, meetings with the beit din, accepting the mitzvot, brit and immersion, and the certificate. Every fact here is taken from the official site of the Conversion Authority, with a link, and if something is not clear – ask your accompanying rabbi.

The tool helps you learn and organize. Every halachic and personal question – to your accompanying rabbi or the beit din. The page does not rule on halacha and does not promise any outcome.

How long does conversion to Judaism take

According to the official site of the Conversion Authority, the course of study usually lasts between ten months and a year. Early on, after about two months, there is a first meeting with the beit din, and at the end of eight months of study there is a summary interview. The time differs from person to person, and there is no "right" or "wrong". What matters is to learn, to gain experience and to feel at home.

Who can apply to open a conversion process in Israel

According to the online service on gov.il, you can apply as an Israeli citizen or a permanent resident, and also as a holder of a legal stay visa in Israel, if no application was submitted before. The service is free of charge.

Which documents to attach to the application

In the online application you attach a copy of your ID card including the appendix (biometric – both sides) and an extended registry extract from the Population and Immigration Authority. Visa holders attach a copy of a temporary ID including the appendix and a copy of their passport. It is worth checking the full list of documents for the beit din hearing with the Conversion Authority, because it gets updated. You have a printable list with what appears on the official site.

What happens after you apply

When you finish applying you receive a reference number. After the file is received in a district, they contact you to continue, and you can also call the district: Jerusalem 02-5099100, Center 03-7406100, South 08-6620900, North 04-6718104. Phone numbers change, so please check on the site.

What the learning looks like

The preparation studies include Jewish thought, halacha, Tanach and Jewish history, with professional teachers. At the same time you are hosted by families on Shabbat and holidays, pray in a synagogue and go on tours. Someone who does not want to study in a class can turn directly to the districts of the Conversion Authority. If you need to study in a language other than Hebrew, ask the Conversion Authority what is available.

A host family: why it matters so much

The host family is a way into society and a living example of a Jewish home: people who host you on Shabbat, explain, answer questions and simply embrace you. According to the official site, converts sometimes live in loneliness, so such a family changes a lot. You can ask the Conversion Authority to match you with a host family. The Torah says "and you shall love the convert", and this is not only a mitzvah but also the way of life of a community.

What happens at the beit din hearing

The hearing is a conversation about family, personal, thought, history and halacha topics. It is not an oral exam on every detail; the beit din is interested mainly in your path, your sincerity and your commitment. Sometimes it asks you to return after completing tasks, and this is a normal part of the way. To get used to the questions you can use the knowledge practice, which is not an official exam.

The declaration of accepting the mitzvot

At the end of the hearing you make a declaration, say "Shema Yisrael" and choose a Hebrew name. The meaning of the official wording appears in stage 5 of the map above. Then the new convert is greeted with "welcome, in a good hour and mazal tov".

Brit milah and immersion

Conversion is made up of immersion, and for men also of milah. According to the official site, the milah is done by a mohel in a hospital, in the presence of a doctor and a beit din team. A man who was already circumcised not for the sake of conversion needs hatafat dam brit. You can read about it on the page Brit milah in conversion, and the actual decision is made by the beit din and the accompanying rabbi.

After the conversion

You receive a conversion certificate and a certificate of change of religion from the Ministry of Interior. Marriage registration for converts is free of charge, according to the official site. On the first Shabbat you may be honored with an aliyah to the Torah, and life goes on: more mitzvot, more Shabbatot, and a community that counts on you. You can build the way with My mitzvot, step by step.

The tools on the site are a chesed (kindness) project: free, no ads, no sign-up and no limits. Everything runs on your phone, and the marks are saved only with you. The pages are also available in Hebrew and Russian.

Frequently asked questions about conversion to Judaism in Israel

How long does conversion to Judaism take in Israel?

According to the official site of the Conversion Authority, the course of study lasts between ten months and a year, with a first meeting with the beit din after about two months and a summary interview at the end of eight months of study. In practice the time varies, so ask your accompanying rabbi.

How do I start the conversion process?

Turn to a body that teaches for conversion, or submit an online application to open a conversion process on gov.il. The service is free of charge, and at the end you receive a reference number.

Which documents are needed to open a conversion file?

In the online application you attach a copy of your ID card including the appendix and an extended registry extract from the Population and Immigration Authority. Visa holders attach a temporary ID and a passport. Check the list of documents for the beit din hearing with the Conversion Authority.

What is a host family?

A family that hosts the convert on Shabbat and holidays, shows a Jewish home from the inside and helps them enter the community. You can ask the Conversion Authority to match you with a family.

Are the studies and the service free?

According to the official site, opening the application is free, Jewish studies are given free, marriage registration for converts is free and help with clarifying Jewish status is free. For other costs, ask directly.

What does the beit din ask?

The hearing is a conversation about family, personal, thought, history and halacha topics. The beit din is interested mainly in your personal path, so it is better to be sincere than to recite answers.

What is the brit milah stage in conversion?

According to the official site, conversion is made up of immersion and milah. A man who already had a circumcision not for the sake of conversion needs hatafat dam brit. Each case is individual, and the decision is made by the beit din and the accompanying rabbi.

How do I receive a conversion certificate?

At the end of the process you receive a conversion certificate from the special conversion courts and a certificate of change of religion from the Ministry of Interior, according to the official site.

Can I consult a mohel before the brit?

Yes. You can ask questions and get general guidance, and the halachic and practical decision is made by the beit din and the accompanying rabbi. The page Brit milah in conversion has a link to get in touch.

Is this tool free?

Yes. Everything is free, with no sign-up, no ads and no limits, and the marks are saved only on your phone.
