What the official site says

The information here is taken from the official site of the conversion authority (gov.il, checked on 22.11.2026). For full and current details, it is worth reading there.

  • Conversion consists of the act of immersion (tevila) and circumcision (milah).
  • Circumcision for men is done according to halacha by a mohel, in a hospital, in the presence of a doctor and a beit din team.
  • Someone who had a circumcision not for the sake of conversion needs hatafat dam brit for the sake of conversion. According to the site, it is "a very simple and short procedure that does not require surgical medical intervention".
  • Immersion is done after the place of the brit has healed.
  • At the end you receive a conversion certificate from the beit din and a change certificate from the Ministry of Interior.

Brit milah for converts: understanding this stage of conversion

For a man who converts, brit milah is part of the conversion process. It is a stage that raises questions and sometimes fear, so it is worth understanding it before you get there. On this page there is a short, calm explanation according to the official site of the conversion authority, and a list of questions worth preparing before the meeting. Everything is free, with no sign-up and no ads.

The tool helps you learn and organize. Every halachic and personal question – to your accompanying rabbi or the beit din. This page contains no medical instructions.

What brit milah in conversion is

According to the official site, conversion consists of the act of immersion and circumcision. Circumcision for men is done according to halacha by a mohel. It is a stage within a longer process that includes studies, meetings with the beit din and kabbalat mitzvot, so there is no need to deal with it too early.

Where it takes place

The official site states that the circumcision is done in a hospital, in the presence of a doctor and a beit din team. So the process takes place within an organized framework, and you are not alone. The details of the place and date are set through the beit din and your accompanying rabbi.

What hatafat dam brit is

According to the official site, someone who had a circumcision not for the sake of conversion needs hatafat dam brit for the sake of conversion. The site describes it as a very simple and short procedure that does not require surgical medical intervention. In every personal case, your accompanying rabbi and the beit din can explain what is relevant for you.

Immersion after the brit

Immersion is done after the place of the brit has healed. The exact schedule is set by the doctor, the beit din and your accompanying rabbi, not by this tool.

Who to turn to

The process is coordinated by your accompanying rabbi and the beit din. Whoever is looking for adult brit milah or hatafat dam brit can write to Gilad, a certified mohel, on WhatsApp or by email. Gilad will take care of you with love and connect you with a professional, experienced mohel who works in a proper clinic.

What to ask

The page has a list of ready questions: when and where, who coordinates, which documents to bring, what the schedule is afterwards, and what it means if you had a circumcision in the past. You can mark them and send them to your accompanying rabbi on WhatsApp.

About fears

Fears before such a stage are natural. It is worth talking about them with your accompanying rabbi, and with a doctor about medical matters. You do not have to cope alone, and many people went through this stage before you.

How to prepare calmly

Good preparation is mostly information and conversation. It is recommended to read the official explanation, prepare questions and ask your accompanying rabbi at the meeting. It is also worth sharing with someone close, a family member or a friend, so that you are not alone with your fears. There is no need to hurry, and the stage comes when the beit din and your accompanying rabbi decide that the time has come.

Why it is part of conversion

In the Jewish tradition, brit milah is the entry into the covenant. That is why, according to the official site, it is part of the act of conversion for men, together with immersion. The full personal and halachic explanation is given by your accompanying rabbi and the beit din, not by this page.

After the conversion

At the end of the process you receive a conversion certificate and a change certificate, and you choose a Hebrew name. You can continue to my mitzvot and my first Shabbat.
