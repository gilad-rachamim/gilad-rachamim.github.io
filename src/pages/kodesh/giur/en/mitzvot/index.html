Mitzvot for beginners: how to progress in small steps

A person on the way to conversion meets many new things at once: Shabbat, kashrut, blessings, prayer and holidays. This page divides the way into six areas, and each area has a few small steps you can mark. There are no grades and no exam. There is only a list that helps you see how far you have come and what the next step is. Everything is free, no sign-up and no ads.

The tool helps you learn and organize. Every halachic and personal question – to your accompanying rabbi or the beit din.

Why small steps work

When the goal is big, it is easy to feel overwhelmed. A small step, like learning one blessing or visiting a synagogue once, is something you can do this week. When you mark it, you feel real progress. Many people on the way to conversion say the hard part is knowing where to start, and the list here answers exactly that.

How to use the mitzvot tracker

Choose an area with the buttons, read the steps, and mark what you have already done. Your progress is saved only on your phone, and you can come back to it at any time. When you want, send a short summary on WhatsApp to your accompanying rabbi, so the rabbi knows where you stand and can help.

Shabbat

The Shabbat area has preparation steps: check the times, read about the preparations, practice the Kiddush and be hosted for one Shabbat. Whoever is preparing for the first Shabbat can continue to the first Shabbat page. The texts and customs depend on the community and the accompanying rabbi.

Kashrut

Kashrut is learned gradually. It is worth getting to know the terms, going over the kitchen with your rabbi, and starting to read kashrut marks on packages. The glossary page has short explanations of words that come up often.

Blessings

The blessings are the most accessible way to bring mitzvot into life. Start with the blessings before eating; the page Blessings over food has short practice without grades. Then add more and more.

Prayer

Prayer starts with a comfortable siddur and a visit to a synagogue. Then you practice the Shema, get to know the structure of the prayer, and look for a regular minyan. The site has prayer pages in different versions to help (in Hebrew).

Holidays

In the holidays area you start with the calendar, choose a coming holiday and read about it, and plan where to celebrate. No rush: every holiday is a chance to learn something new and to be with people.

Between people

The way also includes what is between people: getting acquainted, volunteering, visiting the sick and giving tzedakah. These are simple steps that connect you to the community, and usually also the most moving ones.

The step of the week

Each time, the tool suggests one step that is not marked yet, so you do not have to decide where to continue. If it does not suit you, choose another. The suggestion is only an idea.

How to talk about it with your accompanying rabbi

The list is also meant to open a conversation. You can send the summary and add one question about what is unclear. Your accompanying rabbi can decide what suits you and your pace. The whole area is on the page Conversion to Judaism in Israel, and this page is also in Hebrew and Russian.

Frequently asked questions about the mitzvot tracker

Does the tool check whether I am ready for conversion?

No. The tool is a personal list for organizing your learning; it decides nothing and does not promise any outcome at the beit din. The preparation for the judges is managed by your accompanying rabbi and the beit din.

Do I have to mark everything?

No. The steps are ideas, and you can skip what does not suit you. Your accompanying rabbi can recommend what is important for you now.

Where are my marks saved?

Only on your phone or computer, in the browser. We do not keep them on a server. If you clear the browser data, the marks will be deleted.

How do I send a summary to my rabbi?

Tap "Send on WhatsApp to my rabbi", choose a contact and confirm. The summary is created from your marks only.

Can I print the list?

Yes, there is a print button. The area that is open right now is printed with the marks.

What if I do not understand a step?

Ask your accompanying rabbi, or look up the term in the glossary on the site.

Are the steps halachic instructions?

No. They are ideas for learning and experience. Every halachic question – to your accompanying rabbi or the beit din.

How long does it take to mark everything?

There is no fixed time. Everyone goes at their own pace, and many come back to the list for months.

Can I use it without being in a conversion process?

Yes. Anyone interested in Judaism, or accompanying a convert, can use the page.

Is this tool free?

Yes. Free, no limits, no sign-up and no ads.
