Which Hebrew year is 2026?

Today is 12 Kislev 5787 (י״ב בכסלו תשפ״ז), in the Hebrew year 5787. The Jewish year begins in the autumn, on Rosh Hashanah, the 1st of Tishrei. So 2026 spans two Hebrew years: until Rosh Hashanah it was 5786, and from Rosh Hashanah (September 12, 2026) it is 5787. The next year, 5788, begins on October 2, 2027, starting the evening before. The year 5787 is a leap year with 13 months.

Why the Hebrew date changes in the evening

On the Jewish calendar a day begins at sunset, not at midnight, as the Torah says: "and there was evening and there was morning". So on Wednesday evening, after sunset, it is already Thursday's Hebrew date. The page checks the time and the sunset in the city you choose (or where you are) and shows the right Hebrew date for this moment. In the converter, tick "After sunset" when you look up the Hebrew date of a birth or an event that happened in the evening. Very close to sunset, in the time called bein hashmashot, there is a doubt, and practical questions go to a rabbi.

Hebrew date converter: Gregorian to Hebrew and back

Pick any Gregorian date from 1241 to 2238 and see the Hebrew date, the day of the week, and the holiday or Torah portion of that day. To go the other way, choose a Hebrew day, month and year, and get the Gregorian date. The year can be typed as a number, like 5787, or in Hebrew letters, like תשפ״ז. The list of months changes by itself: in a leap year there are Adar I and Adar II. If you pick a date that does not exist that year, such as 30 Cheshvan in a year when Cheshvan has 29 days, the page says so and does not invent a date.

Hebrew birthday and Bar Mitzvah date

A Bar Mitzvah is on a boy's 13th Hebrew birthday, and a Bat Mitzvah on a girl's 12th Hebrew birthday. Enter the Gregorian date of birth, tick whether the birth was after sunset, and you get the Hebrew birth date, the Bar or Bat Mitzvah date on both calendars, the Shabbat after it with its Torah portion (when the boy is usually called up to the Torah), and a list of Hebrew birthdays for the coming years. The calculator handles leap years with two months of Adar and months without a 30th day.

Yahrzeit calculator

A yahrzeit is the yearly anniversary of a passing on the Hebrew calendar: a memorial candle is lit, Kaddish is said, and the family often visits the grave. Enter the Gregorian date of passing and tick whether it was after sunset. The page shows the Hebrew date of passing and the yahrzeit dates for the next five years, with the evening on which each one begins. A passing in Adar or on the 30th of a month has special rules; the calculation follows the common custom, and in doubtful cases ask a rabbi.

The 12 months of the Hebrew calendar

The Hebrew calendar is lunisolar: months follow the moon and years are aligned with the sun. Each month has 29 or 30 days and begins with Rosh Chodesh. The months are Tishrei, Cheshvan, Kislev, Tevet, Shevat, Adar, Nisan, Iyar, Sivan, Tammuz, Av and Elul. Seven times in nineteen years a leap year adds a 13th month, and then there are Adar I and Adar II. The full month-by-month view, with holidays and fast days, is on the Hebrew calendar page.
