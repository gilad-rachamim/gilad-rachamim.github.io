Learning the Hebrew alphabet

The Hebrew alphabet, called the Alef-Bet after its first two letters, has 22 letters. It is the alphabet of the Torah, of the prayer book and of modern Israeli Hebrew, and the same letters are used to write Yiddish and Ladino. Every letter is a consonant; the vowels are added as small marks. Once you know the 22 letters, the five final forms and about ten vowel signs, you can sound out any word in a Jewish prayer book, even before you understand it.

Reading from right to left

Hebrew is written from right to left. In a printed book the first page is on what an English reader would call the back. Letters are not joined in print, and there are no capital letters. Some letters look alike, so it is worth practising them in pairs: Bet (ב) and Kaf (כ), Dalet (ד) and Resh (ר), Hei (ה) and Chet (ח), Vav (ו) and Zayin (ז), Gimel (ג) and Nun (נ), Samech (ס) and final Mem (ם), Ayin (ע) and Tsadi (צ).

Final letters

Five letters have a special form at the end of a word: Chaf (ך), Mem (ם), Nun (ן), Fei (ף) and Tsadi (ץ). Most of them have a long tail that goes below the line. The final letters do not add new sounds; they only mark where a word ends, which helped readers in the days when words were written close together.

Letters with a dot inside

A dot inside a letter is called a dagesh. In three letters it changes the sound: Bet (בּ, B) and Vet (ב, V), Kaf (כּ, K) and Chaf (כ, CH), Pei (פּ, P) and Fei (פ, F). The letter Shin carries a dot on top: on the right side it is SH (שׁ), on the left side it is S (שׂ), and then it is called Sin. In everyday Hebrew without vowels you learn which sound to use from the word itself.

The vowels: nikud

Hebrew vowels were added to the text about 1,200 years ago by scholars called the Masoretes, to preserve the exact reading of the Torah. Kamatz and Patach both sound like "a", Tsere and Segol like "e", Chirik like "i", Cholam like "o", and Kubutz and Shuruk like "u". The Shva is a very short sound or no sound at all. In prayer books and children's books the vowels are printed; in newspapers, signs and text messages they are left out.

Letters as numbers

Before Arabic numerals reached Europe, Hebrew used its letters as numbers, and it still does for Hebrew dates, years, chapters of the Bible and page numbers in the Talmud. Alef to Tet count 1 to 9, Yod to Tsadi count by tens, and Kuf to Tav count by hundreds. Fifteen and sixteen are written טו and טז (9 + 6, 9 + 7) by custom. Print, handwriting and Torah script

The letters in this chart are the printed "square" letters. Israelis write by hand in a cursive script whose shapes differ quite a lot, and a Torah scroll, tefillin and a mezuzah are written by a scribe in a special calligraphy with small crowns on some letters. Start with the printed letters: they are the ones in every prayer book.

How to learn the Hebrew letters fast

Learn four or five letters a day, starting with the ones that have an easy English match: Bet, Gimel, Dalet, Lamed, Mem, Nun, Shin. Say each letter's name out loud and think of its example word. After a few days, use the quiz on this page until you get ten right in a row, then move on to the vowels and read simple words such as שַׁבָּת (Shabbat) and תּוֹרָה (Torah). Printing the chart and keeping it next to your prayer book helps too.

Questions about the Hebrew alphabet

How many letters are in the Hebrew alphabet?

The Hebrew alphabet has 22 letters, from Alef (א) to Tav (ת). Five of them – Kaf, Mem, Nun, Pei and Tsadi – have a second form used only at the end of a word (ך ם ן ף ץ), so you will sometimes see the count given as 27 forms. All the letters are consonants; vowels are written as dots and lines under, above or inside the letters.

What are the 22 letters of the Hebrew alphabet?

In order: Alef (א), Bet / Vet (ב), Gimel (ג), Dalet (ד), Hei (ה), Vav (ו), Zayin (ז), Chet (ח), Tet (ט), Yod (י), Kaf / Chaf (כ), Lamed (ל), Mem (מ), Nun (נ), Samech (ס), Ayin (ע), Pei / Fei (פ), Tsadi (צ), Kuf (ק), Resh (ר), Shin / Sin (ש), Tav (ת). The chart at the top of this page shows each one with its sound and number.

What are the first four letters of the Hebrew alphabet?

Alef (א), Bet (ב), Gimel (ג) and Dalet (ד). The word "alphabet" itself comes from the first two letters, through the Greek alpha and beta.

How is 666 written in Hebrew?

In Hebrew letters 666 is written תרס״ו: Tav (400) + Resh (200) + Samech (60) + Vav (6). The two small strokes before the last letter (gershayim) show that the letters stand for a number. Type any number in the "Numbers in Hebrew letters" box on this page to see it written out.

Is the Yiddish alphabet the same as Hebrew?

Yiddish is written with the same 22 Hebrew letters, but it uses some of them as vowels: Alef, Ayin, Vav and Yod stand for vowel sounds, and a few letter pairs (such as two Vavs) have their own sounds. So a reader of Hebrew can sound out Yiddish letters, but the spelling rules and the language are different.

Does Hebrew have vowels?

Yes, but they are not letters. Vowels are small marks called nikud, written under, inside or above the letters. Prayer books, the Bible and children's books print them; newspapers and most everyday Hebrew leave them out, and readers fill in the vowels from context. The vowel table on this page shows every mark with its sound.

Is Hebrew written from right to left?

Yes. Hebrew is written and read from right to left, and a book opens from what an English reader would call the back. Numbers written in digits (1, 2, 3) are still read from left to right inside a Hebrew sentence.

What is the difference between Bet and Vet, Kaf and Chaf, Pei and Fei?

They are the same letter with or without a dot inside, called a dagesh. With the dot: B, K, P. Without it: V, a throaty CH, F. Shin and Sin are also one letter: a dot on the right side means SH, on the left side S.

How do I write my name in Hebrew?

Hebrew spells names by sound, so pick the letter for each sound in your name using the chart on this page: for example, David is דוד and Sarah is שרה. Many English names have more than one accepted Hebrew spelling. For a name on a ketubah, a gravestone or any formal document, ask a rabbi or a fluent Hebrew speaker to check the spelling.

What is the numerical value of the Hebrew letters?

Every letter is also a number: Alef to Tet are 1 to 9, Yod to Tsadi are 10 to 90, and Kuf to Tav are 100 to 400. Larger numbers are written by adding letters, so 18 is יח (10 + 8). This is the basis of gematria, where the letters of a word are added up.
