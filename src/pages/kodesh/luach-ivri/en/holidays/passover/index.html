About Passover

Passover 2027 falls on April 22, 2027 – April 29, 2027 and begins on the Wednesday evening, April 21, 2027. Last time it was on April 2, 2026 – April 9, 2026. Below is general background about the holiday: what it celebrates, its main customs and why its date changes from year to year. It is a short introduction, not a guide to practice; for questions about how to keep the holiday, ask a rabbi.

What Passover celebrates

Passover, Pesach in Hebrew, begins on the 15th of Nisan and celebrates the Exodus, when the Israelites left slavery in Egypt. The Torah calls it the festival of matzot and the festival of spring. In Israel Passover lasts seven days; outside Israel it lasts eight. The first and last days are Yom Tov, days on which work is not done, and the days in between are Chol HaMoed, the intermediate days.

The Seder night

On the first night of Passover families gather for the Seder, a festive meal in a set order, and read the Haggadah, the story of the Exodus. Its parts include Kiddush over the first of four cups of wine, the four questions asked by the youngest child, eating matzah and maror (bitter herbs), and songs of praise at the end. Outside Israel a second Seder is held on the second night. For the Kiddush text in Hebrew, transliteration and English, see Kiddush.

Chametz and matzah

All through Passover no chametz is eaten or kept: bread, cakes, pasta, beer and anything made from wheat, barley, spelt, oats or rye that was allowed to rise. Before the holiday the home is cleaned, a search for chametz is made on the evening before Passover, and many sell the chametz they keep through a rabbi. Matzah, unleavened bread, is eaten in its place. Questions about which foods are kosher for Passover, and about medicines, should be asked of a rabbi.

Counting the Omer

On the second night of Passover the counting of the Omer begins: forty-nine days, counted each evening, leading up to Shavuot. Each evening has its own count and blessing; you can see tonight's count on the page Omer count tonight.

Passover in the USA and in Israel

Because Passover is eight days outside Israel and seven in Israel, families who travel or have relatives in Israel see different dates for the last days. The table "Passover in Israel and outside Israel" on this page shows both, for this year and the next.

Why Passover begins in the evening

On the Hebrew calendar each day begins at sunset, not at midnight. So Passover begins on the evening before the first date in the tables, and ends at nightfall on its last day. That is why the box at the top of this page shows both the date and the evening it begins. Candle lighting is usually about 18 minutes before sunset, and each city has its own times.

Questions and answers about Passover

When is Passover 2027?

Passover 2027: April 22, 2027 – April 29, 2027, eight days outside Israel and seven in Israel. It begins on the Wednesday evening, April 21, 2027, at sunset. These are the dates outside Israel; the Israel dates are in the table on this page.

When is Passover 2028?

When was Passover 2026?

Passover 2026 was on April 2, 2026 – April 9, 2026, beginning on the Wednesday evening, April 1, 2026.

What time does Passover start?

Like every Jewish holiday, Passover begins at sunset on the evening before its first date, and ends at nightfall. The exact minute depends on your city. For local times see candle lighting times or ask your synagogue.

Why does Passover fall on a different date every year?

Passover always has the same date on the Hebrew calendar, which follows the moon and adds a leap month in some years. On the Gregorian calendar the date moves by up to a few weeks. You can convert any date with the Hebrew date converter.

When is the Seder?

The first Seder is on the Wednesday evening, April 21, 2027. Outside Israel a second Seder is held on the next evening. In Israel there is one Seder.

How long is Passover?

Eight days outside Israel and seven days in Israel. The first two and last two days are Yom Tov outside Israel; in Israel the first and seventh days.
