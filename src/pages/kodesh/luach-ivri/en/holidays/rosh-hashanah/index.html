About Rosh Hashanah

Rosh Hashanah 2027 falls on October 2, 2027 – October 3, 2027 and begins on the Friday evening, October 1, 2027. Last time it was on September 12, 2026 – September 13, 2026. Below is general background about the holiday: what it celebrates, its main customs and why its date changes from year to year. It is a short introduction, not a guide to practice; for questions about how to keep the holiday, ask a rabbi.

What Rosh Hashanah is

Rosh Hashanah, "head of the year", is the Jewish New Year. It falls on the 1st and 2nd of Tishrei and lasts two days everywhere, in Israel and outside it. In the Torah it is called the day of the sounding of the shofar. The prayers speak of God as King and Judge, and the day opens the Ten Days of Repentance, which end on Yom Kippur.

The shofar

The shofar, a ram's horn, is sounded in the synagogue during the morning service on both days of Rosh Hashanah. There are three kinds of sounds: tekiah, a long blast, shevarim, three broken blasts, and teruah, a series of short blasts. When the first day of Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbat, the shofar is not sounded that day, only on the second day.

Rosh Hashanah foods and greetings

At the festive meals many dip apple in honey for a sweet year, eat a round challah, and eat symbolic foods such as pomegranate, dates, leek, beets and a fish or ram's head, each with a short prayer. The common greeting is "Shanah Tovah", a good year. On the first afternoon, or on the second if the first day is Shabbat, many go to a river, sea or spring for Tashlich, a short prayer.

Which days Rosh Hashanah can fall on

By the rules of the fixed Hebrew calendar, Rosh Hashanah never begins on a Sunday, a Wednesday or a Friday. It always falls on a Monday, Tuesday, Thursday or Shabbat. In the Gregorian calendar it falls in September or early October.

The days after Rosh Hashanah

The day after Rosh Hashanah is the Fast of Gedaliah, a fast from dawn to nightfall; when it falls on Shabbat it moves to Sunday. A week later comes Yom Kippur, and five days after that Sukkot. The table "Rosh Hashanah to Yom Kippur" on this page lists all three.

Why Rosh Hashanah begins in the evening

On the Hebrew calendar each day begins at sunset, not at midnight. So Rosh Hashanah begins on the evening before the first date in the tables, and ends at nightfall on its last day. That is why the box at the top of this page shows both the date and the evening it begins. Candle lighting is usually about 18 minutes before sunset, and each city has its own times.

What time does Rosh Hashanah start?

Like every Jewish holiday, Rosh Hashanah begins at sunset on the evening before its first date, and ends at nightfall. The exact minute depends on your city. For local times see candle lighting times or ask your synagogue.

Why does Rosh Hashanah fall on a different date every year?

Rosh Hashanah always has the same date on the Hebrew calendar, which follows the moon and adds a leap month in some years. On the Gregorian calendar the date moves by up to a few weeks. You can convert any date with the Hebrew date converter.

How long is Rosh Hashanah?

Two days, the 1st and 2nd of Tishrei, both in Israel and outside Israel.
