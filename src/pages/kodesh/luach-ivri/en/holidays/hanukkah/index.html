About Hanukkah

Hanukkah 2026 falls on December 5, 2026 – December 12, 2026 and begins on the Friday evening, December 4, 2026. Last time it was on December 15, 2025 – December 22, 2025. Below is general background about the holiday: what it celebrates, its main customs and why its date changes from year to year. It is a short introduction, not a guide to practice; for questions about how to keep the holiday, ask a rabbi.

What Hanukkah celebrates

Hanukkah begins on the 25th of the Hebrew month of Kislev and lasts eight days. It recalls the victory of the Maccabees, a small group of Jews led by the Hasmonean family, over the Seleucid Greek empire in the second century BCE, and the rededication of the Holy Temple in Jerusalem. The Talmud tells that the Maccabees found only one sealed jar of pure oil, enough to light the Temple menorah for a single day, and it burned for eight days. That is why the holiday lasts eight days and why its central practice is lighting lights. The word Hanukkah means "dedication".

How the menorah is lit

A Hanukkah menorah, also called a hanukkiah, has eight branches in a row plus a separate place for the shamash, the helper candle used to light the others. On the first night one candle is lit, on the second night two, and so on until all eight burn on the last night. Candles are placed from right to left, and the newest one is lit first. The blessings are said before lighting: on the first night three blessings, and on the other nights two. The full text in Hebrew, transliteration and English is on the page Hanukkah blessings. Many families place the menorah by a window or the front door, so that people outside can see the lights. Over the eight nights, counting the shamash, 44 candles are used.

Hanukkah on Friday and Saturday night

On Friday the Hanukkah candles are lit before the Shabbat candles, before sunset, and they need enough oil or long enough candles to keep burning until well after dark. On Saturday night they are lit after Shabbat ends. The exact time to light depends on your city and on family custom; for the time Shabbat begins and ends where you live, see candle lighting times, and for questions of practice ask a rabbi.

Food, dreidel and gelt

Because the miracle was about oil, foods fried in oil are traditional: potato latkes in many homes, and sufganiyot, jelly doughnuts, especially in Israel. Children play with a dreidel, a four-sided spinning top. Its letters, nun, gimel, hei and shin, stand for the words "a great miracle happened there"; in Israel the last letter is pe, for "here". Many families give Hanukkah gelt, coins or chocolate coins, and in many homes gifts too.

Is Hanukkah a day off?

Hanukkah is not a Yom Tov. Work, school and driving go on as usual, and the holiday is marked by lighting the menorah each evening, by adding the Al HaNissim prayer and the full Hallel in the daily prayers, and by festive meals. Hanukkah falls in November or December on the Gregorian calendar, which is why its date seems to move every year.

Why Hanukkah begins in the evening

On the Hebrew calendar each day begins at sunset, not at midnight. So Hanukkah begins on the evening before the first date in the tables, and ends at nightfall on its last day. That is why the box at the top of this page shows both the date and the evening it begins. Candle lighting is usually about 18 minutes before sunset, and each city has its own times.

Questions and answers about Hanukkah

When is Hanukkah 2026?

Hanukkah 2026: December 5, 2026 – December 12, 2026, eight days. It begins on the Friday evening, December 4, 2026, at sunset.

When is Hanukkah 2027?

Hanukkah 2027: December 25, 2027 – January 1, 2028, beginning on the Friday evening, December 24, 2027.

When was Hanukkah 2025?

Hanukkah 2025 was on December 15, 2025 – December 22, 2025, beginning on the Sunday evening, December 14, 2025.

What time does Hanukkah start?

Like every Jewish holiday, Hanukkah begins at sunset on the evening before its first date, and ends at nightfall. The exact minute depends on your city. For local times see candle lighting times or ask your synagogue.

Why does Hanukkah fall on a different date every year?

Hanukkah always has the same date on the Hebrew calendar, which follows the moon and adds a leap month in some years. On the Gregorian calendar the date moves by up to a few weeks. You can convert any date with the Hebrew date converter.

How many candles are needed for Hanukkah?

For all eight nights you need 44 candles: 36 for the nights themselves (1+2+...+8) and 8 for the shamash, one each night. Many boxes of Hanukkah candles hold exactly 44.

What blessings are said on the Hanukkah candles?

On the first night three blessings are said before lighting, and on the other nights two. The text in Hebrew, transliteration and English: Hanukkah blessings.
