About Sukkot

Sukkot 2027 falls on October 16, 2027 – October 24, 2027 and begins on the Friday evening, October 15, 2027. Last time it was on September 26, 2026 – October 4, 2026. Below is general background about the holiday: what it celebrates, its main customs and why its date changes from year to year. It is a short introduction, not a guide to practice; for questions about how to keep the holiday, ask a rabbi.

What Sukkot celebrates

Sukkot, the Festival of Booths, begins on the 15th of Tishrei, five days after Yom Kippur. For seven days meals are eaten in a sukkah, a temporary hut, in memory of the clouds that sheltered the Israelites in the desert after the Exodus. It is also the festival of the harvest, and the Torah calls it "the time of our joy".

Building a sukkah

A sukkah has at least three walls and a roof of schach: branches, palm leaves, bamboo or other plant material that grew from the ground, laid loosely so that more shade than sun comes through. Many decorate it with fruit, paper chains and pictures. The sukkah is built under the open sky, not under a tree or a balcony. For questions about the exact rules, ask a rabbi.

The four species

Each day of Sukkot except Shabbat, the four species are held together and waved: the lulav (a closed palm branch), the etrog (a citron), three myrtle branches and two willow branches. They are held during the Hallel prayer, and in the synagogue there is a procession, the hakafot, around the bimah.

Chol HaMoed and Hoshana Rabbah

Outside Israel the first two days are Yom Tov and the next days are Chol HaMoed; in Israel only the first day is Yom Tov. The last day of Chol HaMoed is Hoshana Rabbah, when seven processions are made in the synagogue and a bundle of willows is beaten on the ground.

Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah

Right after the seven days of Sukkot comes Shemini Atzeret. Outside Israel it is followed by Simchat Torah, when the yearly cycle of Torah reading is finished and begun again, with singing and dancing around the Torah scrolls. In Israel both are celebrated on the same day. The table "Sukkot in Israel and outside Israel" on this page shows the dates side by side.

Why Sukkot begins in the evening

On the Hebrew calendar each day begins at sunset, not at midnight. So Sukkot begins on the evening before the first date in the tables, and ends at nightfall on its last day. That is why the box at the top of this page shows both the date and the evening it begins. Candle lighting is usually about 18 minutes before sunset, and each city has its own times.

Questions and answers about Sukkot

When is Sukkot 2027?

Sukkot 2027: October 16, 2027 – October 24, 2027, nine days outside Israel with Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah, eight in Israel. It begins on the Friday evening, October 15, 2027, at sunset. These are the dates outside Israel; the Israel dates are in the table on this page.

When is Sukkot 2028?

Sukkot 2028: October 5, 2028 – October 13, 2028, beginning on the Wednesday evening, October 4, 2028.

When was Sukkot 2026?

Sukkot 2026 was on September 26, 2026 – October 4, 2026, beginning on the Friday evening, September 25, 2026.

What time does Sukkot start?

Like every Jewish holiday, Sukkot begins at sunset on the evening before its first date, and ends at nightfall. The exact minute depends on your city. For local times see candle lighting times or ask your synagogue.

Why does Sukkot fall on a different date every year?

Sukkot always has the same date on the Hebrew calendar, which follows the moon and adds a leap month in some years. On the Gregorian calendar the date moves by up to a few weeks. You can convert any date with the Hebrew date converter.

When is Simchat Torah?

Outside Israel Simchat Torah is on Sunday, October 24, 2027; in Israel it is on Saturday, October 23, 2027, together with Shemini Atzeret.

How long is Sukkot?

Seven days, followed by Shemini Atzeret. Outside Israel the first two days are Yom Tov and Simchat Torah is a separate ninth day; in Israel the first day is Yom Tov and Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah are one day.
