About Purim

Purim 2027 falls on March 23, 2027 and begins on the Monday evening, March 22, 2027. Last time it was on March 3, 2026. Below is general background about the holiday: what it celebrates, its main customs and why its date changes from year to year. It is a short introduction, not a guide to practice; for questions about how to keep the holiday, ask a rabbi.

What Purim celebrates

Purim recalls the events of the Book of Esther, when Haman, a minister of the Persian king Ahasuerus, plotted to destroy the Jews of the empire, and Queen Esther and her cousin Mordechai turned the decree around. The name comes from "pur", the lot Haman cast to choose the day. Purim falls on the 14th of Adar, and in a leap year on the 14th of Adar II.

The four mitzvot of Purim

There are four main practices on Purim: hearing the Megillah, the Book of Esther, read in the evening and again in the morning; sending mishloach manot, gifts of at least two kinds of ready-to-eat food to at least one friend; giving matanot la'evyonim, gifts to at least two poor people; and a festive meal in the afternoon. Many also give a half-coin to charity before Purim, in memory of the half-shekel.

Costumes and hamantaschen

Children and adults dress up in costumes, and synagogues are noisy during the Megillah reading, when Haman's name is drowned out. Hamantaschen, triangle cookies filled with poppy seed, jam or chocolate, are the best-known Purim food.

The Fast of Esther and Shushan Purim

The day before Purim is the Fast of Esther, from dawn to nightfall; when that day is Shabbat, the fast is held on the Thursday before. In cities that had a wall in the days of Joshua, above all Jerusalem, Purim is celebrated a day later, on the 15th of Adar, called Shushan Purim. The table "Fast of Esther, Purim and Shushan Purim" on this page lists all three.

Is Purim a day off?

Purim is a festive day but not a Yom Tov: work and driving are allowed. In Israel many schools close. In a Hebrew leap year there is also a Purim Katan, a "little Purim", on the 14th of Adar I, a month before Purim, with no special practices.

Why Purim begins in the evening

On the Hebrew calendar each day begins at sunset, not at midnight. So Purim begins on the evening before the first date in the tables, and ends at nightfall on its last day. That is why the box at the top of this page shows both the date and the evening it begins. Candle lighting is usually about 18 minutes before sunset, and each city has its own times.

What time does Purim start?

Like every Jewish holiday, Purim begins at sunset on the evening before its first date, and ends at nightfall. The exact minute depends on your city. For local times see candle lighting times or ask your synagogue.

Why does Purim fall on a different date every year?

Purim always has the same date on the Hebrew calendar, which follows the moon and adds a leap month in some years. On the Gregorian calendar the date moves by up to a few weeks. You can convert any date with the Hebrew date converter.

What are the four mitzvot of Purim?

Hearing the Megillah at night and in the morning, sending mishloach manot to a friend, giving gifts to at least two poor people, and a festive meal.

When is Shushan Purim?

Shushan Purim, Purim in Jerusalem, is the day after Purim: Wednesday, March 24, 2027.
