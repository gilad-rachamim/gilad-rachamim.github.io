Before the event

Print one sheet for the person leading, and send the text to the group so that everyone who was given an honor knows ahead of time. Make sure each person knows what to say and when, and prepare a copy of the texts, such as the Hadran or the Kaddish.

A ready order saves embarrassment

At a unit event – a siyum, a Torah scroll dedication or a memorial – everyone wants to honor the right people, and there is no time to hesitate on stage. A neat sheet with names, roles and times gives the leader confidence and keeps anyone important from being forgotten. And certificates of appreciation for those who contributed: a certificate to print (Hebrew).

A siyum masechet in the unit

A siyum celebrates finishing the study of a tractate. Usually one of those who studied reads the closing lines, then the Hadran is said, followed by Kaddish, and then a festive meal. Many units add a word from the rabbi and from the commander, and thanks to everyone who studied. The order on this page has all of these parts, and you can move or delete any of them to match the custom of your unit.

Bringing in a Torah scroll

A Torah scroll dedication is one of the happiest events in a unit. Usually the last letters of the scroll are written first, which is a great honor. Then the scroll is carried under a chuppah with singing and dancing to the synagogue and placed in the ark. Here you can list who writes the letters, who carries the scroll on each part of the way and who holds the chuppah poles, so everyone knows when their turn comes.

A memorial for the fallen

At a memorial, chapters of Tehillim, El Malei Rachamim and Kaddish are usually said, and people speak about the fallen soldier. Agree in advance with the family who will speak. The name goes in the "In memory of" field and appears in the heading of the sheet. Prayer texts are not given on this page; take them from a siddur.

Who receives the honors

Honors usually go to the commander, the unit rabbi, those who studied or donated the scroll, and at a memorial to the family and friends of the fallen. The list on this page is only a suggestion, and you decide whom to honor.
