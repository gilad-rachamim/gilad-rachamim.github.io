How to calculate a yahrzeit date

The yahrzeit, the yearly anniversary of a passing, is set by the Hebrew date and not by the Gregorian one. So every year it falls on a different Gregorian date, sometimes three weeks or more apart. This yahrzeit calculator takes the Hebrew date of the passing and shows when it falls in each of the next 30 years: the Gregorian date, the day of the week, how many days are left, and the evening to light the memorial candle. If you know only the Gregorian date, enter it and the calculator converts it. Mark whether the passing was after sunset, because the Hebrew day begins in the evening, and then the Hebrew date is already that of the next day.

Yahrzeit, azkarah, anniversary: the same day

All three names describe the same day: the day of passing on the Hebrew calendar, returning every year. "Yahrzeit" is a Yiddish word meaning "time of year"; "azkarah" is the common name in Hebrew, and in Sephardic communities some call it "nachalah". On this day the family gathers, says Kaddish and learns Torah in memory of the departed (le'ilui nishmat).

When to light the yahrzeit candle

The Hebrew day begins in the evening. So the memorial candle is lit on the evening before the Gregorian date the calculator shows, before sunset, and it burns until the end of the next day. When the yahrzeit falls on Shabbat, the candle is lit on Friday before the Shabbat candles. The calculator writes the evening next to every year, and for the time of sunset in your city there are candle lighting times and today's halachic times.

What is done on a yahrzeit: the common order

Customs differ between communities, and this is the common order:

  • The evening before: light a memorial candle, and in the synagogue say Kaddish at the evening prayer (Maariv). The words, with transliteration, are on the Mourner's Kaddish page.
  • The Shabbat before: many go up to the Torah, and in many communities El Malei Rachamim or Hashkavah is said for the departed.
  • The day itself: say Kaddish at all three prayers, and some lead the prayers.
  • Visiting the grave: say Psalms; many read from Psalm 119 the verses that begin with the letters of the name of the departed, and then the letters of the word neshamah. To see the letters of a name, use the Hebrew alphabet page.
  • Learning and a meal: learn Mishnah in memory of the soul, share words of Torah and memories, and some hold a meal and say blessings with intent for the soul of the departed.
  • Charity: it is customary to give tzedakah in memory of the departed.

Some have the custom to fast on the yahrzeit of a father or mother, and some do not. For every such question, ask the family's rabbi.

A week before: what to arrange

A good yahrzeit starts a few days before. Tell the family the day and the hour, make sure there will be a minyan for Kaddish at the cemetery, and arrange with the gabbai an aliyah to the Torah on the Shabbat before. Next to each result there is a button that sends the family a WhatsApp reminder with the date.

Yahrzeit in Adar and in a leap year

A leap year has two months of Adar, and the question is when to keep the yahrzeit of someone who passed away in Adar of a regular year. According to the Shulchan Aruch it is kept in Adar II, according to the Rema in Adar I, and some are strict and keep both. Someone who passed away in Adar I or Adar II of a leap year: in a leap year the yahrzeit is in that same Adar. The calculator shows one date by the rules of the calendar and adds a note, and the decision is left to your rabbi.

When the passing was on the 30th of the month

Cheshvan and Kislev have years of thirty days and years of twenty-nine. For someone who passed away on 30 Cheshvan or 30 Kislev, in a year when the month is short there are different customs whether to keep the 29th of the month or the 1st of the following month. When you enter the year of passing too, the calculator follows the common rule and marks that the date moved. Here too, the decision is with the rabbi.

The first yahrzeit: by the passing or by the burial?

In most cases the yahrzeit is set by the day of passing, also in the first year. When the burial was delayed three days or more, some have the custom, in the first year only, to keep it by the day of burial. If the first year is a leap year, it has thirteen months, and saying Kaddish ends before the yahrzeit. The calculator marks the first year with a note.

Why the yahrzeit moves on the Gregorian calendar

Hebrew months follow the moon, so a Hebrew year of twelve months is about 11 days shorter than a solar year. To keep Passover in the spring, seven years out of every nineteen get a thirteenth month, Adar II. The result: the yahrzeit is always on the same Hebrew date, but on the Gregorian calendar it moves back about 11 days in a regular year and forward about 19 days after a leap year. Is the yahrzeit by the Hebrew date or the Gregorian date?

By the Hebrew date. That is why the Gregorian date of the yahrzeit changes from year to year, sometimes by three weeks or more.

What does yahrzeit mean?

Yahrzeit is a Yiddish word meaning "time of year". It is the yearly anniversary of a passing on the Hebrew calendar. In Hebrew it is called azkarah or yom hashanah.

The passing was at night. Which Hebrew date counts?

The Hebrew day begins at sunset. If the passing was after sunset, the Hebrew date is that of the next day. Tick "after sunset" and the calculator moves the date forward.

When do I light the yahrzeit candle?

On the evening before the Gregorian date of the yahrzeit, before sunset, and it burns through the next day. When the yahrzeit falls on Shabbat, the candle is lit on Friday before the Shabbat candles. The calculator shows the evening for every year.

Is the yahrzeit date by the day of death or the day of burial?

In most cases the yahrzeit is set by the day of passing, also in the first year. When the burial was three or more days after the passing, some have the custom to keep the first yahrzeit only by the day of burial. Ask your rabbi.

What prayer do you say for a yahrzeit?

Kaddish is said in the evening, morning and afternoon prayers. On the Shabbat before, many go up to the Torah, and in many communities El Malei Rachamim or Hashkavah is said for the departed.

Which Psalms are said at a yahrzeit?

A common custom is Psalms 33, 16, 17, 72, 91, 104 and 130, and then the verses of Psalm 119 that begin with the letters of the name of the departed and the letters of the word neshamah. Customs differ between communities.

When does saying Kaddish end?

Practice for the year of mourning differs, and many end Kaddish before the first yahrzeit. When the first year is a leap year, it has thirteen months, and Kaddish ends before the yahrzeit. For your family, ask your rabbi.
