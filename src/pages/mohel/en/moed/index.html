How the calculator works

A brit milah is held on the eighth day after birth: one week after the day of birth, on the same weekday. In Judaism the day begins at sunset, so a baby born after sunset counts as born on the next day, and the brit moves one day later. The calculator checks when sunset was on the date of birth at the place you chose in Israel: sunset is early in winter and late in summer, and slightly different in each place. That way it works out by itself whether the birth was before or after sunset, and it finds the Hebrew date with the calendar built into your browser.

A brit on its proper day is held even on Shabbat and on Yom Tov (a festival). A brit that is not on its proper day, for example after a Caesarean birth or after a postponement for jaundice, does not override Shabbat and is moved to a weekday.

The calculator is a helping tool. The final decision on the date is made together, after I see the baby.

If your baby was born outside Israel

The places in the calculator are Israeli hospitals. If your son was born in another country, leave the time field empty and choose yourself whether the birth was before or after sunset by local time. The calculator then works out the date from your choice. Check the sunset time in your city, and any doubtful case, with your own mohel.

Rules that affect the date

The jaundice test

A jaundice test is a must, and the mohel is told the result and the day and time of the test, because it makes a difference to the decision. If the level is borderline or high, you will be asked to do more tests. Things can still change: a brit is held only when the baby is fit for it, so that there is never any risk to life. Sometimes even a healthy baby has his brit later than the proper day, so be sure to talk with the mohel in advance.

When the baby is unwell

A baby who is unwell does not have his brit until he recovers. The brit is postponed, following the mohel's instructions, until the baby is fully ready. When the illness affected the whole body, we wait seven days after recovery. Such a brit is no longer on the eighth day, so it is not held on Shabbat or a festival.

Caesarean birth

A baby born by Caesarean has his brit on the eighth day as usual, but not on Shabbat, Yom Tov or Yom Kippur. If the eighth day falls on one of them, the brit is held on the first following day that is not Shabbat or a festival.

Born close to sunset

The time between sunset and the appearance of the stars is called bein hashmashot (twilight): there is a doubt whether it is still day or already night. A baby born at that time has his brit on the ninth day, which may be the eighth. If that day falls on Shabbat or Yom Tov, the brit is not held on it, because Shabbat is not overridden for a doubt, so sometimes the brit falls on the tenth or eleventh day. There are different opinions about how long bein hashmashot lasts: if the birth was close to sunset, send me the exact time and we will check it together with a rabbi.

Fast days, Chanukah and Purim

On Yom Kippur, Tisha B'Av and the other fast days, a brit on its proper day is held as usual, but the seudat mitzvah (the festive meal) is not held on the fast day. Some hold the brit closer to sunset and the meal in the evening, after the fast. A brit on Chanukah or Purim is a double joy. The rules for each special day, with sources, are gathered in the special brit calendar.

What time of day

The whole day is fit for a brit, from sunrise to sunset, and by the principle of זריזין מקדימין למצוות (zerizin makdimin lemitzvot), earlier is better. Many families hold the brit at midday, and that is perfectly fine. The main thing is not to set it too close to sunset and to leave a margin, because small delays always happen. Frequently asked questions

On which day is the brit milah held?

On the eighth day after birth. If the baby was born before sunset, the day of birth is day one. If after sunset, counting starts from the next day. For example, a baby born on Monday before sunset has his brit the following Monday, if he is healthy and fit for the brit.

Our baby was born on Friday evening, after sunset. When is the brit?

A birth after sunset on Friday counts as a birth on Shabbat, so the brit is on the following Shabbat, if the baby is healthy. If the birth was during bein hashmashot or by Caesarean, the brit is not held on Shabbat and is set for Sunday.

Is a brit held on Shabbat?

Yes, when Shabbat is the eighth day. A postponed brit, the brit of a baby born by Caesarean, and of a baby born during bein hashmashot are not held on Shabbat.

Our baby has jaundice. Is the brit postponed?

It depends on the jaundice level and how it is changing. Send me the test results with the day and time the test was taken. If needed, the brit is postponed, following the mohel's instructions, until the baby is fully ready. The baby's health comes first.

What time of day is the brit held?

Any time of day, from sunrise to sunset. Many hold it at midday. Just do not set it too close to sunset, and leave a margin.
