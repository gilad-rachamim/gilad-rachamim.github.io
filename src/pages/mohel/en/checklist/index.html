How to use this checklist

This is the list Gilad Rachamim, a certified mohel, gives parents in his guide. It is free, with no sign-up and no ads. Tick an item and the bar at the top shows how much is ready. Your ticks are kept only in your phone's memory: nothing is sent to a server, so the list opens with the same ticks tomorrow or next week. The Share button sends the page link to your spouse, and Reset clears all the ticks.

The list has six groups: pharmacy, hygiene, clothing, sleep and travel, getting the ceremony ready, and what to bring on the day itself. The first four are shopping you can comfortably do while still pregnant. The last two are about organizing the brit milah and packing on the morning of the big day.

Why shop before the birth

Toward the end of pregnancy you never know exactly when the birth will come. That is why it pays to shop early: nothing here spoils, and after the birth you are always busy – within eight days you are hosting a celebration. You don't have to buy everything, though. Some things will probably arrive as gifts. Toys can wait, and most clothing beyond the first days can be bought after the brit, once you see what you were given.

Pharmacy items come first

The pharmacy group is needed right after the brit, and nobody wants to run to a pharmacy with a newborn at home. The product names on the list are the ones used in Israel. If you live outside Israel, ask your own mohel what to use instead, and always follow his instructions. How to use each item after the brit is explained step by step in bris aftercare, day by day.

A two-piece outfit

For the brit, the baby's outfit should be pants or leggings with a separate top. During the brit only the bottom comes off and the top stays on, so the baby is not fully undressed and doesn't get cold. Plan festive clothes for the whole family too: a brit is a joyful event, not only for the baby but for you, the parents who have just become a family.

Car seat and stroller

An infant car seat is a must, because you cannot leave the hospital safely without one. It is handy if it clicks onto the stroller wheels, so a sleeping baby doesn't need to be moved. For safety, a baby shouldn't sleep in the stroller for long stretches, so you will want a crib. The stroller can wait if someone close plans to buy it for you; make sure the handle suits your height.

Getting the ceremony ready

A brit milah takes place in daylight: in the morning or around midday, with a safety margin before sunset. The venue can be anything – a synagogue, a hall, a restaurant or your home. What matters most is that it is comfortable for the mother, who has just given birth. If the hall has no Chair of Elijah, borrow one from a nearby synagogue in advance. More in the chapter preparing the bris ceremony.

Honors without hurt feelings

The sandek holds the baby during the brit. It is the greatest honor, usually given to a grandfather or a rabbi. The kvatter carries the baby into the room, and this honor can go to several people. Another close person says the blessings and announces the name, or you can ask the mohel to do it. Check in the family beforehand who is customarily given which honor, so that nobody is hurt.

The date and jaundice

A brit milah takes place on the eighth day, if the baby is healthy. A jaundice test is a must: tell the mohel the result and the day and time of the test. The brit milah date calculator works out the date, and all the rules are in the chapter the brit date and the jaundice test.

The morning of the big day

The most important thing on the day is to arrive in a good mood and leave the tiredness behind. Feed the baby until about an hour before the brit; in the last hour he is not fed, and until then let him eat as much as he wants. Pack the diaper bag with cloth diapers, diapers, wipes, a change of clothes just in case, formula if he isn't breastfed, a pacifier and diaper cream. What else to bring and how to get the baby ready is in the chapter the day of the brit.

Frequently asked questions

Is the checklist really free?

Yes. It is free, with no sign-up and no ads. Your ticks are stored only on your phone.

What should I buy at the pharmacy for a brit milah?

Esracaine 5%, Novimol, natural oil (almond or paraffin), Bepanthen Plus or mupirocin, and alcohol wipes for the umbilical stump. Buy them all before the birth so you don't need to run to a pharmacy after the brit. Outside Israel, ask your mohel what to use instead.

When is the best time to shop?

While you are still pregnant. Nothing spoils, and after the birth you are always busy: within eight days you host a celebration.

What can wait until after the birth?

Toys and most clothing beyond the first days. At the brit you will see what you were given, and then fill in what is missing.

What do we bring to a brit milah?

A brit pillow, a kiddush cup and dry red wine, two tallitot (for the father and the sandek), fragrant spices (myrtle is customary), a siddur for the blessings, and a diaper bag. The mohel may bring some of these, so ask in advance.

When do we feed the baby before the brit?

Until about an hour before the brit. In the last hour he is not fed, and until then let him eat as much as he can.

What does the baby wear to the brit?

A two-piece outfit: a vest or top, and pants or leggings that can come off. That way the area and the legs are uncovered, while the baby is not fully undressed and doesn't get cold.
