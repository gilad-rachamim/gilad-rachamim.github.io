The night before the brit

Brit Yitzchak – the Evening Before the Brit Milah

One night before the brit, the house fills with people, Torah study and song. For me it is one of the most moving moments of the whole week.

עבריתEnglishРусский

What Brit Yitzchak is

On the evening before the brit milah, many families hold a real gathering: family and friends come together for Torah study, reading from the Zohar, singing, piyutim (traditional Jewish hymns) and refreshments – and they pray for the baby's wellbeing. The evening is also called "the night of watching" (leil shimurim), and in the Moroccan custom "tachdid".

The names differ, the idea is the same: before the big day, you pause for a moment, learn, sing, and ask that everything goes well. Every community has its own order, and you follow your family's custom.

If this is the first time you are hearing about it, that is completely fine. Many families, especially those living abroad or new to Israel, first learn about Brit Yitzchak during the week of the brit. Below is everything you need to hold the evening calmly and without fuss.

Where it falls in the week

  • Days 5–7: the last check before the brit. You send the mohel a photo – he looks carefully, makes sure everything is ready and answers every question. On the evening before the brit – "Brit Yitzchak".
  • The eighth day: the brit milah. In the daytime, before sunset. Feed the baby up to about an hour before, a two-piece outfit, a diaper bag.

The whole first week, day by day

To see how Brit Yitzchak fits in with the other customs, here is a short plan of the week between the birth and the brit:

  • Day of birth: mazal tov! Write down the exact time of birth – it sets the day of the brit. Let the mohel know.
  • Days 2–3: jaundice test and discharge. Send the mohel the discharge letter – it also has the jaundice results. Book the hall, catering and guests.
  • First Friday night: "Shalom Zachar" (by custom).
  • The evening before the brit: Brit Yitzchak – study, Psalms and song around the baby.
  • The eighth day: the brit milah.

What to prepare

Brit Yitzchak does not need a production. From the homes I come to, this is what you really need:

  • A place. Your home, your parents' home, or the synagogue. The main thing is that the mother is comfortable – she has just given birth.
  • Light refreshments. Cakes, fruit, drinks. Not a meal.
  • Study. Who leads and what is read – by your family's custom. If you have no one, you can ask me.
  • Time. In the evening, usually after the evening prayer (Maariv). Not too late – tomorrow is a big day.

A small tip from experience: get tomorrow's things ready before the guests arrive. When the brit bag is already by the door, the evening is much calmer. What goes in that bag is covered in "The day of the brit" and in the preparation checklist.

Who comes

Close family, friends, neighbors, people from the synagogue. It does not have to be a big event. Even ten people around a table, with an open book and one song – that is Brit Yitzchak.

You don't have to send invitations, but if you want to tell guests about both the brit and the evening before, there are ready-made invitation texts and a designed WhatsApp invitation – free, no sign-up.

What I bring to the evening

A short talk (drasha), and if you like, songs and piyutim in your custom. As at the brit itself, I love to bring the guests in with joyful words and songs. And in between, it is the time for last-minute questions: what to bring tomorrow, when to feed, how to dress the baby.

A mohel's tip

I'd be glad to come to Brit Yitzchak, host the evening and give a talk – arrange it with me in advance. It is also a good chance for me to see the baby the evening before, and for you to ask me anything that still worries you before the brit.

Brit Yitzchak and the other customs of the week

Shalom Zachar

In many communities, a Shalom Zachar is held on the first Friday night after the birth. After the Shabbat meal, anyone who wants comes to the house for light refreshments, to sing and to bless. It is a different evening: Shalom Zachar is on Friday night, Brit Yitzchak is the night before the brit.

The kvatter and the honors

Tomorrow at the brit, the kvatter carries the baby in and passes him from hand to hand, and the sandek holds him during the brit. The evening before is a good time to check once more with the family who gets which honor, so that no one close to you is hurt. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Brit Yitzchak, leil shimurim and tachdid?

They are different names for the same evening: "leil shimurim" (the night of watching), and in the Moroccan custom "tachdid". In all of them, family and friends gather for Torah study, reading the Zohar, singing, piyutim and refreshments, and pray for the baby's wellbeing.

When does it start?

On the evening before the brit, usually after the evening prayer. The exact time follows your family's custom and what is comfortable for the mother.

Does the mohel come to Brit Yitzchak?

I'd be glad to come to Brit Yitzchak, host the evening and give a talk – arrange it with me in advance.

Where should we hold it?

At home, at your parents' or in the synagogue. The main thing is that the mother, just after the birth, is comfortable.

Do we need to serve a meal?

No. Light refreshments are enough: cakes, fruit, drinks. The festive meal (seudat mitzvah) comes the next day, after the brit.

Whom do we invite?

Close family, friends, neighbors, people from the synagogue. It doesn't have to be a big event – ten people around a table with an open book and one song is already Brit Yitzchak.

How do we know which evening it is?

First find the day of the brit: the brit milah date calculator works it out for free, no sign-up. The evening before that day is Brit Yitzchak.

How is Brit Yitzchak different from Shalom Zachar?

Many communities hold Shalom Zachar on the first Friday night after the birth, while Brit Yitzchak is the evening before the brit. They are two different customs of the same week.
