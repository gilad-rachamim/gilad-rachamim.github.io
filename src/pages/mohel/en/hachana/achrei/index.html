I am Gilad Rachamim, a certified mohel. Below is bris aftercare the way I explain it to parents: day by day, in plain words, with what is normal and when to call right away. All of it is also in the full guide for parents, free.

On the day of the bris

  • After every diaper change, drip natural oil onto the bandage.
  • Check that the baby is passing urine (look in the diaper).
  • About eight hours after the bris you may wash the baby – only around the area, without wetting the bandage. The first shower or full bath comes only after the bandage is removed the next day.
  • When you close the diaper, point the penis downward.
  • Gently wake a sleepy baby to feed, roughly every three or four hours.
  • A little blood in the diaper is no cause for worry. Only if there is a lot of blood – contact the mohel for instructions.
  • For pain relief you may give Novimol syrup (an Israeli brand) every five or six hours.
  • The mohel checked the baby after the bris and found that everything is fine – there is no reason to worry.

Novimol, the oil and Esracaine are bought before the birth, so you will not need to run to a pharmacy after the bris. Everything is on the shopping list, in the pharmacy section.

The day after the bris

  • Until the mohel removes the bandage, keep washing the baby only around the area, without wetting the bandage. The first full shower – right after the bandage comes off.
  • Keep dripping natural oil onto the bandage after every diaper change: almond or paraffin oil (if you have neither – a good olive oil).
  • Why does it matter? A bandage that has dried and stuck to the wound hurts when it is removed. A soft, oiled bandage comes off easily – and spares the baby needless discomfort.

The mohel comes the day after the bris to remove the bandage. Once it is off, a whitish-yellow discharge appears around the cut – this is natural and part of the healing process.

After the mohel removes the bandage

  • Treat the baby as usual.
  • Apply Bepanthen Plus or mupirocin around the area – twice a day, morning and night, for three days. Do not overdo it – too much ointment is not healthy; if it seems more is needed, only in coordination with and on the advice of the mohel.
  • Bathe the baby twice a day for five days.
"When Mom is calm, the baby is calm too"

The weeks after: full healing

  • Full healing takes about three weeks, and during these weeks you will see changes in color and appearance. This is part of the process.
  • The yellowish discharge starts around the second day and fades over about two weeks.
  • Slight swelling under the head of the penis can stay for up to a month, and it goes away on its own.
  • A soaked bandage darkens and falls off by itself in pieces. Do not pull it.
  • Touch the area only with clean hands.

From one month to one year: keep the groove dry

A small thing that matters

Once a week, after the bath, gently pull the skin below the head of the penis downward and dry the groove that forms there well, especially in a chubby baby. This groove should stay clean and dry.

If after a few months the skin sticks and does not move easily, do not try to pull it by force. Write to me and I will explain what to do.

The healing days: what is normal

  • The baby may be restless in the first hours – swaddling, holding him close and nursing calm him.
  • A few spots of blood in the diaper – no cause for worry.
  • A whitish-yellow discharge around the cut after the bandage is removed – natural and part of the healing process.

When to call right away

  • Bleeding that does not stop, or a blood stain that keeps growing.
  • The baby has not passed urine for many hours after the bris (six to eight hours with no urine).
  • The bandage fell off.
  • Fever, swelling or redness that spreads.
  • Anything that worries you – no question is unnecessary.
A mohel's tip

Not sure whether something is okay? Take a photo and send it to me on WhatsApp – I will look and get back to you. With any question or doubt you can write to me and find out what to do – at any hour, until full healing. A question after the bris? Message me on WhatsApp.

The instructions on this page come from my guide and my experience as a mohel, and are meant for the families I accompany. Bris aftercare: common questions

When is the bandage removed after the bris?

The mohel comes the day after the bris to remove the bandage. Until then, drip natural oil onto the bandage after every diaper change so it stays soft and comes off easily, and wash the baby only around the area without wetting it. The first full shower – after the bandage comes off.

There is blood in the diaper. Is that normal?

A few spots of blood in the diaper are no cause for worry. Bleeding that does not stop, or a blood stain that keeps growing – call right away.

What is the white discharge around the cut?

After the bandage comes off, a whitish-yellow discharge appears around the cut – this is natural and part of the healing process. It starts around the second day and fades over about two weeks.

How long does healing take after a bris?

Full healing takes about three weeks. During those weeks the color and appearance change, and slight swelling under the head of the penis can remain for up to a month and goes away on its own.

What ointment is used after the bandage comes off?

Bepanthen Plus or mupirocin around the area, twice a day, morning and night, for three days. No more than that: if it seems more ointment is needed – only in coordination with the mohel.

When can I bathe my baby after the bris?

About eight hours after the bris you may wash the baby only around the area, without wetting the bandage. The first full shower is after the bandage is removed the next day, and then a bath twice a day for five days.

How soon after the bris should the baby pass urine?

Usually at least once within six to eight hours. If more time has passed and there is no urine in the diaper, write to me or call me.

How often should I feed the baby after the bris?

As usual. A baby who is sleepy after the bris may skip a feed, so gently wake him to eat roughly every three or four hours.
