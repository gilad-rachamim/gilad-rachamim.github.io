And most important of all: come in a good mood, and leave all the tiredness behind.

What you need for the bris itself

  • A bris pillow. The baby lies on it throughout the ceremony. You can also borrow one from various gemachs (community lending funds) for bris supplies
  • A kiddush cup and wine. Make sure you have dry red wine and a kiddush cup, over which the person reciting the blessings will say them
  • 2 tallitot. One for the father and one for the sandek
  • Besamim (fragrant spices). The custom of Sephardic and Mizrahi communities is to bring besamim to smell during the bris. Myrtle (hadassim) is preferred
  • A siddur for the order of blessings, unless the mohel brings one for you
  • Things for the baby. See the organized list of what to bring for the baby, below
Bonus

You can bring sheets with a special prayer (segulah) for the moment the baby cries and hand them to guests as a keepsake of the bris – it makes everyone a participant. The prayer is in the songbook in the bris printables.

Getting the little prince ready for the bris

The baby's clothes

During the bris, the area and the legs need to be uncovered, so dress the baby in a two-piece outfit: the leggings or pants can come off while he keeps an undershirt or top on. That way the baby is not fully exposed and does not get cold.

Feeding the baby

Feed the baby until about an hour before the bris. In the last hour before it he is no longer fed, and until then let him eat as much as he likes, happily.

The diaper bag

I recommend packing: cloth diapers, disposable diapers, wipes, a change of clothes in case of a leak, formula if he is not breastfed, a pacifier and diaper cream.

One more thing worth packing: sugar water and a clean pacifier. A pacifier dipped in sugar water helps calm the baby right after the ceremony. More on the page about pain and soothing at the bris.

Time and place of the bris ceremony

A bris is held during daylight hours, while the sun is still up: in the morning or at midday, whatever suits you and your guests. A bris is not held in the evening, and you leave a safety margin before sunset, because there are always small delays. A bris can be at home, in a synagogue, in a hall or in a restaurant. What matters most is that it is comfortable for the mother, who just gave birth.

Check ahead whether the place has a Chair of Elijah or whether you need to bring one from a nearby synagogue. I bring a speaker and microphone for the songs and blessings. How the bris ceremony unfolds

The baby's entrance and the Chair of Elijah

The kvatter carries the baby into the room, and the guests rise and greet him with the traditional "Baruch haba". You can honor several kvatters, who pass the baby from hand to hand. By tradition, Elijah the Prophet is present at every bris, and the baby is placed for a moment on a chair dedicated to him.

The sandek and the circumcision

The baby is handed to the sandek, who sits wrapped in a tallit and holds him on his knees during the bris. This is the greatest honor. The mohel says the blessing and performs the circumcision, and the father says his own blessing – on bringing his son into the covenant of Abraham our forefather.

The blessings over the cup and the naming

Blessings are said over a cup of wine, and in the prayer that follows, the baby's name is heard for the first time. It is one of the most moving moments. Then comes "Mazal tov", songs and piyyutim according to your custom, and the festive meal (seudat mitzvah).

Who receives which honor – sandek, kvatter, the person who reads the blessings and the name – is decided ahead of time within the family. If you prefer, the mohel can recite the blessings in a traditional melody and announce the baby's name. You can prepare an honors card for free with the bris honors tool, and read more about the sandek.

After the ceremony

After the blessings the baby goes back into his mother's arms. On the day of the bris you drip natural oil onto the bandage after every diaper change, and the next day the mohel removes the bandage. Bris ceremony day: common questions

What should I bring to a bris?

A bris pillow, a kiddush cup and dry red wine, two tallitot (for the father and the sandek), besamim (myrtle is customary), a siddur for the blessings, and a diaper bag for the baby. Some of these you can check with the mohel – he may bring them.

When should I feed the baby before the bris?

Until about an hour before the bris. In the last hour he is no longer fed, and until then let him eat as much as he can.

What does the baby wear at the bris?

A two-piece outfit: an undershirt or top, and pants or leggings that can come off. That way the area and the legs are uncovered, while the baby is not fully exposed and does not get cold.

What goes in the diaper bag?

Cloth diapers, disposable diapers, wipes, a change of clothes, formula (if he is not breastfed), a pacifier and diaper cream. Plus sugar water and a clean pacifier to soothe him after the ceremony.

What time of day is best for a bris?

Morning or midday, whatever suits you and your guests. Just not too close to sunset: leave a safety margin before sunset, because there are always small delays.

How long does a bris ceremony take?

The ceremony itself is short. The circumcision lasts just moments, followed by the blessings, the name and the singing. Most of the time you will spend at the meal, celebrating with your family.

What is the Chair of Elijah?

By tradition, Elijah the Prophet is present at every bris and watches over the baby. So a special chair is prepared for him, and the baby is placed on it for a moment before the bris. Many synagogues have one; at a hall you need to check in advance.

When is the baby's name first heard?

In the blessings after the bris. Until then it is customary not to reveal the name – not even to the mohel.
