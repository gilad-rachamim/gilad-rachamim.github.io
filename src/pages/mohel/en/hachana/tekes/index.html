Planning the Bris Ceremony

A brit has to be held during daylight hours, while the sun is still up. Everything else you can plan calmly.

Choosing a venue

  • Time of the brit: morning or midday. A brit isn't held in the evening, and you leave a safety margin of time before sunset.
  • Chair of Elijah: find out whether the venue has one, or whether you need to bring one from a nearby synagogue – and if so, arrange it ahead of time.
  • Location: convenient for the guests and, most importantly, convenient for you, the parents. It's your event, and the mother has just given birth and needs to be the most comfortable of all.
  • Style of venue: think about which style suits you: a synagogue, a hall, a restaurant, or your own home.
  • Catering: included with the venue, or brought in from outside.
  • How many guests. I bring a speaker and microphone for the piyutim and blessings. For music at the meal you can also have a DJ.
A mohel's tip

A brit can be at home, in a synagogue, in a hall or in a restaurant, and it's moving wherever it is. What sets the atmosphere isn't the place, but who stands around the baby and what is sung. Songs for the brit by tradition.

Planning the ceremony: the honors

It's customary for one close person to be the sandek, and another close person to read the brit blessings and the newborn's name. If you like, you can also ask the mohel to chant the blessings in the traditional melody and say the baby's name.

Sandek

The person who sits on the Chair of Elijah and holds the baby during the brit. The greatest honor, usually for a grandfather or a rabbi.

Chair of Elijah

According to tradition, Elijah the Prophet is present at every brit, and the baby is placed for a moment on a chair dedicated to him.

Kvatter

Carries the baby into the brit room and passes him from hand to hand. You can honor several. In many families this is the honor for a couple waiting to have children.

The blessings and the naming

After the circumcision, a blessing is said over a cup of wine and the baby's name is announced for the first time.

An important note

Be careful not to hurt any relative who may be expecting one of the honors. So find out ahead of time in the family who traditionally gets to be sandek for the first son, and so on. That way everyone will be happy.

What time of day to hold the bris ceremony

The whole day is valid for a brit, from sunrise to sunset, and by the principle of זריזין מקדימין למצוות (zerizin makdimin lemitzvot), earlier is better. Many families hold the brit at midday, and that's perfectly fine. What matters is not to set it too close to sunset and to leave a safety margin, because there are always small delays.

The day itself is the eighth day after birth. If the baby was born before sunset, the day of birth counts as day one; if after sunset, you start counting from the next day. You can work out the date with the brit milah date calculator, and all the rules are in the chapter “The brit date”.

A bris on Shabbat or a festival

A brit on time, on the eighth day, is held on Shabbat and on Yom Tov too. I perform brit ceremonies on Shabbat and festivals as well, and we coordinate the place and time in advance so it's convenient for you and the family. A brit that was postponed, and isn't on the eighth day, is not held on Shabbat or Yom Tov; it's set for a weekday. A baby born by C-section has his brit on the eighth day as usual, but not on Shabbat, Yom Tov or Yom Kippur.

On Yom Kippur, Tisha B'Av and the other fast days, a brit on time is held as usual, and the festive meal isn't held on the fast day. A brit on Chanukah or Purim is a double celebration. The special days in detail: calendar of special brit dates.

What to bring to the ceremony

  • A brit pillow. The baby lies on it throughout the brit. You can also borrow one from a gemach (free lending service) of brit equipment.
  • A kiddush cup and wine. Dry red wine, and a cup for whoever says the blessings.
  • Two tallitot. One for the father and one for the sandek.
  • Spices. Sephardic and Mizrahi families customarily bring fragrant spices to smell during the brit, preferably myrtle (hadas).
  • A siddur with the order of the blessings, unless the mohel brings one.
  • A bag for the baby: cloth diapers, disposable diapers, wipes, a change of clothes, a pacifier and diaper cream.

Frequently asked questions

Where is a bris held: at home, in a synagogue or in a hall?

In any of them. A synagogue near home is convenient and moving, a hall suits a lot of guests and a meal, and you can have a beautiful brit at home too. What matters: that it's comfortable for the mother, who has just given birth, and that there's a Chair of Elijah or you bring one.

What time is best for a bris?

Morning or midday, whatever suits you and the guests. Just not too close to sunset: leave a safety margin before sunset, because there are always small delays.

What is the Chair of Elijah?

According to tradition, Elijah the Prophet is present at every brit and watches over the baby. So a special chair is prepared for him, and the baby is placed on it for a moment before the brit. Many synagogues have such a chair; at a hall, check in advance.

Do we need a sound system at the bris?

It adds a lot: everyone hears the blessings, the name and the piyutim. I bring a speaker and microphone to every brit, so there's no need to worry about it.

How long does a bris ceremony take?

The ceremony itself is short. The circumcision takes a few moments, followed by the blessings, the name and the singing. Most of the time you'll spend at the meal, celebrating with the family.

Is a bris held on Shabbat?

Yes, when Shabbat is the eighth day. A postponed brit, the brit of a baby born by C-section, and the brit of a baby born during bein hashmashot (twilight) are not held on Shabbat.

What do we bring to a bris?

A brit pillow, a kiddush cup and dry red wine, two tallitot (for the father and the sandek), spices (customarily myrtle), a siddur for the blessings, and a diaper bag for the baby. You can check with the mohel – he may bring some of these himself.

When do we feed the baby before the bris?

Until about an hour before the brit. In the last hour you don't feed him, and until then let him eat as much as he likes.
