Chapter 5

Brit Milah: How to Choose a Mohel

The four things that matter when you choose a mohel, and then four questions worth asking – me included.

עבריתEnglishРусский

The baby's health

It is important to find a mohel who is skilled in his work, who studied brit milah in an orderly way, and who can give precise instructions for caring for the baby after the brit, so the baby stays calm and comfortable.

A mohel who is available to you

A mohel who goes with you from the first conversation – on WhatsApp for any question, with the shopping, following the jaundice and doing the check before the brit – and who comes to your home after the brit to take off the bandage gently and with the care it needs, so the baby hurts as little as possible and you are completely at ease.

The atmosphere at the brit

At a brit milah ceremony the mohel matters a great deal, far beyond the circumcision itself. The mohel can add so much to the atmosphere by bringing the guests in with joyful words and fitting songs.

Your peace of mind as parents

It is important to choose a mohel who will stay with you, the parents, all the way – from your son's birth to the baby's quick and full recovery – so you can go through these days calmly and happily.

Four questions to ask any mohel

  1. Where did you study, and who certified you?A good answer is the name of an institution and a teacher, not "years of experience".
  2. How do you get to know us and the baby before the brit?Support before the brit is the difference between a mohel who shows up to an event and a mohel who stays with a family.
  3. What happens after the brit, and how do we reach you?Who takes off the bandage, and what you do if a question comes up at night.
  4. What does the brit itself look like with you?Songs, piyutim, an explanation for the guests. A good brit is also a beautiful moment, not just a procedure.

When to contact a mohel

Most comfortable is already during the pregnancy. That way there is time for a relaxed introductory call, to ask anything that worries you, and to get the shopping list ready on time. But contacting a mohel a day or two after the birth is usually enough too: the brit is on the eighth day, and a good mohel will go with you from the moment you write to him, including following the jaundice and the check before the brit.

How it started

I began learning the work of a mohel before the brit of my firstborn son: I wanted to do his brit myself, so I learned the craft from the ground up. What started as a father's love became a mission – today I accompany other families through this moment. I know from the inside what a mom and dad go through in these eight days, because I stood there myself, twice.

What the support looks like

That is why my support does not start on the day of the brit and does not end when the guests go home. Frequently asked questions

What is a certified mohel?

A mohel who studied the laws of brit milah and its medical side in an orderly program, went through practical training and an exam, and received a certificate. I am a certified mohel from Machon Aliyah, and a member of the national professional mohels' forum.

How much does a mohel cost?

I don't name a sum. For me a brit milah is a mission and a mitzvah. You give what seems right to you, for the effort, the responsibility and the travel, including the visit after the brit. The introductory call is free and with no obligation.

How far ahead should we contact a mohel?

Most comfortable is during the pregnancy, or right after the birth. The introductory call is short and free – just write to me on WhatsApp.

Does the mohel come after the brit too?

With me, yes. The day after the brit I come to your home to take off the bandage gently and check that everything is fine. And until full healing I am available on WhatsApp for any question.

Do you come outside Jerusalem?

Yes. I live in Jerusalem, work in Jerusalem and the surrounding area, and come to families anywhere in the country when needed.

Do you do a brit on Shabbat and festivals?

Yes. A brit on time, on the eighth day, is held on Shabbat and festivals too, and I do those as well. We coordinate the place and time in advance.

Can we ask for songs in our own custom?

Yes. I accompany the brit with songs and piyutim in your family's custom. If you like, I can also read the blessings in the traditional tune and say the baby's name.

What matters most when choosing a mohel?

The baby's health, the mohel's availability before and after the brit, the atmosphere at the ceremony, and your peace of mind. It is worth asking where he studied, how he gets to know the family beforehand, and what happens after the brit. Everything on this site is free, with no sign-up.
