When Is the Brit Milah? Timing the Eighth Day

According to Jewish law, the brit milah is held on the eighth day after a son is born – and that is also the most recommended time health-wise.

עבריתEnglishРусский

The day of the brit

If the baby was born before sunset, the day of birth counts as day one. If he was born after sunset, the first day of the count is the next day. All this assumes the newborn is in a condition fit for the brit.

The easy way to remember it: the brit is one week after the day of birth, on the same day of the week. A boy born on a Monday before sunset has his brit the following Monday. In Judaism the day begins at sunset, so a baby born after sunset counts as born on the next day, and the brit moves one day later.

The jaundice test

The baby needs a jaundice test, and you should tell the mohel the result along with the day and time the test was taken, because it matters. If the jaundice is borderline or high, you will be asked to do more tests. Everything can still change, and the baby is circumcised only when he is fit for the brit, so that, heaven forbid, there is never any danger to life.

Of course the mohel goes with you through setting the date and makes sure your son is healthy enough for a brit on time. There are cases where even a healthy baby's brit is not on time, so you must talk to the mohel beforehand.

The test is usually done in the hospital or at Tipat Chalav (the well-baby clinic). Keep the result with the day and time. The hospital discharge letter you get on days 2–3 also includes the jaundice results – send it to the mohel.

A baby who is not well

A baby who is not well is not circumcised until he recovers. The brit is postponed according to the mohel's guidance, until the baby is completely ready. With an illness of the whole body, you wait seven days from recovery. Such a brit is no longer on the eighth day, so it is not held on Shabbat or a festival.

A brit on Shabbat and Yom Tov

A brit on time, on the eighth day, is held on Shabbat and on Yom Tov (a festival) too. I do brit ceremonies on Shabbat and festivals as well, and we coordinate the place and time in advance so it is comfortable for you and your family.

A brit that was postponed, and is not on the eighth day, is not held on Shabbat or Yom Tov; it is set for a weekday. In any such case we will talk and coordinate.

Born by C-section

A baby born by C-section has his brit on the eighth day as usual, but not on Shabbat, not on Yom Tov and not on Yom Kippur. If the eighth day falls on one of them, the brit is held on the first day after it that is not Shabbat or a festival.

Born close to sunset

The time between sunset and nightfall (tzet hakochavim) is called "bein hashmashot" (twilight). It is a doubt – maybe day, maybe night. A baby born at that time has his brit on the ninth day, which may be the eighth. If that day falls on Shabbat or Yom Tov, the brit is not held on it, because Shabbat is not set aside for a doubt, and so the brit sometimes falls on the tenth or eleventh day.

There are different opinions on how long bein hashmashot lasts. If the birth was close to sunset, send me the exact time, and we will check it together with a rabbi.

Sunset is earlier in winter and later in summer, and slightly different in every place. That is why the calculator asks for the date, the hospital and the time of birth, and works out by itself whether the birth was before or after sunset.

A brit on a fast day, on Chanukah and on Purim

On Yom Kippur, Tisha B'Av and the other fast days, a brit on time is held as usual, and the festive meal (seudat mitzvah) is not held on the fast day. Some hold the brit relatively close to sunset and the meal in the evening, after the fast. A brit on Chanukah or Purim is a double joy.

What time of day

The whole day is fit for a brit, from sunrise to sunset, and by the principle of זריזין מקדימין למצוות (zerizin makdimin lemitzvot), earlier is better. Many families hold the brit at midday, and that is perfectly fine. What matters: don't set it too close to sunset, and leave a safety margin of time before sunset, because there are always small delays.

Frequently asked questions

On which day is the brit milah held?

On the eighth day after birth. If the baby was born before sunset, the day of birth is day one. If he was born after sunset, you start counting from the next day. For example, a baby born on Monday before sunset has his brit the following Monday, if he is healthy and fit for the brit.

The baby was born Friday evening, after sunset. When is the brit?

A birth after sunset on Friday counts as a birth on Shabbat, so the brit is on the next Shabbat, if the baby is healthy. If the birth was at bein hashmashot or by C-section, the brit is not held on Shabbat and is set for Sunday.

Is a brit held on Shabbat?

Yes, when Shabbat is the eighth day. A postponed brit, the brit of a baby born by C-section, and the brit of a baby born at bein hashmashot are not held on Shabbat.

The baby has jaundice. Is the brit postponed?

It depends on the jaundice level and its trend. Send me the test results, with the day and time it was taken. If needed, the brit is postponed according to the mohel's guidance, until the baby is completely ready. The baby's health comes first.

What time of day is the brit?

Any time during the day, from sunrise to sunset. Many hold it at midday. Just don't set it too close to sunset, and leave a safety margin.

The baby was born by C-section. When is the brit?

On the eighth day as usual, but not on Shabbat, Yom Tov or Yom Kippur. If the eighth day falls on one of them, the brit is on the first day after it that is not Shabbat or a festival.

What is bein hashmashot and why does it matter?

It is the time between sunset and nightfall, when it is unclear whether it is day or night. A baby born then has his brit on the ninth day. If the birth was close to sunset, send me the exact time and we will check it together with a rabbi.

Can we hold the festive meal on a fast day?

A brit on time on a fast day is held as usual, but the meal is not held on the fast day. Some hold the brit closer to sunset and the meal in the evening, after the fast.
