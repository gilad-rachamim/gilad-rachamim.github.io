Who does what at the brit

Bris Ceremony Honors: Sandek, Kvatter and Everyone Else

A brit has several honors, and each one is a great privilege for the person who receives it. Here you'll find what each person does, and how to share the honors so that everyone is happy.

עבריתEnglishРусский

The sandek

The sandek is the most honored person at the brit. He sits wrapped in a tallit and holds the baby on his knees during the brit itself. That's why two tallitot are prepared for the brit: one for the father and one for the sandek.

In many families the honor of sandek goes to a grandfather, and some families follow a fixed order: for the first son, the grandfather on one side; for the second, the grandfather on the other side. Some honor a righteous, God-fearing person as sandek. Whatever your family's custom is, it's a good one.

What does the sandek need to do? Mainly to sit steadily, hold the baby the way the mohel shows him, and pray. The brit printables include a special prayer for the sandek that you can print and give him before the brit.

The kvatter

The kvatter carries the baby into the room where the brit takes place, and everyone stands and greets him with “Baruch haba” (ברוך הבא), the traditional greeting for the baby. You can honor several kvatters, who pass the baby from hand to hand until he reaches the Chair of Elijah. In many families this honor goes to a couple waiting to have children, as a segulah (a good sign).

The Chair of Elijah

According to tradition, Elijah the Prophet is present at every brit and watches over the baby. A special chair is prepared for him, and the baby is placed on it for a moment before the brit. You can honor one person with placing the baby on the chair, and another with lifting the baby from the chair and handing him to the father. Many synagogues have such a chair; at a hall, check in advance.

The honors after the circumcision

  • Holding the baby during the blessings: after the brit, once the baby is wrapped up, someone close holds him during the blessings over the cup.
  • The blessings and the naming: the person honored says the blessing over the cup of wine, and in the prayer “Kayem et hayeled hazeh” (קיים את הילד הזה) announces the baby's name for the first time. If you like, I can chant the blessings in the traditional melody.
  • Singing and piyutim: whoever loves to sing can lead the singing. I bring the speaker and microphone.
  • Words of Torah at the meal: someone close, or the community rabbi, says a few words of Torah in honor of the brit.
  • Zimun at the meal: at the seudat mitzvah (festive meal), someone is honored with the zimun, the invitation to Grace after Meals.
How to share the honors without hurting anyone

Before you decide, find out in the family who traditionally gets to be sandek for the first son, and who was expecting an honor at the previous brit. There are enough honors to make many people happy: several kvatters, placing the baby on the Chair of Elijah and lifting him from it, the sandek, holding the baby during the blessings, the blessings and the naming, the singing, the words of Torah and the zimun. That way everyone will be happy.

The bris ceremony step by step: where each honor fits

To make sharing the honors easier, here is the order of the ceremony and where each person comes in:

  • Bringing in the baby. The kvatter carries the baby in and the guests stand to welcome him. With several kvatters, the baby passes from hand to hand.
  • The Chair of Elijah. The baby is placed for a moment on the chair dedicated to the prophet, and then lifted from it.
  • The sandek. The baby goes to the sandek, who sits wrapped in a tallit and holds him on his knees during the brit.
  • The blessings and the circumcision. The mohel says the blessing “al hamilah” (על המילה) and performs the circumcision. The father says the blessing of bringing his son into the covenant of Abraham (להכניסו בבריתו של אברהם אבינו), and in many customs also “Shehecheyanu” (שהחיינו). The guests respond: כשם שנכנס לברית, כן ייכנס לתורה ולחופה ולמעשים טובים.
  • The blessings over the cup and the name. The honored person holds the wrapped baby, the blessings over the wine are said, and his name is heard for the first time.
  • Singing and the meal. Mazal tov, songs and piyutim in your family's tradition, followed by the seudat mitzvah.

A prayer while the baby cries

It is said that the time the baby cries at the brit is an auspicious time (et ratzon), and the guests pray for whatever is in their hearts. You can print the prayer and hand it out to the guests, which makes everyone a partner in the moment. It's in the brit printables, together with the order of the brit in the Sephardic, Mizrahi and Ashkenazi customs, and the songbook.

What to prepare for the sandek and the honored guests

  • Two tallitot – for the father and for the sandek.
  • A kiddush cup and dry red wine – for whoever says the blessings.
  • A siddur with the order of the blessings, unless the mohel brings one.
  • Spices (besamim). Sephardic and Mizrahi families customarily bring fragrant spices to smell during the brit, preferably myrtle (hadas).

Frequently asked questions

What does the sandek do at a bris?

He sits wrapped in a tallit and holds the baby on his knees during the brit itself. It's the greatest honor at the brit.

Who can be the sandek?

In many families it's a grandfather, and some honor a righteous, God-fearing person. What matters is that it's someone close to your heart, who will sit steadily and hold the baby the way the mohel shows him.

What is the difference between a sandek and a kvatter?

The kvatter carries the baby into the room and passes him on. The sandek holds him during the brit itself.

How many kvatters can there be?

As many as you like. The baby passes from hand to hand, so you can make several people happy with this honor.

Who reads the blessings and announces the name?

Whoever the parents honor with it, usually someone close in the family. If you like, the mohel can chant the blessings in the traditional melody and say the name.

Does the sandek need a tallit?

Yes, the sandek sits wrapped in a tallit. That's why two tallitot are prepared for the brit: one for the father and one for the sandek.

When do the guests first hear the baby's name?

In the blessings after the brit, in the prayer “Kayem et hayeled hazeh”. Until then the name is usually not revealed, not even to the mohel.

Is there a Chair of Elijah at the hall?

Many synagogues have one; at a hall, check in advance. If there isn't one, arrange to bring a chair from a nearby synagogue ahead of time.
