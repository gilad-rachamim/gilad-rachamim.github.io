How to make a bris invitation in a minute

The maker runs right in your phone or computer browser. Nothing to install, and the celebration details never leave your device: they are saved on your phone, so when you come back to the page everything is still there.

1. Pick the celebration

Brit milah, Zeved Habat or pidyon haben (redemption of the firstborn). The wording, verses and fields change by themselves: the pidyon gets a "Kohen" field, the brit gets a Shalom Zachar line, and Zeved Habat gets a kiddush and meal after the prayer.

2. Fill in when and where

Date, time, venue and address. If there is a prayer before the brit, enter it separately: guests who want to pray with you come earlier, and the rest come straight to the brit. The address powers the Waze directions button.

3. Choose a design and send

Five designs, and the preview updates instantly. Then: an image for WhatsApp, text to copy, a PDF with clickable buttons and an A4 notice with a QR code for the synagogue.

What every bris invitation must include

Nice words are lovely, but guests need three things first. The maker won't let you forget any of them: the day and date, both Hebrew and civil, so nobody mixes up the day; the time, plus the prayer time if the brit follows a prayer, because these are two different times and guests need both; and the venue with a full address – street, number and city – so the guest link opens the right directions.

When to send the invitation

Usually a day or two after the birth, once you have a mohel and a venue. Don't wait until the last day: people need time to clear their schedule and plan the trip. If the date is uncertain – for example jaundice, or a birth close to sunset – send a birth announcement first and the invitation once things are clear. The brit date calculator helps you find the day. A brit on its eighth day takes place even on Shabbat.

Shalom Zachar is held on the first Friday night after the birth, at home or in the synagogue. Tick it in the maker and the line appears both on the image and in the WhatsApp text. For the day itself, use the brit preparation checklist.

Bris invitation questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No payment, no premium version and no limits. No sign-up and no account: you leave your name and phone once, and that's it. There is a small line with the site link at the bottom of the image – that's all.

What do I do with the invitation?

Send the image on WhatsApp to family and friends, or copy the text. The guest link gives them Waze directions, add-to-calendar and RSVP.

Can I change the invitation after I've finished?

Yes. The details are saved on your phone. Come back to the page, change what you need and download again.

We don't have an exact date yet. What now?

Work it out in the brit date calculator. If there is doubt (jaundice, a birth close to sunset), send a birth announcement first, and the bris invitation once you have a mohel and a venue.

Does it work for Zeved Habat and pidyon haben?

Yes. At the top of the page choose "Zeved Habat" or "Pidyon haben" and the wording, verses and fields change. A designed invitation for every baby celebration. For Shalom Zachar there is ready wording and a line in the bris invitation itself.

Why does it say "announce" and not "invite"?

There is a common custom, cited by the Rema (Yoreh De'ah 265), not to invite people to the brit meal directly, so that someone who can't come isn't seen as turning down a seudat mitzvah. That's why the text says "we would be glad to share our joy with you". More about invitation wording.

Which language will the invitation be in?

On this page – English. The verse at the top, if you choose one, stays in Hebrew as in the original. If you need a Hebrew text, make the invitation on the Hebrew page (Hebrew).

Can I talk to the mohel before the birth?

Yes. A get-to-know call with Gilad Rachamim is free, even during pregnancy. The parents' guide helps you get ready ahead of time.
