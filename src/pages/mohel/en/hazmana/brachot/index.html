The traditional blessing

After the brit milah, everyone present says together: "כְּשֵׁם שֶׁנִּכְנַס לַבְּרִית, כֵּן יִכָּנֵס לְתוֹרָה, לְחֻפָּה וּלְמַעֲשִׂים טוֹבִים". Everyone says it as one, and it is always the right thing to write.

How it is read: "K'shem shenichnas labrit, ken yikanes l'Torah, l'chuppah u'l'ma'asim tovim." It is a wish that the baby grow up to Torah study, marriage (the chuppah) and good deeds.

Everything below is a variation on it: shorter, more personal, depending on who is wishing whom. Replace the name in square brackets and send. All texts on this page are free to copy, with no sign-up.

If you received a bris invitation

The brit milah is on the eighth day after birth, so the invitation usually comes only a few days ahead. The wording may look unusual: by a widespread custom, people are not invited outright to the bris meal – they are told when and where it is. So the text says "we would be happy to share our joy with you" rather than "you are invited". More on that, plus 12 ready bris invitation texts.

Can't come? That's fine. A warm wish on WhatsApp is plenty, and there is a ready text for that above. If the bris was postponed, for example because of jaundice, that is common. The parents need calm and support, not worry.

What to bring to a bris?

Nothing is required. If you'd like to, here are three ideas that are always welcome:

  • For the baby: clothes in size 3–6 months (not newborn – they have piles of those), a blanket, a first children's book.
  • For the parents: a ready meal for the evening after, a café voucher, or two hours of babysitting. That's what is really missing in the first week.
  • In the baby's merit: tzedakah (charity), or a sick person's name on a "refuah shleimah" (full recovery) list. A written wish that the parents will keep is always the cheapest and the most precious gift.

Quick questions

What do you say at a bris?

After the brit milah, everyone says the traditional blessing: "כשם שנכנס לברית, כן ייכנס לתורה, לחופה ולמעשים טובים". To the parents you say "mazel tov" and add a personal wish: health, nachas, that they may raise him.

What do you write in a bris card?

Three lines are enough: mazel tov on the birth of your son, one wish, and your signature. You can add a short verse in Hebrew. Examples above, ready to copy.

What do you say if the bris was postponed?

No pressure: "Mazel tov on your son! Wishing you an easy recovery, and may the bris take place soon, b'sha'ah tovah." A delay because of jaundice is common, and the parents need calm, not worry.

What gift do you bring to a bris?

A gift is not required. People usually bring something for the baby (clothes for 3–6 months, a blanket, a book) or for the parents (a ready meal, a voucher), and some give tzedakah in the baby's merit. A warm written wish is a gift too.

Why does a bris invitation say "we are happy to share" and not "you are invited"?

There is a widespread custom, cited by the Rema (Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah 265), not to invite people outright to the bris meal but to let them know when and where. It is a mitzvah meal, and no one who cannot come should be seen as turning it down. See the bris invitation wording page.

How do you reply if you cannot come to the bris?

A short, warm message is enough. For example: "We can't make it to the bris, but our hearts are with you. May he grow up healthy and bring you endless joy." You can copy a text like this from the wishes on this page.

What honors are there at a bris?

The kvatter carries the baby in, he is placed on the Chair of Elijah and taken from it, the sandek holds him during the brit milah, and someone recites the blessings and the naming. More on the page about the sandek and honors and in the bris honors card.
