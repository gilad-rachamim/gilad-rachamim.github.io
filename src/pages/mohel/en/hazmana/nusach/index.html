First: why "we are happy to share" and not "you are invited"

There is a widespread custom, cited by the Rema (Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah 265), not to invite people outright to the bris meal, but to let them know when and where. The reason is simple and beautiful: the bris meal is a mitzvah meal (seudat mitzvah), and no one who cannot come should feel they turned it down.

That is why every text here says "we would be happy to share our joy with you" or "the brit milah will take place, with G-d's help", and not "you are invited". If you are less strict about this, you can of course change it. What matters is that the message is warm, clear and has all the details.

Three things that must be there

The day and date (preferably Hebrew and civil), the time (and if there is a prayer service before, its time too), and the place with an address for navigation. Everything else is extra.

1. The classic wording

For the main message to family and friends. Works as a designed image and as text.

בס"ד
With praise and thanks to the Almighty,
we are happy to announce the birth of our son,
and would be happy to share with you the joy of his entering the covenant of Avraham Avinu.

The brit milah will take place, with G-d's help,
on [Thursday], [2 Kislev 5787] ([November 12, 2026])
Shacharit (morning prayer) at [9:15], the bris at [10:00]
at [Ohel Yitzchak Synagogue], [5 HaRav Kook St., Jerusalem]

A seudat mitzvah (festive meal) will follow the bris.

[Shira and Yossi Cohen]

4. Sephardic wording

In Sephardic communities it is customary to open with a piyut or a blessing, and to mention the Zohar study on the night before the bris.

בס"ד
"בָּרוּךְ הַבָּא בְּשֵׁם ה׳"

With joy and thanks to the Creator of the world,
we announce the birth of our son, b'sha'ah tovah u'mutzlachat.

The brit milah will take place, with G-d's help, on [Thursday], [2 Kislev 5787] ([November 12, 2026])
Shacharit [9:15], the bris [10:00]
at [Ohel Yitzchak Synagogue], [5 HaRav Kook St., Jerusalem]

On the night before the bris, [Wednesday] from [20:30], we would be happy to see you for Zohar study and the Brit Yitzchak at [the family home].

Joyfully announcing,
[Shira and Yossi Cohen]

5. A bris on Shabbat or a holiday

A bris on its proper day is held on Shabbat too. It is worth writing so guests can plan ahead, and adding information about where to stay.

בס"ד
We are happy to announce the birth of our son,
and would be happy to share with you the joy of the brit milah,
which will take place, with G-d's help, on Shabbat Kodesh, Parashat [Toldot], [4 Kislev 5787] ([November 14, 2026]),
after Shacharit (at about [10:00]), at [Ohel Yitzchak Synagogue], [Jerusalem].

A kiddush and seudat mitzvah will follow the bris.
If you would like to join us for Shabbat, there are places to stay nearby. We'd be glad to help arrange it.

[Shira and Yossi Cohen]

6. Postponed bris: an update message

Jaundice, or another medical reason. A short, calm message, no drama.

An update about our son's bris 🙏
On the doctor's and the mohel's instructions, the bris is postponed by a few days.
The new date: [Sunday], [Nov 15], at [10:00], at [the same place].
Thank you for understanding – see you at the simcha.
[Shira and Yossi]

10. Shalom Zachar

On the first Friday night after the birth. Usually at the family home or in the synagogue. About the custom.

בס"ד
With joy and thanks to the Almighty for the birth of our son,
we would be happy to see you at a "Shalom Zachar"
on Friday night, Shabbat Kodesh, Parashat [Chayei Sarah], [27 Cheshvan] ([Nov 6]),
from [20:30], at [the family home, 5 HaRav Kook St., Jerusalem].

Shabbat shalom and mazel tov,
[Shira and Yossi]

11. Pidyon haben

On the thirty-first day after birth, for a firstborn son born by natural delivery, when the parents are not Kohanim or Levites. Who is obligated and how it works.

בס"ד
With joy and thanks to the Almighty,
we would be happy to share with you the mitzvah of the pidyon haben (redemption) of our firstborn son,
which will take place, with G-d's help, on [Sunday], [3 Tevet 5787] ([December 13, 2026]),
at [19:30], in [the Ohel Yitzchak Synagogue hall], [Jerusalem].

A seudat mitzvah will follow the pidyon.

[Shira and Yossi Cohen]

12. Zeved HaBat

A celebration for the birth of a daughter. Many communities hold it on Shabbat, after she is named at the Torah reading.

בס"ד
"זְבָדַנִי אֱלֹהִים אֹתִי זֵבֶד טוֹב"

With joy and thanks to the Almighty for the birth of our daughter,
we would be happy to share with you the "Zeved HaBat" and her naming,
on Shabbat Kodesh, Parashat [Toldot], [4 Kislev 5787] ([November 14, 2026]),
after Shacharit, at [Ohel Yitzchak Synagogue], [Jerusalem].
Kiddush and a meal after the prayers.

[Shira and Yossi Cohen]

Five small tips before you hit "Send"

  • Hebrew and civil date together. Grandma looks for the Hebrew date, a colleague for the civil one. The bris date calculator gives you both.
  • A full address, not just the name of the place. There is more than one "Ohel Yitzchak Synagogue". Street and city save you phone calls on the day of the bris.
  • Prayer time and bris time separately. Whoever comes only for the bris knows when to arrive, and whoever wants to pray with you does too.
  • Don't wait with the invitation. The bris is on the eighth day. Send it a day or two after the birth, once you have booked a mohel and a place. Not sure yet? Send a birth announcement first.

Questions parents ask

Why does a bris invitation say "we are happy to share" instead of "you are invited"?

There is a widespread custom, cited by the Rema (Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah 265), not to invite people outright to the bris meal but to let them know when and where. The bris meal is a mitzvah meal, and no one who cannot come should be seen as turning it down. So the wording is "we would be happy to share our joy with you" or "the brit milah will take place, with G-d's help".

What must a bris invitation include?

The day and date (preferably Hebrew and civil), the time (and the prayer time if there is a service before), and the place with an address for navigation. It is worth adding who is announcing, and whether there is a meal after the bris.

When do you send a bris invitation?

A bris on time is on the eighth day after birth, so you send it as early as you can, usually a day or two after the birth, once you have booked a mohel and a place. If there is doubt (jaundice, a birth close to sunset), send a birth announcement first and the bris details later.

We haven't booked a mohel yet. What comes first?

First the mohel, then the place, then the invitation. A get-to-know-you call with me, Gilad Rachamim, a certified mohel, is free and with no obligation, even during pregnancy. How to choose a mohel.

Can a bris be held on Shabbat?

A bris on its proper day, the eighth day, is held on Shabbat too. A bris that is not on time, for example after a C-section or after a delay because of jaundice, does not override Shabbat and moves to a weekday. There is a separate Shabbat text above.

When do you send an invitation to a Shalom Zachar or a pidyon haben?

The Shalom Zachar is on the first Friday night after the birth, usually at home or in the synagogue, so people are told right away. A pidyon haben is held on the 31st day after the birth, and the invitation goes out about a week before.
