A bris invitation

The brit takes place on the eighth day, and on its eighth day even on Shabbat. The maker writes the Hebrew and civil date, the prayer time and the brit time separately, the venue and the address. You can add a line for the seudat mitzvah and for Shalom Zachar. The brit date calculator tells you the exact day.

A Zeved Habat invitation

Zeved Habat celebrates the birth of a daughter. It is often held on Shabbat, after she is named at the Torah reading. The invitation offers a kiddush and a meal after the prayer, with verses chosen for a girl.

A pidyon haben invitation

Pidyon haben (redemption of the firstborn) is held on day 31 after birth, for a firstborn of a natural birth whose parents are not kohanim or leviim. The invitation adds a field for the Kohen's name. Who is obligated and what you need: see pidyon haben.

And Shalom Zachar

Shalom Zachar is held on the first Friday night after the birth, at home or in the synagogue. No separate invitation needed: in the bris invitation tick "Shalom Zachar on Friday night before the brit", and the line appears on the image and in the WhatsApp text.

Why does a bris invitation say "announce" and not "invite"?

A common custom, cited by the Rema (Yoreh De'ah 265), is not to invite people to the brit meal directly, so that someone who can't come isn't seen as turning down a seudat mitzvah. That's why the brit wording says "we would be glad to share our joy with you". For Zeved Habat and pidyon haben the wording is similar and fits the occasion. More about the wording.
