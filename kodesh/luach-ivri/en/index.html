Hebrew calendar online: every date in one place

The calendar shows every day twice: the Gregorian date in large numbers and the Hebrew date under it. Next to the day it says whether it is a holiday, a fast day, Rosh Chodesh, Memorial Day or Independence Day, and every Shabbat shows the weekly Torah portion. Use the arrows to move to the next or previous month, pick any month and year, or jump back to today with one tap. Holiday and month names are written the way they are said in Hebrew – Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Hanukkah, Purim, Passover, Shavuot – with a short English explanation where it helps.

What is today's Hebrew date?

Above the calendar you always see today's date: the day of the week, the Gregorian date and the Hebrew date, for example "Monday, November 23, 2026 · 13 Kislev 5787". On the Jewish calendar a day begins in the evening, at sunset, not at midnight. So on Wednesday evening it is already Thursday's Hebrew date. If you open the page after sunset, it also shows the date that has already begun. Tap any day of the calendar to see its full Hebrew date in English and in Hebrew letters (י״ג בכסלו תשפ״ז), the way it is written on invitations and memorial plaques.

Which Hebrew year is 2026?

The Hebrew year is counted from the creation of the world and begins in the autumn, on Rosh Hashanah, the 1st of Tishrei. That is why every Gregorian year spans two Hebrew years. 2026 began in the year 5786 and from Rosh Hashanah it is 5787. The current Hebrew year is 5787, written תשפ״ז in Hebrew letters, and the next one, 5788, begins on October 2, 2027.

Jewish holidays 2026 and 2027

Below the calendar are two tables: the holidays of the current Hebrew year and of the next one. Each row has the holiday's name, the Hebrew date, the Gregorian date and the day of the week, and the evening it begins – such as the Passover Seder night or the eve of Yom Kippur. So you can see in advance when Hanukkah (December 5, 2026 – December 12, 2026), Purim, Passover (April 22, 2027), Israel's Independence Day, Shavuot, Rosh Hashanah (October 2, 2027 – October 3, 2027), Yom Kippur (October 11, 2027) and Sukkot (October 16, 2027) will be. Past holidays in the table are faded and the next one is highlighted.

The 12 months of the Hebrew calendar

The Hebrew calendar is lunisolar: months follow the moon and years are kept in line with the sun. A month has 29 or 30 days, and each one begins with the new moon, Rosh Chodesh. In 5787 the months are: Tishrei, Cheshvan, Kislev, Tevet, Shevat, Adar I, Adar II, Nisan, Iyar, Sivan, Tammuz, Av, Elul. It is a leap year, so it has 13 months: Adar I and Adar II. Choose "By Hebrew month" to see a whole Hebrew month with the Gregorian date of each day.

Holidays and days off in Israel

Days marked "day off" are Yom Tov days and Independence Day: in Israel there is no work, and schools and offices are closed. Chol HaMoed (the intermediate days of Sukkot and Passover), Hanukkah and Purim have a different color: they are festive days, but not legal days off. The exact school vacation dates are set every year by Israel's Ministry of Education, so check them there as well. This calendar follows the custom of the Land of Israel; outside Israel, some festivals have a second day.

Weekly Torah portion and Rosh Chodesh

Every Shabbat in the calendar shows the weekly Torah portion read in synagogues in Israel: Bereshit, Noach, Lech Lecha and so on, through the yearly cycle. Below the calendar is a list of the special days of the month: Shabbat portions, Rosh Chodesh, holidays and fast days. Rosh Chodesh days are green in the calendar and fast days are purple.

Questions and answers

What is today's Hebrew calendar date?

Today's Hebrew date is written above the calendar, and today is framed in gold. The Hebrew day begins at sunset, so in the evening the page also shows the date that has already begun. Tap any day to see its full Hebrew date, holiday and Torah portion.

What are the 12 months of the Hebrew calendar?

Counting from the new year: Tishrei, Cheshvan, Kislev, Tevet, Shevat, Adar, Nisan, Iyar, Sivan, Tammuz, Av and Elul. In a leap year Adar is doubled into Adar I and Adar II, so the year has 13 months. In the Torah the months are counted from Nisan, the month of the Exodus.

Which Hebrew year is 2026?

2026 spans two Hebrew years: 5786 until Rosh Hashanah, and 5787 from Rosh Hashanah on. The Hebrew year 5787 runs from September 12, 2026 to October 1, 2027.

What is the Hebrew calendar for the year 2026?

Pick any month of 2026 above to see every day with its Hebrew date, holidays, fast days, Rosh Chodesh and the weekly Torah portion. Below the calendar are two tables with all the Jewish holidays of 5787 (2026–2027) and 5788 (2027–2028).

When is Rosh Hashanah?

Rosh Hashanah 5787: September 12, 2026 – September 13, 2026. Rosh Hashanah 5788: October 2, 2027 – October 3, 2027. Like every Jewish holiday, it begins the evening before the first day.

Is this calendar for Israel or for outside Israel?

For Israel: one day of Yom Tov for each festival, and the Torah portions as they are read in Israel. Outside Israel some festivals last one day longer, and the weekly portion is sometimes a week apart.

Why is Rosh Chodesh sometimes two days?

When the previous month has 30 days, its 30th day is the first day of Rosh Chodesh and the 1st of the new month is the second day. When the previous month has 29 days, Rosh Chodesh is one day.

How do I print the Hebrew calendar?

Press "Print month" to get one clean page with the month on the screen. "Print the whole year" prints every month of the year, two per page. In the print window you can also choose "Save as PDF".

Is the calendar free?

Yes. The calendar, printing and holiday tables are free, with no sign-up and no ads. It opens in any phone or computer browser; there is nothing to install.
