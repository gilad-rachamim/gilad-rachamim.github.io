About Tu BiShvat

Tu BiShvat 2027 falls on January 23, 2027 and begins on the Friday evening, January 22, 2027. Last time it was on February 2, 2026. Below is general background about the holiday: what it celebrates, its main customs and why its date changes from year to year. It is a short introduction, not a guide to practice; for questions about how to keep the holiday, ask a rabbi.

What Tu BiShvat is

Tu BiShvat is the 15th day of the Hebrew month of Shevat. "Tu" is the number 15 written in Hebrew letters. The Mishnah calls it the New Year for Trees. It is the date from which the age of a fruit tree and the year of its fruit are counted for the laws of tithes and of orlah, the fruit of a tree's first three years.

The seven species

On Tu BiShvat many eat fruit, especially the seven species for which the Torah praises the Land of Israel: wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and dates. Some try a fruit they have not yet eaten that season, so as to say the Shehecheyanu blessing.

The Tu BiShvat Seder

The Kabbalists of Safed began the custom of a Tu BiShvat Seder: a meal of many fruits, in an order, with four cups of wine from white to red, and readings about trees and the Land of Israel. Today it is held in many homes, schools and synagogues.

Planting trees in Israel

In Israel Tu BiShvat falls at the end of the winter rains, when the almond trees begin to blossom. Schoolchildren and families plant trees all over the country, and the day has become a day of caring for nature.

Is Tu BiShvat a day off?

Tu BiShvat is not a Yom Tov. Work and school go on as usual. The penitential Tachanun prayer is not said, and many avoid fasting on that day.

Why Tu BiShvat begins in the evening

On the Hebrew calendar each day begins at sunset, not at midnight. So Tu BiShvat begins on the evening before the first date in the tables, and ends at nightfall on its last day. That is why the box at the top of this page shows both the date and the evening it begins. Candle lighting is usually about 18 minutes before sunset, and each city has its own times.

Questions and answers about Tu BiShvat

When is Tu BiShvat 2027?

Tu BiShvat 2027: January 23, 2027. It begins on the Friday evening, January 22, 2027, at sunset.

When is Tu BiShvat 2028?

Tu BiShvat 2028: February 12, 2028, beginning on the Friday evening, February 11, 2028.

When was Tu BiShvat 2026?

Tu BiShvat 2026 was on February 2, 2026, beginning on the Sunday evening, February 1, 2026.

What time does Tu BiShvat start?

Like every Jewish holiday, Tu BiShvat begins at sunset on the evening before its first date, and ends at nightfall. The exact minute depends on your city. For local times see candle lighting times or ask your synagogue.

Why does Tu BiShvat fall on a different date every year?

Tu BiShvat always has the same date on the Hebrew calendar, which follows the moon and adds a leap month in some years. On the Gregorian calendar the date moves by up to a few weeks. You can convert any date with the Hebrew date converter.

What fruits are eaten on Tu BiShvat?

Many eat the seven species of the Land of Israel: wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and dates, and other fruits too, especially dried fruit and carob.

Is Tu BiShvat a Jewish holiday?

It is a minor festive day, not a Yom Tov. Work and school go on as usual.
