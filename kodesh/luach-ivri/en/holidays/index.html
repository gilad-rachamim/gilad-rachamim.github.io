The Jewish holidays: what they are and when they fall

The Jewish holidays follow the cycle of the Hebrew year. In the autumn, in the month of Tishrei, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah come one after another. Winter brings Hanukkah, Tu BiShvat and Purim. Spring brings Passover, Israel's national days, Lag BaOmer and Shavuot. In the summer come the fasts of the 17th of Tammuz and Tisha B'Av. This page lists all of these dates for 2026 and 2027: the tables above give the date and the day of the week, and below is a short explanation of each holiday with its next date.

Rosh Hashanah – the Jewish New Year

Rosh Hashanah falls on the 1st and 2nd of Tishrei and opens the new Jewish year. It lasts two days everywhere, in Israel and abroad. The shofar, a ram's horn, is sounded in the synagogue, and many families eat apple dipped in honey for a sweet year. Rosh Hashanah begins the Ten Days of Repentance, which end on Yom Kippur. Next date: October 2, 2027 – October 3, 2027. Rosh Hashanah dates for the next five years.

Yom Kippur – the Day of Atonement

Yom Kippur, the 10th of Tishrei, is the most solemn day of the Jewish year. It is a fast of about 25 hours, from before sunset until nightfall the next day, and most of the day is spent in prayer in the synagogue. It ends with the Neilah prayer and a long blast of the shofar. Next date: October 11, 2027. Yom Kippur dates for the next five years.

Sukkot – the Festival of Booths

Sukkot begins on the 15th of Tishrei. Meals are eaten in a sukkah, a hut roofed with branches, in memory of the forty years in the desert after the Exodus. On the holiday the four species are held: the lulav (palm branch), etrog (citron), myrtle and willow. Outside Israel the first two days are Yom Tov, followed by the intermediate days of Chol HaMoed. Next date: October 16, 2027 – October 17, 2027. Sukkot dates for the next five years.

Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah

Right after the seven days of Sukkot comes Shemini Atzeret. Outside Israel it is followed by a second day, Simchat Torah, when the yearly cycle of Torah reading is completed and begun again, with dancing around the Torah scrolls. In Israel both are celebrated on a single day. Next date: October 24, 2027.

Hanukkah – the Festival of Lights

Hanukkah starts on the 25th of Kislev and lasts eight days. Each night one more candle is lit, from one on the first night to eight on the last. It recalls the rededication of the Holy Temple in Jerusalem in the days of the Maccabees. Hanukkah is not a Yom Tov: work and school go on as usual. Latkes and doughnuts are the traditional foods. Next date: December 5, 2026 – December 12, 2026. Hanukkah dates for the next five years.

Tu BiShvat – the New Year for Trees

Tu BiShvat is the 15th of Shevat. Many eat the fruits the Land of Israel is praised for: grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and dates. In Israel it is a day for planting trees. Next date: January 23, 2027. Tu BiShvat dates for the next five years.

Purim

Purim falls on the 14th of Adar, or the 14th of Adar II in a leap year. The Book of Esther (the Megillah) is read, gifts of food are sent to friends, gifts are given to the poor, and there is a festive meal. Children and adults dress up in costumes. In Jerusalem Purim is celebrated a day later, on Shushan Purim. The day before Purim is the Fast of Esther. Next date: March 23, 2027. Purim dates for the next five years.

Passover (Pesach)

Passover begins on the 15th of Nisan and celebrates the Exodus from Egypt. On the first night, and outside Israel on the second night too, families gather for the Seder and read the Haggadah. Matzah is eaten and no chametz (leavened food) is eaten all through the holiday. Outside Israel Passover lasts eight days: the first two and last two days are Yom Tov, with Chol HaMoed in between. Next date: April 22, 2027 – April 23, 2027. Passover dates for the next five years.

Lag BaOmer

Lag BaOmer is the 33rd day of the Omer count, the 18th of Iyar. The Omer is counted for forty-nine days, from Passover to Shavuot. In Israel bonfires are lit, and many travel to Mount Meron. Next date: May 25, 2027.

Shavuot – the giving of the Torah

Shavuot falls on the 6th of Sivan, seven weeks after Passover, and marks the giving of the Torah at Mount Sinai. Many stay up the first night learning Torah, and dairy foods are customary. Outside Israel Shavuot lasts two days, in Israel one. Next date: June 11, 2027 – June 12, 2027. Shavuot dates for the next five years.

Tisha B'Av and the other fast days

Tisha B'Av, the 9th of Av, is a day of mourning for the destruction of the First and Second Temples. The fast begins before sunset the evening before and ends at nightfall. There are four more fast days, from dawn to nightfall: the Fast of Gedaliah, the 10th of Tevet, the Fast of Esther and the 17th of Tammuz. When a fast falls on Shabbat it is moved, and the tables show the moved date. Next date: August 12, 2027.

Israel's national days

In spring Israel marks Yom HaShoah, Holocaust Remembrance Day (27 Nisan), Yom HaZikaron, the memorial day for Israel's fallen soldiers (4 Iyar), Yom HaAtzmaut, Israel Independence Day (5 Iyar), and Yom Yerushalayim, Jerusalem Day (28 Iyar). These dates are sometimes moved by a day or two so they do not touch Shabbat; the tables show the day they are actually marked. Next date: May 12, 2027.

Why holidays begin in the evening

On the Hebrew calendar the day begins at sunset. So a holiday begins on the evening before the date in the table. For example, if the first day of Passover is April 22, 2027 – April 23, 2027, the first Seder is held on the evening of April 21, 2027. The same goes for the fasts of Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av: they begin in the evening. A holiday ends not at midnight but at nightfall. The tables note, next to the main holidays, on which evening they begin. For the exact candle-lighting time in your city, check a local synagogue or Jewish calendar for your city.

Why the dates are different every year

The Hebrew calendar is lunisolar. A month is one cycle of the moon, 29 or 30 days, and so that the holidays do not drift out of their seasons, some years add a thirteenth month, Adar II. That is why Passover is always on the 15th of Nisan on the Hebrew calendar, but on the Gregorian calendar it can fall at the end of March or late in April. To see how the Hebrew months line up with the Gregorian ones, open the Hebrew calendar by month, or convert any single date with the Hebrew date converter.

Holidays in the USA and in Israel

The tables on this page follow the custom outside Israel, as kept in the USA, Canada, the UK and every other country outside the Land of Israel. Outside Israel Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret, Passover and Shavuot each have an extra day of Yom Tov, so Passover lasts eight days and Shavuot two, and Simchat Torah is the day after Shemini Atzeret. In Israel Passover lasts seven days, Shavuot one, and Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah are the same day. Rosh Hashanah is two days everywhere and Yom Kippur one day everywhere. The table "Holiday dates in Israel and outside Israel" shows both.

Planning work and school around the holidays

Many people use this page to plan time off for Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Passover and Shavuot, and to schedule school tests, meetings and events so they do not fall on a holiday. Remember that each holiday starts the evening before, so an event on the evening before Yom Tov will also clash. Hanukkah, Purim and the days of Chol HaMoed are festive days but are not Yom Tov.

What are the most important holidays in Judaism?

The holidays written in the Torah are Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur and the three pilgrimage festivals: Passover, Shavuot and Sukkot (with Shemini Atzeret). Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are known as the High Holidays. Hanukkah and Purim were added later, in the days of the Second Temple and the Book of Esther.

Why do Jewish holidays start at sunset?

On the Hebrew calendar the day begins at sunset, not at midnight. So every holiday and every fast day begins on the evening before the date in the table, and ends at nightfall.

Why do the dates of Jewish holidays change every year?

The Hebrew calendar is lunisolar: months follow the moon, and some years get a thirteenth month (Adar II) so the holidays stay in their season. On the Hebrew calendar a holiday always has the same date; on the Gregorian calendar it moves. You can convert any date with the Hebrew date converter. Learning to read Hebrew: the Hebrew alphabet.

Why are some holidays one day longer outside Israel?

Outside Israel the first and last days of Passover, the first day of Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret and Shavuot are each kept for two days, so Passover lasts eight days and Shavuot two. In Israel each of these is one day. Rosh Hashanah is two days everywhere and Yom Kippur one day everywhere. The tables on this page follow the dates outside Israel, and a separate table shows the dates in Israel.

What does "Yom Tov" mean in the tables?

Yom Tov marks a festival day on which work is not done, much like Shabbat. Chol HaMoed, Hanukkah and Purim are festive days but not Yom Tov. For questions about what is allowed on a given day, ask a rabbi.
