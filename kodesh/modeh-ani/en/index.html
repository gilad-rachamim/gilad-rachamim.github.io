Modeh Ani in Hebrew, transliteration and English, on your phone

Modeh Ani (also spelled Mode Ani or Modeh Ani Lefanecha) is the first prayer a Jewish person says in the morning. It is four short lines, and it can be learned by a three-year-old and said by anyone at any age. This page gives the Hebrew text with vowels in large print, a transliteration under it for anyone who does not yet read Hebrew fluently, and an English translation taken from a named source. It is the same wording as the one in the Hebrew morning prayers page of this site, where it appears together with the whole morning order.

What is Modeh Ani?

The Hebrew name means “I give thanks”. The prayer is a single sentence of thanks to G-d for returning the soul in the morning. It is said immediately on waking, while still in bed, even before getting up. It is short enough to say with eyes closed, and many families teach it as the first Hebrew sentence a child learns.

Modeh Ani transliteration

The transliteration follows modern Israeli pronunciation: “ch” is the throaty sound of the letters chet and chaf, “a” is as in “father”, and an apostrophe is not used because the line has no hard breaks. Read the Hebrew with your eyes and the Latin letters with your lips; after a week the Hebrew line stays in the memory without help.

Modeh Ani meaning, word by word

“Modeh ani” – I give thanks. “Lefanecha” – before You. “Melech chai vekayam” – living and everlasting King. “Shehechezarta bi nishmati” – that You have returned my soul to me. “Bechemla” – with mercy. “Rabba emunatecha” – great is Your faithfulness. In plain words: every morning the soul comes back, and the first thing a person does with the new day is thank. This is a plain explanation and not an authoritative commentary; for any deeper question, ask a rabbi.

Modeh Ani for boys and Modah Ani for girls

The Hebrew word for “thank” changes with the speaker. Boys and men say modeh (vowel segol), girls and women say modah (vowel kamatz). Everything else in the prayer is identical, and both versions are written out separately above so that no one has to guess which vowel to use.

When do you say Modeh Ani?

As soon as you wake up, before getting out of bed, before washing hands and before any other morning blessing. The page is saved on the phone after the first visit, so it opens even without a connection, even beside the bed in the dark.

Why Modeh Ani is said before washing hands

The wording of Modeh Ani does not contain the Name of G-d. Because of that, it may be said even with unwashed hands, which is why it comes first, before the hand washing and the blessing over it. After Modeh Ani come netilat yadayim, “Asher Yatzar” and the other morning blessings, all in order on the morning blessings page (in Hebrew).

How to teach Modeh Ani to a child

Add one part each day. Day one: “Modeh ani lefanecha”. Day two: “melech chai vekayam”. Day three: “shehechezarta bi nishmati bechemla”. Day four: “rabba emunatecha”. After four days the child says the whole line in one breath. Say it together every morning in the same place, check that the child uses the form for their gender, and keep this page open on the phone until they stop looking at it. No grades and no comparisons: what matters is that the first words of the day are words of thanks.

Modeh Ani for adults who are learning

If you are converting, returning to observance or just starting a morning routine, Modeh Ani is the easiest first step: one sentence, no equipment, no hands washing, no time pressure. When it feels natural, continue to the next steps in order: putting on tefillin step by step (Hebrew), the Shema Yisrael in English, and the Traveler's Prayer for the road. For the Hebrew letters themselves, there is an Alef-Bet page in English.

The bedtime counterpart

The day that begins with a short prayer of thanks can end with a short prayer before sleep. That text, with the Shema said on the bed, is on the page Shema al hamita (Hebrew).

Is there a difference between communities?

The text here is the one found in the usual Ashkenaz siddurim and it is the same as in the Hebrew page of this site. If your family follows a different custom, keep it, and ask your rabbi for any practical question.

Questions and answers

What is Modeh Ani?

Modeh Ani is the short Jewish prayer of thanks said first thing in the morning, while still in bed: “I give thanks to You, living and everlasting King, for You have restored my soul with mercy.”

When do you say Modeh Ani?

Immediately on waking, before washing hands and before any other morning blessing.

Why can Modeh Ani be said before washing hands?

Because its wording does not contain the Name of G-d, so it may be said even with unwashed hands.

Do women say Modeh Ani or Modah Ani?

Women say Modah ani, boys and men say Modeh ani. Both are on the page, each in its own heading.

What does Modeh Ani mean in English?

“I give thanks to You living and everlasting King for You have restored my soul with mercy. Great is Your faithfulness.” (Sefaria, Metsudah-based translation, CC-BY.)

How do you teach Modeh Ani to children?

Add one of the four parts each day, say it together every morning in the same place, and keep the vowelized text in front of the child until they remember it alone.
