    What is gematria?

    Gematria (Hebrew גימטריה) is the practice of reading Hebrew letters as numbers and adding them up. Hebrew has no separate digits in its traditional writing: the letters themselves count, from Alef = 1 to Tav = 400. That is why Hebrew dates, years and chapter numbers are still written with letters, and why every Hebrew word also has a number. For example, שלום (shalom) = 300 + 30 + 6 + 40 = 376.

    Jewish tradition treats these numbers as a way of noticing connections between words. The sages of the Talmud and the Midrash used gematria to point out hints in the Torah, and classic commentators such as Rashi quote them often. The Mishnah in Pirkei Avot calls gematria "side dishes of wisdom": it adds depth and flavor to learning, alongside the plain meaning of the text.

    Three methods of counting

    Standard gematria (mispar hechrechi)

    The usual method, and the one meant when people simply say "gematria". Each letter has its value from 1 to 400, and the final letters count like the regular ones: final Mem (ם) is 40, just like Mem (מ).

    Large finals (mispar gadol)

    Here the five final letters continue the count after Tav: final Kaf 500, final Mem 600, final Nun 700, final Pei 800 and final Tsadi 900. Words without final letters have the same value in both methods.

    Small gematria (mispar katan)

    The zeros are dropped, so every letter is worth 1 to 9: Yod is 1, Kaf is 2, Kuf is 1, Resh is 2, Shin is 3 and Tav is 4. This method gives small numbers that are easy to compare.

    Famous gematria examples

    Chai, life: חי (chai) = 8 + 10 = 18. This is why gifts and donations are given in multiples of 18.

    One and love: אחד (echad) = 1 + 8 + 4 = 13, and אהבה (ahavah) = 1 + 5 + 2 + 5 = 13. Both are 13.

    Wine and secret: יין (yayin, wine) and סוד (sod, secret) are both 70. The Talmud says that when wine goes in, a secret comes out.

    Eliezer and the 318 men: the Torah says Abraham went out to war with 318 men; the Talmud notes that אליעזר (Eliezer, Abraham's servant) has the gematria 318.

    Tzitzit and the 613 commandments: ציצית (tzitzit) is 600; Rashi explains that with the 8 strings and 5 knots of each fringe it makes 613, the number of the commandments of the Torah.

    Mashiach and nachash: משיח (Mashiach, Messiah) and נחש (nachash, snake) are both 358, a pair often discussed in Chassidic teaching.

    Gematria of your Hebrew name

English spellings do not have a gematria in the Jewish sense, and the same English name can be written in Hebrew in more than one way: Michael can be מיכאל (101), and a name written with an extra Yod or Vav gains 10 or 6. If you are not sure of the spelling, ask your rabbi.

    Writing numbers in Hebrew letters

    Gematria also works the other way: any number can be written in Hebrew letters by adding the largest letters first. 613 is תרי״ג, and the Hebrew year 5787 is written תשפ״ז, usually without the thousands. By custom 15 and 16 are written טו and טז (9 + 6 and 9 + 7) instead of Yod-Hei and Yod-Vav, because those letters spell part of the Name of God.     Gematria questions and answers

    What is gematria?

    Gematria is the Jewish method of adding up the numerical values of the Hebrew letters in a word. Every Hebrew letter is also a number: Alef is 1, Bet is 2, up to Tav, which is 400. The gematria of a word is the sum of its letters, so חי (chai, "life") is 8 + 10 = 18. The calculator on this page does the sum for any Hebrew word, name or verse.

    How do I use this gematria calculator?

    Type a Hebrew word, name or verse in the box, or tap the letters on the Hebrew keyboard under it if your device has no Hebrew keyboard.     What is the gematria of my name?

    Write your Hebrew name in Hebrew letters, as it appears on your ketubah, bar or bat mitzvah certificate or Hebrew documents, and type it in the calculator. If you are not sure how your name is spelled in Hebrew, ask your rabbi: different spellings give different values. The table of popular names on this page shows common examples.

    What is the numerical value of each Hebrew letter?

    Alef to Tet are 1 to 9, Yod to Tsadi are 10 to 90 in steps of ten, and Kuf, Resh, Shin and Tav are 100, 200, 300 and 400. The five final letters (ך ם ן ף ץ) have the same value as the regular letters in the standard method, and 500 to 900 in the large-finals method. The full table is on this page.

    What is the difference between standard, large finals and small gematria?

    Standard gematria (mispar hechrechi) counts final letters like regular ones. Large finals (mispar gadol) continues after Tav: final Kaf 500, final Mem 600, final Nun 700, final Pei 800, final Tsadi 900. Small gematria (mispar katan) drops the zeros, so Yod is 1, Kuf is 1 and Resh is 2. The calculator shows all three at once.

    Why do Jews give gifts in multiples of 18?

    Because 18 is the gematria of חי (chai), "life". A gift or donation of 18, 36 or 180 dollars carries a wish for life. Many people wear a Chai pendant for the same reason.

    What is gematria used for?

    In Jewish tradition gematria is one of the ways of finding connections in the Torah: the Talmud, the Midrash and classic commentators such as Rashi point out words of equal value. The Mishnah in Pirkei Avot (chapter 3) calls gematria "side dishes of wisdom", meaning it adds flavor to Torah study but does not replace its plain meaning. People also use it for names, dates and meaningful numbers.

    What is 666 in gematria?

    In Hebrew letters 666 is written תרס״ו: Tav 400 + Resh 200 + Samech 60 + Vav 6. Type 666 in the number box on this page to see Hebrew words and Tanach phrases whose letters add up to 666.
