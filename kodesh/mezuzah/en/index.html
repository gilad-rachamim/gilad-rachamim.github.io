The blessing for affixing a mezuzah

Hold the mezuzah in its place on the doorpost, say the blessing, and affix it right away.

Before affixing the mezuzah – the blessing

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו וְצִוָּנוּ לִקְבֹּעַ מְזוּזָה:

Say: Baruch ata A-donai Eloheinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu likbo'a m'zuzah.

Meaning: Blessed are You, Lord our God, King of the Universe, who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to affix a mezuzah.

Those present answer: Amen

After affixing it – in Siddur Edot HaMizrach

זֶה־הַשַּׁעַר לַה', צַדִּיקִים יָבֹאוּ בוֹ:

Say: Zeh hasha'ar la'A-donai, tzadikim yavo'u vo.

Meaning: This is the gate of the Lord, the righteous shall come through it! (Psalms 118:20)

Siddur Edot HaMizrach writes that after affixing the mezuzah one kisses it and says this verse.

Hebrew: Siddur Sefard and Siddur Edot HaMizrach, Torat Emet editions, through Sefaria (public domain); the blessing is the same in both. English: Sefaria Community Translation (CC0). The Name of God is written in short form. Transliteration: written for this page.

The verse the mezuzah comes from

וּכְתַבְתָּם עַל־מְזוּזֹת בֵּיתֶךָ וּבִשְׁעָרֶיךָ:

Say: Uchtavtam al m'zuzot beitecha uvish'arecha.

Meaning: And thou shalt write them upon the door-posts of thy house, and upon thy gates. (Deuteronomy 6:9)

Hebrew: Tanach with vowels on Sefaria (public domain). English: The Holy Scriptures, JPS 1917 (public domain).

How to put up a mezuzah, step by step

1. Get a kosher mezuzah scroll

The mezuzah is the parchment scroll, handwritten by a scribe (sofer), not the decorative case. A printed copy is not kosher. Buy it from a reliable Judaica store or through your rabbi, and roll it from the end toward the beginning, so the first word, Shema, is on the outside.

2. Know the place on the doorpost

Siddur Edot HaMizrach, the source of this page's text, says the mezuzah goes on the right doorpost as you enter, within the doorway, at the beginning of the upper third of its height, and upright. Some communities tilt it; customs differ. Ask your rabbi about your own doors and which rooms need a mezuzah.

3. Say the blessing

Hold the mezuzah in its place and say the blessing: “Baruch ata A-donai Eloheinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu likbo'a m'zuzah.”. Anyone standing with you answers Amen.

4. Affix it right away

Fix the case to the doorpost with nails, screws or strong tape, without talking about other things between the blessing and the affixing. In Siddur Edot HaMizrach, after affixing it one kisses it and says “Zeh hasha'ar la'A-donai, tzadikim yavo'u vo” – This is the gate of the Lord, the righteous shall come through it.

Questions about your own home

Which doors need a mezuzah, whether to say one blessing for several, when to put it up in a new or rented home, and how often to have the scrolls checked are questions of Jewish law. Ask an Orthodox rabbi; many will come and help put up the mezuzot.

What is written inside a mezuzah

The scroll holds the first two paragraphs of the Shema: “Shema Yisrael” (Deuteronomy 6:4-9) and “V'haya im shamoa” (Deuteronomy 11:13-21). Both include the commandment to write these words “upon the doorposts of your house and upon your gates”. Read both paragraphs, line by line with transliteration and English, on our Shema Yisrael page.

Why Jews have a mezuzah on the door

A commandment from the Torah

The mezuzah is one of the 613 commandments. The verse in Deuteronomy says to write God's words on the doorposts, and the blessing says so: God “commanded us to affix a mezuzah”.

A reminder at every door

Each time we come in or go out, the mezuzah reminds us of the Shema: that God is one, and that we love Him with all our heart, soul and might. Many touch the mezuzah and kiss their fingers when they pass it.

The mezuzah and a new home

Putting up the mezuzot is often the center of a housewarming (chanukat habayit). Family and friends gather, the blessing is said, and a meal follows.

Reading the transliteration

The transliteration spells each word in English letters. “ch” sounds like the end of “Bach”, “tz” like the end of “cats”, and an apostrophe marks a very short vowel, as in kid'shanu. The Name of God is written “A-donai” out of respect. To learn the letters, see the Hebrew alphabet page.

Questions and answers

What is the blessing for putting up a mezuzah?

Before affixing the mezuzah, say: "Baruch ata A-donai, Eloheinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu likbo'a m'zuzah" – Blessed are You, Lord our God, King of the Universe, who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to affix a mezuzah. The Hebrew with vowels is at the top of this page.

What words are inside a mezuzah?

The parchment inside a mezuzah holds two passages from the Torah: the first two paragraphs of the Shema, "Shema Yisrael" (Deuteronomy 6:4-9) and "V'haya im shamoa" (Deuteronomy 11:13-21), handwritten by a scribe. You can read both paragraphs with transliteration and English on our Shema page.

Is the Shema prayer in the mezuzah?

Yes. The mezuzah scroll is the first two paragraphs of the Shema, including the verse "And you shall write them upon the doorposts of your house and upon your gates" – the source of the mitzvah itself.

What is the significance of a mezuzah?

The mezuzah fulfills the Torah's commandment to write God's words on the doorposts of our homes. It marks the home as a Jewish home, and every time we pass the door it reminds us of the Shema: that God is one, and that we are to love Him with all our heart.

Where and how is the mezuzah placed?

Siddur Edot HaMizrach, printed on this page's source, says it is placed on the right side of the doorway as you enter, in the upper third of the doorway, upright. Customs about tilting it differ between communities. For your own doors – which rooms need one, and how to place it – ask an Orthodox rabbi.

Do you put a mezuzah on the inside or outside of the door?

It goes on the doorpost itself, on the right side as you walk in. Exactly where on the doorpost, and which doors need a mezuzah, are questions of Jewish law; ask your rabbi about your home.

Do I say a blessing for each mezuzah?

When putting up several mezuzot at once, the question of one blessing or several is a question of Jewish law. Ask your rabbi before you start.

Where do I get a kosher mezuzah?

A kosher mezuzah is a parchment handwritten by a qualified scribe (sofer). Printed copies are not kosher. Buy from a reliable Judaica store or through your rabbi, and have the mezuzot checked from time to time as your rabbi advises.
