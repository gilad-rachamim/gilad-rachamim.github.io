A Judaism quiz to practice before meeting the rabbi

On this page you can practice with short questions on the basics of Jewish knowledge: the Tanach, the holidays, the Hebrew calendar, prayer, Jewish objects and the blessings over food. Every question comes with four answers and a short explanation, and there are no scores. Whoever makes a mistake sees the right answer and moves on. Everything is free, with no sign-up and no ads.

The tool helps you learn and organize. Every halachic and personal question – to your accompanying rabbi or the beit din. This is not an official test, and it makes no prediction about the beit din.

What the tool is, and what it is not

This is a tool for practicing general knowledge. It does not replace learning with an accompanying rabbi, and it does not test you on behalf of the beit din. The beit din asks mainly about your personal path, your connection to Judaism and your commitment to a Jewish life. The questions here help mostly to build confidence and a shared language, so that conversations with the rabbi and in the community feel more familiar.

Which topics are included

The quiz is divided into seven topics: Tanach, holidays, calendar, prayer, mitzvot and objects, blessings, and places and terms. You can choose one topic, or go through everything together. Each topic has simple questions and a few that are a little more challenging.

How to practice well

Ten minutes a day are better than an hour once a week. Every question you got wrong gets a short explanation, and questions you answered correctly the first time do not come back until you finish all the questions in the topic. Your progress is saved on your phone, so you can come back at any hour.

Tanach

In the Tanach topic you are asked about the Five Books of the Torah, the Patriarchs and Matriarchs, Moshe and Yehoshua, and the megillot that are read on the holidays. These are the basis of almost every conversation about Judaism, and they come up again and again in the weekly Torah portion.

Holidays and the Hebrew calendar

The holidays and the Hebrew calendar are part of life in every community. Here you get to know the months, the order of the holidays, and the foods and objects of each holiday. You can continue to the Jewish calendar (Hebrew) on the site to see the dates.

Prayer

The prayers during the day, the siddur, the synagogue and the minyan. These are the topics that come up when you arrive at a synagogue for the first time. The prayer pages on the site, such as Shacharit (Hebrew), can help.

Blessings

Questions about the blessings before eating, according to the texts on the blessings over food page. On that page you can also practice as much as you like, with the full Hebrew text and a transliteration.

Mitzvot and objects

Mezuzah, tefillin, tzitzit, challah and Shabbat candles: what they are and what they are used for. The explanation here is short, and the details are learned with your accompanying rabbi. You can keep track of what you are learning in my mitzvot.

Places and terms

Jerusalem, the Western Wall, the Mishnah, the Talmud, the Haggadah and Kiddush: terms that come up again in conversations with a rabbi and in the synagogue. If a term is not clear, you can look it up in the Judaism glossary.

Why general knowledge helps

Whoever knows the figures, the holidays and the objects feels more at home in the community, and can follow conversations on Shabbat and holidays. General knowledge also helps you ask better questions and understand what your accompanying rabbi explains. It is not a goal in itself, but a tool that makes the path easier.

Everyone at their own pace

Some people practice a little every day, and some come back to the page once a week. Both ways are good. There is no need to go through all the questions at once, and there is no required order. You can stop in the middle and continue later, and your progress stays on your phone.

Learning tips

It is worth reading the explanation even when you answer correctly, because sometimes it adds a new detail. It is worth practicing together with a spouse, a friend or someone from the community, and asking each other questions. And a question that especially interests you is worth writing down and asking your accompanying rabbi.

What to do with questions that stay open

Sometimes a question raises curiosity or confusion. That is natural and even good. Write it down and ask your accompanying rabbi at the next meeting. The rabbi is the one who can explain according to the tradition and according to your personal situation, and the practice here is only meant to prepare the ground.

Who this practice is for

For converts at the beginning of the path and for those who are further along, and also for anyone interested who wants to understand what is learned in the process. The questions are written in plain English, and Jewish terms keep their Hebrew names with a short explanation, so that you get used to the words you will hear in the community.

Why there is no score

A score creates pressure, and we do not want pressure. Whoever learns under pressure forgets quickly. That is why the tool only shows how many questions you answered, and not "passed" or "failed". This way you can enjoy the learning.

What to do after practicing

After a few rounds you can send your accompanying rabbi on WhatsApp how much you practiced, and ask about what is not clear. You can continue to my first Shabbat, or go back to the conversion roadmap. The quiz is also available in Hebrew and in Russian.

Frequently asked questions about the Judaism quiz

Is this the beit din test?

No. This is personal practice of general knowledge. It has no official connection to the beit din, and it makes no prediction about any result. The beit din asks mainly about your personal path.

How many questions are in the tool?

There are 69 questions in seven topics, and your progress is saved on your phone.

What happens when I make a mistake?

You see the right answer with an explanation, with no score and no remarks. Making mistakes is part of learning.

Are the answers based on a source?

The questions deal with well-known general knowledge. The texts of the blessings are taken from a siddur on the Sefaria site. Detailed halachic questions – to your accompanying rabbi.

Can I practice without signing up?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no ads and no payment.

Where is my progress saved?

In your own browser only. We do not keep it on a server.

Can I choose a specific topic?

Yes, with the buttons above the question.

How do I send my progress to my accompanying rabbi?

Tap "Send to my accompanying rabbi on WhatsApp", choose a contact and confirm.

Are there questions about halacha?

The questions are general knowledge, such as the names of holidays, blessings and terms. Detailed halachic topics stay with your accompanying rabbi.

Is this tool free?

Yes. Free, with no limit, no sign-up and no ads.
