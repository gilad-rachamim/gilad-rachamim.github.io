The blessings before eating

The texts are taken as they are from the Ashkenaz siddur on Sefaria (public domain). God's name is written in short form.

Washing the hands (netilat yadayim)

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ עַל נְטִילַת יָדָים:
Transliteration
Baruch ata A-donai E-loheinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu al netilat yadayim.

When: Before eating bread, after washing the hands. The source says: before eating bread one washes the hands and says it.

HaMotzi

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם הַמּוֹצִיא לֶחֶם מִן הָאָרֶץ:
Transliteration
Baruch ata A-donai E-loheinu melech ha'olam, ha-motzi lechem min ha'aretz.

When: Over bread. Examples: bread, such as challah or a roll.

Borei minei mezonot

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם בּוֹרֵא מִינֵי מְזוֹנוֹת:
Transliteration
Baruch ata A-donai E-loheinu melech ha'olam, borei minei mezonot.

When: Over a cooked dish of the five grains and over baked goods. Examples: cake, cookies, cooked pasta.

Borei pri hagafen

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הַגָּפֶן:
Transliteration
Baruch ata A-donai E-loheinu melech ha'olam, borei p'ri hagafen.

When: Over wine. Examples: wine, and grape juice according to custom.

Borei pri ha'etz

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָעֵץ:
Transliteration
Baruch ata A-donai E-loheinu melech ha'olam, borei p'ri ha'etz.

When: Over fruit of the tree. Examples: apple, orange, pear, grapes.

Borei pri ha'adama

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה:
Transliteration
Baruch ata A-donai E-loheinu melech ha'olam, borei p'ri ha'adama.

When: Over produce of the ground. Examples: carrot, cucumber, potato.

Shehakol

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם שֶׁהַכֹּל נִהְיָה בִּדְבָרוֹ:
Transliteration
Baruch ata A-donai E-loheinu melech ha'olam, shehakol niyah bidvaro.

When: Over meat and fish, milk, egg and cheese, mushrooms, and also over drinks other than wine. Examples: meat, fish, egg, cheese, water, chocolate.

Source: Sefaria, Ashkenaz siddur, "Blessings of enjoyment – first blessings" (Daat Siddur Ashkenaz, public domain). The transliteration and examples are a reading aid and not part of the text.

Jewish blessings over food: what to say on each food

The blessings of enjoyment (birchot hanehenin) are the blessings said before enjoying something, mainly before eating and drinking. Someone who starts learning finds there are several different blessings, and it is not always clear which one goes with which food. This page has seven blessings in full Hebrew with niqqud, a transliteration at a tap and short practice that teaches you to choose correctly. Everything is free, no sign-up and no ads.

The tool helps you learn and organize. Every halachic and personal question – to your accompanying rabbi or the beit din.

Why we bless before eating

A blessing before eating is a way to give thanks for what we have and to stop for a moment before the first bite. It is short, and it goes with every meal and every snack. People usually start with the basic blessings and add more over time.

How to remember which bracha to say

The easy way is to split foods into groups: bread, baked goods and grain dishes, wine, fruits that grow on a tree, vegetables that grow from the ground, and everything else. According to the siddur on Sefaria, "Shehakol nihyah bidvaro" covers meat and fish, milk, egg and cheese, mushrooms, and also drinks other than wine.

The blessing over bread

Over bread we say "HaMotzi lechem min ha'aretz". Before that, according to the siddur, we wash the hands and say the blessing on washing the hands. After the meal we say Birkat Hamazon, which is in our bencher (Hebrew).

The blessing over cake and grain dishes

Over a cooked dish of the five grains and over baked goods we say "Borei minei mezonot". Familiar examples: cake, cookies and cooked pasta. When there is a doubt, for example with a mixed food, ask your accompanying rabbi.

The blessing over wine

Over wine we say "Borei pri hagafen". This blessing is also familiar from Kiddush on Shabbat, where it appears as part of a longer text. You can see it on the page My first Shabbat.

The blessing over fruits and vegetables

Over a fruit that grows on a tree we say "Borei pri ha'etz", for example apple, orange, pear and grapes. Over a vegetable or fruit that grows from the ground we say "Borei pri ha'adama", for example carrot, cucumber and potato. For some fruits the question is more complex, so in such cases turn to your accompanying rabbi.

The blessing over water, meat, cheese and chocolate

Over the great majority of other foods and drinks we say "Shehakol nihyah bidvaro": water, meat, fish, egg, cheese, milk and chocolate. It is the general blessing, and it has its own card on the page.

How to practice so you remember

The practice here is short: one food at a time, six options and an explanation after you choose. Ten minutes a day are enough. There are no grades and no "you failed"; whoever is wrong simply sees the right text and tries again. Your progress is saved on your phone.

How to read the transliteration

The transliteration in Latin letters appears when you tap "Transliteration", and it is meant to help those who do not yet read Hebrew with niqqud easily. Over time you move on to reading the text itself. How to pronounce God's name – ask your accompanying rabbi.

Blessings after eating

After eating you say an after-blessing. The bencher on the site has ready texts of Birkat Hamazon, "Al hamichya" and "Borei nefashot", in several versions. The version that suits you depends on your community and your accompanying rabbi.

Frequently asked questions about blessings over food

What blessing do we say over bread?

"HaMotzi lechem min ha'aretz", after washing the hands and the blessing on the washing. According to the siddur on Sefaria, this is done before eating bread.

What blessing do we say over cake?

Over baked goods and a cooked dish of the five grains we say "Borei minei mezonot", according to the siddur on Sefaria.

What blessing do we say over fruit?

Over fruit of the tree "Borei pri ha'etz", and over produce of the ground "Borei pri ha'adama". For fruits whose category is unclear, ask your accompanying rabbi.

What blessing do we say over water?

"Shehakol nihyah bidvaro", like other drinks except wine, according to the siddur.

What blessing do we say over meat, fish and cheese?

"Shehakol nihyah bidvaro". The siddur lists meat and fish, milk, egg and cheese, and mushrooms.

What blessing do we say over wine?

"Borei pri hagafen".

What blessing do we say over chocolate?

Usually "Shehakol nihyah bidvaro". If the food is a mix of other ingredients, it is worth asking your accompanying rabbi.

How do I remember the blessings?

Practice a few minutes every day with the foods on the cards, and your memory improves fast. You can also print the page and hang it near the kitchen.

When do we say the blessing before eating?

Before the first bite. If you forgot, ask your accompanying rabbi what to do.
