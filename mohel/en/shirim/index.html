When do people sing at a brit milah?

Music goes with the whole day, and each moment has its own mood. This is how it usually goes:

Bringing the baby in

The kvatter (the person who carries the baby in) enters the hall with the baby, and the guests stand and greet him with ברוך הבא (Baruch haba), the traditional greeting for the baby. This is a time for singing. In Yemenite families the baby is carried in to the piyut "Shamati Mipaatei Teiman". You can honor several kvatters, who pass the baby from hand to hand.

After the blessings and the naming

When the baby is already wrapped, the blessings are said over a cup of wine, and his name is heard for the first time. It is one of the most moving moments. Right after it come "Mazal tov", songs and piyutim in your family's custom.

The festive meal

At the meal (seudat mitzvah) you can play the playlist you chose. If the hall has a sound system or a DJ, just send them the Spotify link. I bring a speaker and a microphone for the piyutim and blessings to every brit, so you don't need to worry about it.

At a brit milah the mohel matters far beyond the circumcision itself. He can add a great deal to the atmosphere by bringing the guests in with joyful words and fitting songs. So when you choose a mohel, it is worth asking how a brit sounds with him: songs, piyutim, a few words of explanation for the guests.

Songs in the week before the brit

The singing starts before the day of the brit. In many communities, on the first Friday night after the birth, families hold a Shalom Zachar: after the Shabbat meal, guests come to the house for light refreshments, to sing and to give blessings. The Ashkenazi playlist includes Shalom Zachar songs.

On the evening before the brit, many families hold a Brit Yitzchak: family and friends gather for Torah study, reading of the Zohar, singing, piyutim and refreshments, and pray for the baby. Any of the playlists can fit, according to your family's custom.

And when the baby cries during the brit, it is said to be a time of favor, and the guests pray for what is in their hearts. That prayer is in the songbook, which you can print and hand out to the guests. The full order of the brit and the songbooks are on the page printable brit pages.

Frequently asked questions

What songs are sung at a brit milah?

It depends on your family's custom. At a Sephardic or Mizrahi brit people sing piyutim such as "Baruchim Atem Kehal Emunai" (ברוכים אתם קהל אמוני) and "Besiman Tov" (בסימן טוב); at an Ashkenazi brit, melodies such as "Hamalach Hagoel" (המלאך הגואל); and at a Yemenite brit, Yemenite piyutim. This page has five playlists, one for each custom.

When do people sing at a bris?

When the baby is carried into the hall, after the blessings and the naming, and at the meal. At the ceremony itself I sing with the guests in your custom, with a speaker and a microphone that I bring.

Can we give the playlist to the DJ or the hall?

Yes. Send them the link to the playlist you chose on Spotify, and they will play it as the guests arrive and during the meal.

Are the playlists free?

Yes. They are free to listen to, with no sign-up on our site: in the Spotify app, or right here with the "Play here on the page" button. The Yemenite playlist also has a YouTube version.

Where can we get the song lyrics so everyone sings along?

Download the printable songbook for free: one for Sephardic and Mizrahi families, one for Ashkenazi families. The texts are in Hebrew. It also includes the prayer for the moment the baby cries.

Do we need a sound system at the brit?

It helps a lot: everyone hears the blessings, the name and the piyutim. I bring a speaker and a microphone to every brit. For music at the meal you can also hire a DJ.

We don't know which style suits us. What should we do?

Message me on WhatsApp, tell me where your family comes from and what you like to sing, and we will choose together. What sets the mood is not the place but the people standing around the baby and what they sing.

What songs are sung at a Shalom Zachar or a Brit Yitzchak?

Songs and piyutim in your family's custom. The Ashkenazi Hasidic playlist has Shalom Zachar songs, and for the Brit Yitzchak evening any playlist close to your family will fit.
