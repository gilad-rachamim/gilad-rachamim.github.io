The question every parent asks

Bris Aftercare and Pain: What Helps Your Baby Calm Down

This is the question I hear most, and rightly so. I am Gilad Rachamim, a certified mohel, and this is how I explain it to parents: simply, without frightening, and without promising what cannot be promised.

עבריתEnglishРусский

What the baby feels

The baby feels discomfort and pressure, mainly during the minutes of the bris itself, and usually calms down quickly in Mom's or Dad's arms. What calms him most is what calms any baby: swaddling, being held close, a familiar voice and feeding.

That is why I make sure he has something to soothe him right at the end, and that his parents are already beside him with the swaddle and some quiet.

What really helps

  • A pacifier dipped in sugar water. Boil water, let it cool, add sugar and dip the pacifier in it. The pacifier is only for soothing, not to keep his mouth shut, and if the baby does not want it – do not force it.
  • Swaddling and holding. Wrapping him in a cloth diaper and a close hug calm him quickly, which is why after the bris he goes straight back into his parents' arms.
  • Feeding after the bris. Nursing or a bottle calms him and brings him back to his routine. Before the bris the instruction stays the same: feed him until about an hour before.
  • Numbing on the bandage after the bris. This is the method everyone accepts, and the one I recommend preparing in advance (see the shopping list, pharmacy section).
  • Novimol when the baby is unsettled. Give it according to the dose on the package; my timing instructions are on the aftercare page. When in doubt, ask me or your pediatrician.
"When Mom is calm, the baby is calm too"

Three ways to numb, briefly and fairly

There are three methods people talk about, and each has its own considerations. I am not ruling on halacha here, and every family talks with me about what suits them.

  • A numbing injection before the bris. The injection hurts in itself, it involves medical considerations, and some halachic authorities object to it, so it is not the accepted method.
  • A numbing cream before the bris. This involves both a halachic and a medical consideration, so we talk about it in a conversation before the bris, not on the day itself.
  • Numbing on the bandage after the bris. Accepted by everyone, and it calms the baby in the hours afterward.
One important thing to tell me in advance

If there is fava bean sensitivity in the family (G6PD deficiency), tell me before the bris. It affects the decision whether to use a numbing cream, so it is important that I know ahead of time.

How to prepare at home

  • Prepare sugar water and a clean pacifier in advance and put them in the diaper bag.
  • Buy the Novimol and the numbing for the bandage before the birth, so they are at home the moment you need them.
  • Fill in the details the mohel needs and send them to me, so we can talk about all of this calmly.
  • After the bris, log in the after-bris tracker (Hebrew) when he ate and when he passed urine. The tracker is free, with no sign-up, and saves only on your phone.

The first hours and days after the bris

Soothing does not end with the ceremony. Here is what, from my instructions, helps the baby feel better in the first days:

  • After every diaper change, drip natural oil onto the bandage – almond or paraffin oil. A soft, oiled bandage prevents rubbing that can hurt, and later comes off easily, without needless pain.
  • Gently wake a sleepy baby to feed, roughly every three or four hours. Feeding calms him and brings him back to his routine.
  • When you close the diaper, point the penis downward.
  • The baby may be restless in the first hours – swaddling, holding him close and nursing calm him. This is normal.
  • The next day the mohel comes to remove the bandage, and from there everything follows the day-by-day aftercare instructions.

Pain at the bris: common questions

Does a bris hurt the baby?

The baby feels discomfort during the minutes of the bris and usually calms down quickly in his parents' arms. Swaddling, holding, a pacifier in sugar water and feeding after the bris calm him.

Can a bris be done with anesthesia?

There are three methods people talk about: an injection before, a cream before, and numbing on the bandage after. Each has medical and halachic considerations, so we discuss it in a conversation before the bris.

What is given to the baby for pain after the bris?

Numbing on the bandage, and Novimol according to the dose on the package when the baby is unsettled. When in doubt, ask me or your pediatrician.

How do I make sugar water for the baby?

Boil water, let it cool, add sugar and dip a pacifier in it. The pacifier is only for soothing.

When should I feed the baby before the bris?

Until about an hour before the bris. In the last hour he is no longer fed, and until then let him eat as much as he wants. After the bris, feeding helps him calm down again.

Why tell the mohel about G6PD deficiency?

Fava bean sensitivity (G6PD) in the family affects the decision whether to use a numbing cream. So tell me about it before the bris.

The baby is restless in the first hours after the bris. Is that normal?

Yes. The baby may be restless in the first hours. Swaddling, holding him close and nursing calm him. If something worries you, take a photo and send it to me on WhatsApp.

What should I buy ahead to ease the baby's pain?

Esracaine 5% for numbing the baby's pain after the bris, Novimol, and natural oil (almond or paraffin) for the bandage. All of it is on the shopping list, and you buy it before the birth.
