The five chapters in short

1. Shopping before the birth

Toward the end of the pregnancy you never know exactly when the birth will come, and after it you are always busy, with a whole event to put together in eight days. So it is best to shop early: these things don't spoil. You don't have to buy everything, since some items will come as gifts. The key part is the pharmacy items for the days after the brit: buy them before the birth, so you don't have to run to a pharmacy later. The full list is in the chapter shopping before the birth.

2. The date and jaundice

The brit is held on the eighth day after birth. Born before sunset, the day of birth is day one; born after sunset, you count from the next day. The baby has a jaundice test, and you tell the mohel the result, plus the day and hour of the test. The brit is done only when the baby is ready for it. The brit milah date calculator gives you the exact date.

3. The ceremony

A bris ceremony is held in daylight, in the morning or at midday, with a safety margin before sunset. The place can be your home, a synagogue, a hall or a restaurant: what matters most is that the mother, after the birth, is comfortable. Check whether the place has a Chair of Elijah, or bring one from a synagogue. And ask in the family ahead of time who usually gets to be sandek, so no one close to you feels hurt.

4. The day of the brit

Bring a brit pillow, a kiddush cup and dry red wine, two tallitot (for the father and the sandek), besamim (in the Sephardic and Mizrahi custom, myrtle is best) and a siddur. Feed the baby until about an hour before the brit, and dress him in a two-piece outfit. And most important: come in a good mood, and leave the tiredness behind.

5. The mohel

Choose a mohel who studied in a structured program, is available to you on WhatsApp, brings a good atmosphere, and stays with you from your son's birth until he is fully healed. The easiest time to contact him is during the pregnancy, but a day or two after the birth is usually not too late.

Eight days and after: what happens when

  • During the pregnancy: shopping, choosing a mohel, and thinking about the place.
  • After the birth: write to the mohel, send the jaundice result, and fill in the details form.
  • The first Friday night: many communities hold a Shalom Zachar: after the Shabbat meal, guests come to the house to sing and give blessings.
  • Days 5–7: a last check before the brit: you send the mohel a photo, and he makes sure everything is ready.
  • The evening before: Brit Yitzchak, with Torah study, singing and prayers for the baby.
  • The eighth day: the brit milah, in daylight, before sunset.
  • The next day: the mohel comes to your home and carefully removes the bandage.
  • Day 31: pidyon haben, if this is the mother's firstborn and you are obligated.

Once the place, the time and the honors are settled, you can let everyone know: a designed bris invitation for WhatsApp, free. And you can make an honors card for each honored guest with the brit honors tool.

Frequently asked questions

What do we need to prepare for a bris ceremony?

A place (home, synagogue or hall), a time with a safety margin before sunset, a Chair of Elijah, the sharing of honors, a brit pillow, a kiddush cup and wine, two tallitot, besamim, and a two-piece outfit for the baby. And from the pharmacy: what you need for the days after the brit. It is all in the five chapters.

When should we start preparing?

Already during the pregnancy: the shopping, choosing a mohel and thinking about the place. After the birth you have eight days, and it is better if they are calm ones.

Is all of this in one document?

Yes. The guide for parents includes all the chapters, free, sent to your email. There is also a preparation checklist you tick off on your phone.

How much time do we have after the birth?

Eight days. If the shopping is done and the mohel is chosen, what is left is to settle the place and time, share out the honors and send the invitations.

What do we bring on the day of the bris?

A brit pillow, a kiddush cup and dry red wine, two tallitot, besamim, a siddur, and a diaper bag: cloth diapers, disposable diapers, wipes, a change of clothes and a pacifier.

When do we feed the baby before the brit?

Until about an hour before the brit. In the last hour you don't feed him, and until then let him eat as much as he wants.

When should we contact the mohel?

The easiest time is during the pregnancy or right after the birth. The first conversation is short and free: just write on WhatsApp.

What are Brit Yitzchak and Shalom Zachar?

Many communities hold a Shalom Zachar on the first Friday night after the birth, and a Brit Yitzchak on the evening before the brit, with Torah study, singing and prayers for the baby. All according to your family's custom.
