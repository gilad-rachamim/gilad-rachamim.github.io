Your son's first mitzvah

Brit Milah: What It Is and How It Works

The moment your son enters the covenant of our forefather Abraham. Here is how it looks, step by step, so you arrive calm and enjoy every moment.

עבריתEnglishРусский

What is a brit milah?

Brit milah (circumcision) is the commandment given to our forefather Abraham: "ובן שמנת ימים ימול לכם כל זכר לדרתיכם" (Genesis 17:12), the verse that says every boy is circumcised at eight days old. Ever since, for thousands of years, every son of the Jewish people has entered the covenant of Abraham at eight days old. It is the baby's first mitzvah, and one of the mitzvot the Jewish people kept in every generation, even in the hardest times.

A mohel performs the brit, in front of the family and the guests, with blessings, singing and great joy. It is an emotional moment, and for me also a moment of warmth and good feeling. What sets the mood is the people standing around the baby and what they sing.

"Brit" is Hebrew for "covenant" and "milah" means "circumcision". Many people also call it a "bris", the Ashkenazi pronunciation. It is the same ceremony.

When is the brit held?

On the eighth day after birth. If the baby was born before sunset, the day of birth counts as day one. If he was born after sunset, you start counting from the next day. The brit is done only when the baby is healthy and ready for it, so jaundice is checked beforehand. A brit on the eighth day takes place even on Shabbat and on Yom Tov (festivals). All the details, including C-sections and births close to sunset, are in the chapter the date of the brit, and you can work out the date itself with the brit milah date calculator.

Examples and special cases

For example, a baby born on a Monday before sunset has his brit the following Monday, if he is healthy. A baby born by C-section has his brit on the eighth day as usual, but not on Shabbat, not on Yom Tov and not on Yom Kippur. A postponed brit, one that is not on the eighth day, is also not held on Shabbat or a festival, and is set for a weekday.

The ceremony, step by step

  • 1. Bringing the baby in: the kvatter carries the baby into the hall, and the guests stand and greet him with ברוך הבא (Baruch haba), the traditional greeting for the baby. You can honor several kvatters, who pass the baby from hand to hand.
  • 2. The Chair of Elijah: by tradition, Elijah the prophet is present at every brit. The baby is placed for a moment on a chair dedicated to him.
  • 3. The sandek: the baby passes to the sandek, who sits wrapped in a tallit and holds him on his knees during the brit. This is the greatest honor at a brit.
  • 4. The blessings and the milah: the mohel says the blessing "al hamilah" (על המילה) and performs the circumcision. The father says the blessing "lehachniso bivrito shel Avraham avinu" (להכניסו בבריתו של אברהם אבינו), about bringing his son into the covenant of Abraham, and in many customs also "Shehecheyanu" (שהחיינו). The guests answer: "כשם שנכנס לברית, כן ייכנס לתורה ולחופה ולמעשים טובים", a wish for the child of Torah, marriage and good deeds. In the Mizrahi custom, between the milah and the periah, the guests say the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy (י"ג מידות של רחמים), at a time of favor.
  • 5. The blessings over the cup and the naming: after the brit, when the baby is already wrapped, the person honored holds him during the blessings. A blessing is said over a cup of wine, and in the prayer "Kayem et hayeled hazeh le'aviv ule'imo" (קיים את הילד הזה לאביו ולאמו) his name is heard for the first time. It is one of the most moving moments of the brit.
  • 6. Singing and the festive meal: "Mazal tov", songs and piyutim in your custom, and then the seudat mitzvah (festive meal). I bring a speaker and a microphone for the piyutim and the blessings.
When the baby cries

It is said that the time the baby cries at the brit is a time of favor, and the guests pray for what is in their hearts. You can print this prayer and hand it out to the guests: it is on the page printable brit pages, together with the order of the brit in the Sephardic, Mizrahi and Ashkenazi customs, and the songbook.

Who performs the brit?

A mohel. It is important to choose a certified mohel, who studied the laws and the medical side in a structured program, and who stays with you both before the brit and after it. I wrote about this in how to choose a mohel, with four questions to ask any mohel.

When to contact a mohel

The easiest time to contact a mohel is during the pregnancy: there is time for a calm first conversation and for the shopping. But reaching out a day or two after the birth is usually enough too: a good mohel is with you from the moment you write, including following the jaundice and the check before the brit.

How to prepare

A brit takes place in daylight, while the sun is still up, in the morning or at midday, with a safety margin before sunset. The place can be your home, a synagogue, a hall or a restaurant: what matters is that the mother, who has just given birth, is comfortable, and that there is a Chair of Elijah, or you bring one.

The baby and what to bring

Feed the baby until about an hour before the brit, and dress him in a two-piece outfit, so the pants can come off while the undershirt stays on. Bring a brit pillow, a kiddush cup and dry red wine, two tallitot (one for the father and one for the sandek), besamim (fragrant herbs) and a siddur. Everything, item by item, is in the chapters preparing the ceremony and the day of the brit, and you can tick things off in the preparation checklist on your phone, free and with no sign-up.

After the brit

After the blessings, the baby goes back to his mother's arms. On the day of the brit you drip oil on the bandage after every diaper change, and the next day the mohel removes the bandage. The full instructions, day by day, are in care after a brit milah, and what is worth buying before the birth is in the shopping list.

Frequently asked questions

What is a brit milah?

The commandment given to our forefather Abraham, in which every son enters the covenant of Abraham on the eighth day after birth. A mohel performs it, with blessings, the naming of the baby, singing and a festive meal.

What is the difference between a brit milah and a bris?

None. "Brit milah" is the Hebrew name, and "bris" is how many Ashkenazi Jews pronounce "brit". Both mean the covenant of circumcision on the eighth day.

On which day is a brit milah done?

On the eighth day after birth, if the baby is healthy. Born before sunset, the day of birth is day one; born after sunset, you count from the next day. The date calculator gives you the exact date.

Who says the blessings at a brit milah?

The mohel says "al hamilah", and the father says the blessing about bringing his son into the covenant of Abraham. The blessings over the cup and the naming are said by the person honored with them, and if you wish, the mohel reads them in the traditional melody.

When do we hear the baby's name?

In the blessings after the brit, in the prayer "Kayem et hayeled hazeh". Before that it is customary not to reveal the name, not even to the mohel.

How long does a brit milah take?

The ceremony itself is short. The milah takes only a few moments, followed by the blessings, the name and the singing. Most of the time you will spend at the meal, celebrating with the family.

What is the difference between a sandek and a kvatter?

The kvatter carries the baby into the hall. The sandek holds him on his knees during the brit itself, and that is the greatest honor. About all the honors: sandek, kvatter and the honors at a brit.

Is a brit milah done on Shabbat?

Yes, when Shabbat is the eighth day. A postponed brit, a brit of a baby born by C-section, and a brit of a baby born at bein hashmashot (twilight, between sunset and nightfall) are not held on Shabbat.
